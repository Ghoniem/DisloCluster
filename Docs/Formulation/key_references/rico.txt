Atomistically-informed cluster dynamics modeling of concurrent interstitial
and vacancy a-loop evolution in irradiated alpha-zirconium

Jose F. March-Rico * , Sophie Blondel , Brian D. Wirth

Department of Nuclear Engineering, University of Tennessee, Knoxville, TN, USA, 37996

ARTICLE INFO      ABSTRACT

Keywords:         A cluster dynamics framework is described that incorporates an extensive database of atomistic simulation re-
Zirconium         sults to minimize assumptions in microstructure evolution modeling in irradiated alpha-zirconium. This database
Cluster dynamics  informs mechanisms of: 1) defect production behavior in displacement cascades, 2) thermal dissociation rates for
Microstructure    all defect clusters, 3) point defect and defect cluster mobility (including anisotropy), 4) spontaneous and thermal
Dislocation loop  drift defect capture at dislocation loops, and 5) cascade overlap effects on defect generation rates. The proposed
Modeling          model mechanistically predicts the concurrent nucleation and growth of interstitial and vacancy a-loops; vacancy
                  loop nucleation is assisted by in-cascade vacancy clustering while vacancy loop growth is stabilized by the
                  incorporation of thermal drift capture radii. Cascade overlap effects on defect generation rates are found to be a
                  necessary component for model predictions of a-loop densities and sizes consistent with the experimental
                  literature. Key characteristics such as average loop diameters, number densities, and the relative ratio of loops
                  with interstitial and vacancy character are in good agreement with experimental observations for neutron-
                  irradiated pure, single-crystalline -zirconium. As such, the scale-bridging approach between atomistic simula-
                  tions and cluster dynamics modeling provides several improvements over the assumptions that are commonly
                  implemented in cluster dynamics models and set the stage for future experimental validation.

                                                                                       additional correlated recombination that occurs as the mobile species
                                                                                       diffuse through and ultimately away from the less mobile cascade debris
                                                                                       of a single displacement cascade.

                                                                                             The implementation of the cascade efficiency factor chosen in Eq.
                                                                                         (28) alters the defect generation rate as a function of dose. However,
                                                                                         overlap effects should realistically depend on the integrated defect
                                                                                         density or, alternatively, on the total defect volume to physically
                                                                                         represent the underlying mechanisms. For a given temperature and
                                                                                         irradiation condition, dose can act as a surrogate for these properties,
                                                                                         but the chosen efficiency curve in Fig. 15(b) may not be applicable for
                                                                                         other irradiation conditions as we expect this to depend on irradiation
                                                                                         temperature and dose rate. In order to inform a generalized efficiency
                                                                                         curve, the CD model at T = 573 K with the efficiency curve selected in
                                                                                         Fig. 15(b) was used to calculate the predicted Frenkel pair (FP) density
                                                                                         at a range of doses between 0.0001 and 10 dpa. The integrated FP
                                                                                         density at a given dose was calculated by summing up the total number
                                                                                         of interstitials in the system. The cascade efficiency originally plotted in
                                                                                         Fig. 15(b) was then plotted against the integrated FP density rather than
                                                                                         the corresponding dose in Fig. 19. This analysis clearly demonstrates
                                                                                         that there is a critical defect density (approximately 1024 FP/m3) at
                                                                                         which cascade overlap effects are expected to initiate. The cascade ef-
                                                                                         ficiency factor decreases sharply until it saturates at a defect density of
                                                                                         approximately 1025 FP/m3. This behavior can be reproduced with an
                                                                                         equation of an equivalent form to Eq. (28) using the constants C1 = 5.3 ×
                                                                                         10­25, C2 = 3.7 × 1024, and C3 = 0.0075 for FP densities in units of m-3.

                                                                                         greater transferability of the model to other irradiation conditions. It is
                                                                                         recommended that a similar efficiency factor be implemented in future
                                                                                         CD models for zirconium and other material systems.
