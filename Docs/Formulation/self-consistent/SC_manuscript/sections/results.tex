\section{Results: a 1\,\texorpdfstring{$\mu$}{u}m Hexagonal Single Crystal to 40\,dpa}
\label{sec:results}

Every result below comes from one calculation: the calibrated parameter set of
\cref{tab:fitted}, all nine families, three moments each, peripheral emission
active, the basal chain inactive, and the grain-boundary loop channel of
\cref{sec:gb-loops} active. Conditions are $T=573\,$K,
$G=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{dpa\,s^{-1}}$, zero applied stress, and sixteen dose
intervals from $10^{-4}$ to 40\,dpa. The upper limit is set by the measurements
rather than by the model: the \cloop{} data reach 35\,dpa, so a calculation
stopping at 10 would have to be extrapolated before it could be compared with
them. A companion section reports the same formulation on a 500\,nm crystal
(\texttt{sections/results\_500nm.tex}); the two differ in grain size and in
nothing else.

\subsection{Geometry, discretization and system size}
\label{sec:geometry}

The domain is a hexagonal prism of 1000\,nm across corners --- 866\,nm across
flats --- and 1600\,nm along $[0001]$, meshed with a refined boundary layer
150\,nm deep and five elements across (\cref{fig:mesh}). The refinement is not
cosmetic: \cref{sec:results-mobile} measures interstitial boundary layers of
$11$--$34\,$nm, so an unrefined mesh would resolve the steepest gradient in the
problem with a single element.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{figures/fe_mesh.png}
\caption{The finite-element mesh, cut in half so that the interior is visible:
129\,638 tetrahedra on 25\,907 mesh vertices, carrying 189\,533
cluster-dynamics nodes. The refinement toward the six prismatic faces and the
two basal faces is the boundary layer, 150\,nm deep and five elements across.
The prism is 1000\,nm across corners, 866\,nm across flats and 1600\,nm along
$[0001]$. Crystal axes as in \cref{fig:hcp-cell}: $[10\bar10]$,
$[\bar2\bar1\bar10]$ and $[0001]$.}
\label{fig:mesh}
\end{figure}

The cluster-dynamics trial functions live on second-order elements, so the field
nodes are more numerous than the mesh vertices: 189\,533 of them, filling a
crystal of $1.036\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{nm^{3}}$, or $4.454\times10^{10}$ atoms.
The resulting system sizes are those of \cref{tab:cost}: 758\,132 mobile degrees
of freedom solved as one coupled algebraic system 32 times, and 5\,117\,391
immobile ones marched as 159\,113 independent 36-dimensional stiff systems at
each of 160 substeps --- $9.16\times10^{8}$ scalar ordinary differential
equations over the march, in 4\,h\,16\,m on 24 cores. One fast solve costs
$3\,$m$\,12\,$s and one slow substep $53.4\,$s, so the two sides of the split are
within a factor of four of each other in total: $40\%$ of the wall clock is the
coupled algebraic problem and $56\%$ the decoupled stiff one. Per
point-integration the slow side costs $336\,\mathrm{\mu s}$, and identical
states are solved once, which removes $16\%$ of the work ($159\,113$ of
$189\,533$).

The whole surface is a boundary. No face is periodic, so every face carries
\cref{eq:bc}, and the maximum distance from any node to the nearest face is
432\,nm --- the inradius, set by the apothem rather than by the half-height, and
the cap $R_{\max}$ used in \cref{eq:trunc}. Of the field nodes, $19.8\%$ are
nearer a basal face than a prismatic one, which is the weighting behind the
directional measurements below.

\subsection{Mobile defect fields}
\label{sec:results-mobile}

\Cref{fig:mobile} shows the four mobile species at three doses: early in the
transient ($10^{-4}\,$dpa), at 1\,dpa, and at 40\,dpa, on two orthogonal
mid-cuts. Every panel has the same structure --- an interior plateau and a
depleted shell --- and the differences between them are in the width of that
shell and the level of the plateau.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/mobile_early_late.png}
\caption{Mobile species at $10^{-4}$, 1 and 40\,dpa, on one vertical and one
horizontal mid-cut. Rows, top to bottom: $C_v$, $C_i$, $C_{2i}$, $C_{3i}$; each
row has its own logarithmic color scale, so levels are comparable across dose
within a row and not between rows. The middle column is where the evolution is
visible: between $10^{-4}$ and 1\,dpa the interior plateau falls and the
depleted shell narrows, both because the sink density has risen, and between 1
and 40\,dpa almost nothing further happens.}
\label{fig:mobile}
\end{figure}

The boundary layer is a diffusion length and it orders the species. Measuring
the layer as the distance at which the binned median recovers $1-e^{-1}$ of the
interior plateau:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Dose (dpa) & $C_v$ & $C_i$ & $C_{2i}$ & $C_{3i}$ \\
\midrule
$10^{-4}$ & 23.2\,nm & 43.9\,nm & 100.6\,nm & 95.5\,nm \\
$10^{-2}$ & 23.2\,nm & 43.9\,nm &  69.7\,nm & 69.7\,nm \\
$1$       & 12.9\,nm & 33.5\,nm &  33.5\,nm & 33.5\,nm \\
$40$      & 12.9\,nm & 33.5\,nm &  33.5\,nm & 33.5\,nm \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two readings, and both are consequences of $L\sim\sqrt{D/k^{2}}$ rather than of
anything fitted. \emph{Across species at fixed dose}, the layer widens with
mobility: the vacancy, least mobile, is depleted over the shortest distance,
while the interstitial clusters --- which reach a sink from further away ---
carry the widest layer. \emph{Across dose at fixed species}, every layer
narrows, by a factor of $1.8$ for the vacancy and $3.0$ for the clusters between
$10^{-4}$ and 1\,dpa, because $k^{2}$ has grown with the loop population the
same calculation is building. The boundary layer is therefore not a fixed
geometric feature of the domain but a running measure of the sink density; and
it stops moving after 1\,dpa, at the same dose at which every other aggregate in
this section saturates.

Anisotropy acts on the shape of the layer, not only its width. Because the
domain has two basal faces and six prismatic ones, the layer can be measured
separately toward each set, using only the nodes for which that set is the
nearer boundary:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{$10^{-4}$\,dpa} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{40\,dpa} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-3}\cmidrule(lr){4-5}
Species & toward basal & toward prismatic & toward basal & toward prismatic \\
\midrule
$C_v$    & 39.0\,nm & 23.7\,nm  & 20.6\,nm & 13.2\,nm \\
$C_i$    & 11.5\,nm & 44.7\,nm  & 11.5\,nm & 34.2\,nm \\
$C_{2i}$ & 43.6\,nm & 102.6\,nm & 16.0\,nm & 34.2\,nm \\
$C_{3i}$ & 43.6\,nm & 97.4\,nm  & 16.0\,nm & 34.2\,nm \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This calculation was run at $p^{m}=(1.02,\,0.7,\,0.7,\,0.7)$, so the vacancy is
very nearly isotropic and the anisotropy is carried by the interstitials. The
two rows therefore separate the two effects cleanly, which a calculation with
both species anisotropic could not.

The vacancy row measures the \emph{geometry} alone. At $p^{v}=1.02$ transport is
isotropic to $6\%$, yet the basal layer is $1.65$ times the prismatic one at
$10^{-4}\,$dpa and $1.57$ at 40. That factor is the shape of the prism: a node
near a prismatic face has the neighbouring faces of the hexagon within reach and
is drained from several directions at once, while a node near a basal face has
its counterpart 1600\,nm away and is drained from one. A hexagonal grain
therefore imposes a directional signature on a perfectly isotropic species, and
any anisotropy inferred from a boundary-layer profile has to be corrected for it.

The interstitial row measures the anisotropy \emph{against} that background, and
it reverses the ordering. At $p^{i}=0.7$ the interstitial is
$(p^{i})^{6}=0.118$ as mobile along $[0001]$ as within the basal plane, so
$L\sim\sqrt D$ predicts a basal-to-prismatic ratio of $(p^{i})^{3}=0.343$ from
transport alone. Measured, the ratio is $0.256$ at $10^{-4}\,$dpa and $0.335$ at
40 --- the same order and on the correct side of unity, but not a clean reading
of the diffusivity ratio, because the geometric factor of $1.65$ acts in the
opposite direction and the sink field differs between the two directions.

Two consequences. First, an SRCD calculation on a shaped grain measures the
convolution of the diffusion tensor with the grain geometry, so extracting one
requires the other to be known --- an argument for calibrating $p^{m}$
atomistically rather than from a boundary-layer profile
(\cref{sec:conclusions}). Second, the narrowest layer in the problem is the
interstitial one toward a basal face, $11.5\,$nm and unchanging with dose,
against a boundary element of order $20\,$nm: the steepest gradient the
calculation contains is resolved by well under one element.

Interior median concentrations fall over the march --- $C_v$ from
$6.66\times10^{-6}$ to $3.30\times10^{-6}$, $C_i$ from $7.52\times10^{-10}$ to
$6.33\times10^{-10}$, and the clusters by a factor of four --- which is the
signature of a rising sink density at constant production. The vacancy falls
furthest, and the near-constancy of $C_i$ is the transport defect recorded above
showing itself in the field: with $D_i$ sixteen times larger than the growth law
assumes, the interstitial is drained to a level the loop population barely
perturbs.

\subsection{Immobile fields and their moments}
\label{sec:results-moments}

\Cref{fig:moments-cf,fig:moments-a1} show the three carried moments of the
faulted basal family and of one prismatic interstitial variant, at the same
three doses. The three rows are not three views of one thing; they answer
different questions, and their spatial structures differ.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/moments_cf.png}
\caption{The basal faulted family $c_f$ at $10^{-4}$, 1 and 40\,dpa: number
density $n$ (top), stored content $c$ (middle), second content $q$ (bottom).}
\label{fig:moments-cf}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/moments_a1.png}
\caption{The prismatic interstitial variant $a_1$ at the same three doses, same
three moments. The variants $a_2$ and $a_3$ are identical at zero applied stress
and are not shown.}
\label{fig:moments-a1}
\end{figure}

Number is boundary-peaked, content is interior-peaked, and that is the physics.
Cascade nucleation is spatially uniform, but the only channel that removes a
loop in the interior is coalescence, whose rate is driven by the absorbed mobile
flux --- and that flux vanishes where the Dirichlet condition pins the mobile
species. Near a boundary, loops are therefore nucleated and not removed, so $n$
rises steeply toward the surface. They are also not \emph{fed}, because the same
vanishing flux is what would grow them, so $c$ does not rise with $n$ and the
mean size $c/n$ collapses. Without the channel of \cref{sec:gb-loops} that shell
dominates every domain average, which is the artifact \cref{sec:results-volume}
quantifies.

The second moment does work, and it is realizable everywhere. The dispersion
$\Delta_k=q n/c^{2}$ of \cref{eq:lognormal} must be $\ge1$ for the three carried
numbers to be the moments of any distribution at all. Measured over the
interior:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{$c_f$} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$a_1$}
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{$a_1^{v}$} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-3}\cmidrule(lr){4-5}\cmidrule(lr){6-7}
Dose (dpa) & median & min & median & min & median & min \\
\midrule
$10^{-4}$ & 1.004 & 1.004 & 1.006 & 1.006 & 1.001 & 1.001 \\
$10^{-2}$ & 1.887 & 1.872 & 1.261 & 1.261 & 1.477 & 1.476 \\
$1$       & 1.450 & 1.404 & 1.247 & 1.246 & 2.362 & 2.240 \\
$40$      & 1.450 & 1.404 & 1.247 & 1.246 & 2.361 & 2.240 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three things follow. At $10^{-4}\,$dpa every family is monodisperse to three
decimal places, as it must be --- the loops present are all fresh nuclei
deposited at $m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$. By 1\,dpa the vacancy prismatic distribution
has broadened to $\Delta=2.36$, i.e. $s=\sqrt{\ln\Delta}=0.93$, a log-normal
nearly a decade wide at $\pm1\sigma$: a model carrying only $n$ and $c$ would
represent that population by a single size. And $\Delta\ge1$ holds at
\emph{every} interior node at every dose, minimum 1.001, which is the check that
the channels debiting $q$ are consistent with those debiting $n$ and $c$ --- a
check no tolerance can substitute for, since $\Delta<1$ is not an inaccuracy but
a statement that the state is not a moment triple.

The basal family passes through a maximum, $1.887$ at $10^{-2}\,$dpa falling to
$1.450$ by 1\,dpa, where the two prismatic families do not. The fall is the
boundary channel: \cref{eq:gbloop} removes the large end of the basal
distribution preferentially, and removing the large end \emph{narrows} what
remains. The prismatic interstitial family stays close to monodisperse at
$\Delta=1.25$ throughout, which under the transport defect above is what one
should expect --- a family that never grows never broadens.

\subsection{Global conservation}
\label{sec:results-conservation}

The six atom-balance accumulators integrate the production, recombination and
sink absorption of each character, and the two accumulators of
\cref{sec:gb-loops} integrate the loop content taken by the boundary. In a
spatially resolved calculation on a Dirichlet domain the balance cannot close on
those channels alone, and the residual is not solver error: it is the atoms that
left through the surface as \emph{mobile} species.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\caption{The atom balance over $0\to40\,$dpa, volume-averaged, per host atom.
Production and recombination are exactly equal between the characters, as
Frenkel-pair production and recombination must be. The two channels that differ
are where the physics is --- and on this march the network row differs by far
more than a bias would give, for the reason given below.}
\label{tab:conservation}
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Channel & interstitial & vacancy & \%\,prod ($i$) & \%\,prod ($v$) \\
\midrule
produced                  & 40.0000 & 40.0000 & 100.00 & 100.00 \\
recombined                &  2.0951 &  2.0951 &   5.24 &   5.24 \\
absorbed at the network   &  0.2938 & 10.2051 &   0.73 &  25.51 \\
\textbf{loops at the boundary} & \textbf{0.0050} & \textbf{3.5729}
                          & \textbf{0.01} & \textbf{8.93} \\
stored in the microstructure & 0.0004 & 0.0016 & 0.00 & 0.00 \\
\midrule
mobile flux to the boundary (by difference)
                          & 37.6057 & 24.1253 &  94.01 &  60.31 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Five readings, and the third is a caution rather than a result.

Both columns close on production, by construction of the last row, so once the
loop channel is carried explicitly what remains in the residual is the mobile
flux alone and the two do not contaminate each other. Before that channel was
booked separately, its $8.9\%$ of vacancy production was silently absorbed into
the residual, and because the residual is closure by difference, nothing looked
wrong.

The boundary is the dominant sink by a wide margin, taking $94\%$ and $60\%$ of
production as mobile species against $0.7\%$ and $26\%$ at the network. That is
a property of the geometry --- a $1\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ grain with every face
absorbing --- and it is precisely what a mean-field model represents by a single
$k^{2}_{gb}$ and cannot resolve.

The network row is contaminated by the transport defect and must not be read as
a bias. It absorbs $34.7$ times more vacancies than interstitials, where a
dislocation bias would give a modest excess the \emph{other} way. Network
absorption is $\sim Z\rho_N\bar D_m c_m$ for each species, so its ratio is
exactly the arrival ratio $x$ of \cref{eq:fluxscales}, and $x=14.2$ on this
march for the reason set out at the head of this section. The row is reported
because it is what the calculation did; it is not evidence about the bias.

The loop channel is strongly asymmetric, and that part is geometric rather than
transport-driven. It removes $3.573$ vacancies per atom against $0.005$
interstitials, a factor of $709$. The asymmetry follows from size: basal loops
reach a mean diameter of $195\,$nm where prismatic interstitial loops stay at
$1.9\,$nm, so a basal loop intersects a face from far deeper inside the crystal.
Note also that the boundary removes \emph{2270 times more} vacancy loop content
than the microstructure retains ($3.573$ against $0.0016$), which is the
quantitative form of the statement that on this grain size the loop population
is boundary-controlled.

The balance is confirmed independently. Evaluating the volume integral of the
mobile reaction rate on the converged steady field --- which equals the outward
surface flux by the divergence theorem, and touches none of the accumulators ---
reproduces the closure column to between $1.003$ and $1.039$ at every dose from
$10^{-4}$ to 40\,dpa. On the 500\,nm crystal the same diagnostic disagreed by a
factor of $3.6$, so on this geometry the boundary channel is measured rather
than merely inferred.

decomposition. The fractions sum to unity once the boundary channels are
included; without them the sum reached only $\sim0.3$.

A bookkeeping caveat for anyone reading the raw output. The six atom-balance
accumulators are reset at every dose interval, so each snapshot holds that
interval's contribution and not the running total; the cumulative balance is
their partial sum, which recovers a production of exactly $40.0000$ per atom at
40\,dpa. The two grain-boundary accumulators are \emph{not} reset and are
already cumulative. \Cref{tab:conservation} is assembled accordingly.

The zone is real and sharp. The face layer is swept to $2.5\times10^{-7}$ of the
companion calculation --- the concentration floor, so as complete a removal as
this model can express. Beyond a few nanometres the \aloop{} density has already
recovered, while the \cloop{} density is still at $0.12$ of the twin at
$7$--$9\,$nm and $0.23$ at $24$--$32\,$nm: the basal zone is an order of
magnitude wider than the prismatic one, which is the size effect again, since
\cref{eq:mgb} removes a loop when its own radius reaches the face.

The mean diameter recovers monotonically from $6.8\,$nm at the boundary to
$45\,$nm in the interior, which is the size gradient a denuded zone is
identified by in a micrograph: not merely fewer loops near the boundary, but
\emph{smaller} ones, because the large end of the distribution is what
\cref{eq:gbloop} removes first.

A local channel with non-local consequences. Beyond $80\,$nm the ratio to the
twin exceeds unity and keeps rising, reaching $2.4$ and $2.1$ at $200\,$nm from
any face, where \cref{eq:mgb} removes nothing. The cause is the operator split
working as intended: removing the shell's loops removes their sink strength from
\cref{eq:KL}, the fast solve returns a different mobile field everywhere, and
the interior responds to it. A model in which the boundary were only a
mean-field sink strength could not produce this, because the boundary's effect
on the interior would be a single number rather than a changed field.

How this compares with experiment needs stating carefully. Loop-denuded and
void-denuded zones at grain boundaries are a standard observation in irradiated
metals~\citep{singh1973influence,bai2010efficient,sun2013situ,cheng2016grain},
and the mechanism assumed here --- the boundary as a strong sink whose influence
extends a diffusion length into the grain --- is the standard interpretation. We
do not compare quantitatively against a measured zone width in zirconium: no
such measurement is in the data set of \cref{tab:targets}, and the width
predicted here is offered as a prediction rather than as a validated result. It
is also, at a few nanometres for the prismatic family, comparable with the
$20\,$nm element size of the boundary layer, so the \aloop{} zone in particular
is under-resolved and its width should be treated as an upper bound set by the
mesh.

Every entry is volume-weighted on the crystal itself rather than on its bounding
box, and $d$ is the number-weighted mean diameter of \cref{fig:volavg}, so the
table is that figure read at one dose. With the channel the domain and interior
means differ by only $1.35$ in $N_a$ and $2.26$ in $N_c$, and the ordering is
the physical one: the interior carries \emph{more} loops than the domain mean,
which is what a sink at the surface should produce. A domain average is
therefore a statement about the material. That is the strongest single argument
for carrying the channel: it removes an artifact that otherwise makes the most
natural summary statistic meaningless.

Against experiment, the basal and prismatic families must be judged separately,
because only one of them is a test of the model.

The basal comparison is meaningful. At $573\,$K and 24.5\,dpa the measurements
give $N_c=8.0\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and $d_c=95\,$nm. That dose is
inside the calculation rather than beyond it, so no extrapolation is needed: the
interior gives $N_c/N^{\exp}=0.73$ and $d_c/d^{\exp}=2.23$, and between 1 and
40\,dpa neither quantity moves by more than a percent, so which snapshot is read
makes no difference to either ratio. The density is now essentially right ---
within $27\%$ --- and the size is a factor of two too large. The direction is
the informative part: the model retains too much vacancy content per basal loop,
and since the stored basal content scales as $N_c d_c^{3}$ it is $8\times$ what
is observed. It is not a density problem that a nucleation term could absorb;
the channels that could remove the content --- coalescence, peripheral emission,
the small-end leak --- are the ones the calibration has already tuned.

The prismatic comparison is not meaningful on this march, and it is worth saying
so rather than reporting the ratio. The measured \aloop{} diameter at these
conditions is $6.6$--$18.6\,$nm; this calculation gives $2.5$--$2.8\,$nm. The
reason is not a parameter. The interior mean defect content of the $a_1$ family
is $\bar m=49.1$ at \emph{every} one of the sixteen doses, against its own
nucleation size $n^{\mathrm{nuc}}_{iL}=53.79$: the family sits below the size at
which it is born, from $10^{-4}$ to 40\,dpa, so no loop in it ever absorbs a net
interstitial and the population is replaced by fresh cascade nuclei as fast as
it shrinks. That is exactly what \cref{eq:growconds} requires at $x=14.2$
against a ceiling of $x_a=0.951$, and it is the transport defect of the opening
box rather than a statement about the formulation. Reconciling the two
interstitial mobilities places $x$ inside the window and the family grows.

describing it is a statement about an ensemble and not about this crystal. At

because it raised the basal radius by $\sqrt2$ and a larger loop is harder to
fit. The ledger of \cref{eq:ledger} nonetheless closes on stored defects to
machine precision and on loop number to the integer draw at every one of the
four doses.

\subsection{Evolution of the loop size distributions}
\label{sec:results-spectrum}

The three moments carried per family close on a log-normal
(\cref{eq:lognormal}), so the model holds a genuine size distribution at every
node and not merely a mean. Two distributions are in play and they are not the
same. At one node the closure gives a log-normal in the defect count $m$, with
$\bar m=c/n$ and dispersion $\Delta=qn/c^{2}=e^{s^{2}}$. Over the crystal the
population is the \emph{volume-weighted mixture} of those per-node log-normals,
and a mixture of log-normals is not log-normal: it is broader than any of its
components and may be multimodal where the field varies strongly. The mixture is
the observable one --- it is what a micrograph of a whole specimen samples ---
and it is what \cref{fig:spectrum-dose} plots, as $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}d$
against a linear diameter axis, which is the form a measured size distribution
takes.

Working in diameter is exact rather than numerical: if $m$ is log-normal with
parameters $(\mu,s)$ then $d=2\lambda_k\sqrt m$ is log-normal with
$(\ln 2\lambda_k+\mu/2,\ s/2)$, so each node contributes a closed-form curve and
the mixture is accumulated directly on a logarithmic diameter grid. Every
distribution below is taken from the final state \emph{after} grain-boundary
absorption, so the removals of \cref{eq:gbloop} are already inside the moments
being inverted.

The distributions are born monodisperse and broaden. At $10^{-4}\,$dpa every

and the distribution is a spike a few percent wide at the nucleation size. This
is not an artifact of the closure but its correct statement of the physics ---
at that dose every loop present is a fresh cascade nucleus deposited at
$m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$, and there has been no time for anything to broaden it.

By $10^{-2}\,$dpa the basal spike has become a distribution, and by 1\,dpa the
shape has stopped changing: the 1\,dpa and 40\,dpa rows are identical to three
decimal places in every family and every region. The population saturates in
shape as well as in the aggregates of \cref{sec:results-volume}, which is the

nucleation size at all.

The boundary shell is exactly monodisperse, $\Delta=1.000$ for every family at
every dose, and it stays so. That is a physical result and not a numerical
floor. Both terms that broaden a family in \cref{eq:q-FP} are proportional to
the absorbed mobile flux --- the drift through $\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k$ and the
spreading through $\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k$ --- and that flux vanishes where the
Dirichlet condition pins the mobile species. In the shell, loops are nucleated
at one size and are neither fed nor starved, so nothing acts on the width. They
are also small, $21\,$nm for the basal family against $171\,$nm in the interior,
because they never grow.

The interior is broad, and the vacancy prismatic family is the broadest at
$\Delta=1.63$, a log-normal with $s=0.70$. The whole-crystal spectrum is
therefore a mixture of a narrow shell peak near $21\,$nm and a broad interior
distribution centred near $171\,$nm for the basal family, and \emph{that} is the
origin of the multimodality in \cref{fig:spectrum-dose}, not any structure in a
local distribution. The distinction matters for what happens next: a locally
broad population coarsens, because its large loops grow at the expense of its
small ones; a spatially sorted one does not, because its two peaks are not in
contact.

The growth conditions predict which families move, and on this march they

\label{sec:annealing-outlook}%
The natural next experiment on this model is a post-irradiation anneal: switch
off the source, hold the temperature, and follow the distributions. It is worth
setting out what the formulation predicts, because the model has no fitted
annealing lifetime --- the two lifetimes of the mean-field treatment were
deleted and replaced by peripheral emission against each family's own
size-dependent binding energy (\cref{app:emission}) --- so an anneal is a genuine
prediction rather than a fit.

The flux ratio diverges, and the family ordering inverts. With the source off
the interstitial supply collapses first, since interstitials are orders of
magnitude more mobile and drain to the sinks within $\tau_M\sim(k^{2}D)^{-1}$.
So $x=\Phi_V/\Phi_I\to\infty$, which by \cref{eq:growconds} places \emph{every}
vacancy family on the growing side and every interstitial family on the
shrinking one. The prismatic interstitial loops --- the dominant population at
the end of irradiation in a correctly transported calculation --- should
therefore dissolve, while the prismatic vacancy family should grow. An anneal is
the one experiment that reverses the sign of the interstitial--vacancy
competition without changing a parameter.

The small end evaporates and the mean rises. Once production stops the driver is
thermal emission, and the binding energy of \cref{eq:Eb} falls with loop size,
so small loops emit fastest. The distribution should lose its small end while
its large end persists --- a rise in $\bar d$ accompanied by a fall in $N$, which
is Ostwald ripening and is what annealing experiments on irradiated Zr report.
In the moment language, $\dot n<0$ and $\dot c<0$ with $|\dot n|/n>|\dot c|/c$,
and $\Delta$ falls: \emph{annealing narrows the distribution}, where irradiation
broadened it.
