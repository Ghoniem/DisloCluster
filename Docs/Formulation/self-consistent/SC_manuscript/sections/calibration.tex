\subsection{The objective, and the sense in which it is a posterior mode}
\label{sec:objective}

Let $\bm\theta$ be the calibrated parameters of \cref{tab:controls} and
$\mathbf y_{\exp}$ the measurements of \cref{tab:targets}. The estimator used
here minimizes
\begin{equation}
  J(\bm\theta)
  = \sum_{f\in\{A,C\}} w_f
    \left[
      \frac{1}{|\mathcal N_f|}\!\!\sum_{r\in\mathcal N_f}\!\!
        \left(\log_{10}\frac{N_r(\bm\theta)}{N_r^{\exp}}\right)^{2}
      +\frac{1}{|\mathcal D_f|}\!\!\sum_{r\in\mathcal D_f}\!\!
        \left(\log_{10}\frac{d_r(\bm\theta)}{d_r^{\exp}}\right)^{2}
    \right]
    \;+\;J_{\mathrm{os}}(\bm\theta),
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with $J_{\mathrm{os}}$ a penalty on non-monotonic dose response. This is
\emph{not} an arbitrary least-squares choice; it is the maximum \emph{a
posteriori} estimator under a stated model of the data. Taking the measurement
errors to be log-normal --- which is the right family for a quantity spanning
four decades and reported as an order of magnitude plus a factor --- with
$\log_{10}N_r^{\exp}\sim\mathcal N(\log_{10}N_r(\bm\theta),\sigma_f^{2})$, and
taking $p(\bm\theta)$ uniform on the bounded box of physically admissible
values, Bayes' rule gives
\begin{equation}
  -\log p(\bm\theta\mid\mathbf y_{\exp})
  = \mathrm{const} + \sum_{f,r}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}_{f}}
      \left(\log_{10}\frac{y_r(\bm\theta)}{y_r^{\exp}}\right)^{2}
    \;-\;\log p(\bm\theta),
  \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
so that minimizing \cref{eq:objective} over the box is exactly maximizing
\cref{eq:posterior}, with $w_f\propto\sigma_f^{-2}$ and $-\log p(\bm\theta)$
flat inside the box and infinite outside. The reported parameter set is
therefore a MAP point, and the weights $w_f$ are an explicit statement about the
relative credibility of the two data sets rather than a tuning knob.

The logarithmic form matters and was not the original choice. An
absolute-ratio residual $(d-d^{\exp})/d^{\exp}$ is bounded below by $-1$ and
unbounded above, so it penalizes a model that is a factor of two \emph{high}
four times as heavily as one that is a factor of two \emph{low}. Against a
diameter the model systematically underpredicts, that asymmetry rewards
shrinking. \Cref{eq:objective} is symmetric under inversion of the ratio, which
is the property a ratio of two positive quantities should have.

What has \emph{not} been done. No posterior has been sampled. The
full Bayesian pipeline this framing points at --- a space-filling design
screened by variance-based global sensitivity analysis, a Gaussian-process
emulator trained on it to stand in for the solver, and Markov-chain sampling of
\cref{eq:posterior} on the emulator --- is tractable here, because one
evaluation of \cref{eq:objective} costs 0.2--0.5\,s over the thirteen
temperatures and 78 points, but it has not been run. Everything reported below
is a point estimate, and no credible interval accompanies it.

\subsection{The search}
\label{sec:search}

The parameters were fitted in stages, each stage a bounded Nelder--Mead search
over a subset, with the full set polished jointly at the end:
\cloop{} nucleation and the small-size floor; the bias; \cloop{} removal;
the \aloop{} side; then the joint polish. Staging is a practical response to a
strongly anisotropic objective and not a claim about identifiability. A
screening pass over the 26 candidate parameters found nine of them inert in this
formulation --- a change over their whole admissible range moved $J$ by less
than the convergence tolerance --- and those were held fixed.

\subsection{Four parameters rest on their bounds}
\label{sec:bias-caveat}

A lever on its bound is not a fitted value. Of the calibrated set, the
four bias parameters and the three small-size-floor parameters sit exactly on
the edge of the admissible box, which means the data wanted to push them further
and the box stopped them. The number to check is what the fitted anisotropy
implies for the diffusion tensor. From \cref{eq:pm},
\begin{equation}
  \frac{D^{v}_{\parallel}}{D^{v}_{\mathrm{basal}}}=\bigl(p^{v}\bigr)^{6}=16.8,
  \qquad
  \frac{D^{i}_{\parallel}}{D^{i}_{\mathrm{basal}}}=\bigl(p^{i}\bigr)^{6}=1.00 .
  \label{eq:Dratio}
\end{equation}
\emph{That is a strong claim about vacancy transport in $\alpha$-Zr, and it is
not one the data in \cref{tab:targets} can support.} It is what the loop
densities and diameters prefer, at a ceiling of $p^{v}=1.6$ chosen so the
implied ratio remains within an order of magnitude of what is reported
atomistically; released, the search continues in the same direction. The
resulting efficiencies are
\begin{equation*}
  Z_{\mathrm{basal}}(v)=2.080,\quad Z_{\mathrm{basal}}(i)=1.300,\quad
  Z_{\mathrm{prism}}(i)=1.300,\quad Z_{\mathrm{prism}}(v)=1.294,
\end{equation*}
which satisfy the co-growth criterion of \cref{sec:coexistence}
($p^{i}=1.00<1.60=p^{v}$) but do so at an anisotropy no independent measurement
has been produced for.

Two readings follow, and they should be kept apart. As a \emph{prediction}, the
calibrated model reproduces four decades of \aloop{} data and is used as such in
\cref{sec:results}. As an \emph{inference about transport}, \cref{eq:Dratio} is
the strongest signal in this work that a channel is missing rather than
mis-parameterized: a fit that must go to the edge of physical plausibility in one
parameter to reproduce a diameter is telling us something about the model, and
the diagnosis is taken up in \cref{sec:conclusions}. Independent constraints on
$p^{v}$ from atomistics --- which \cref{eq:Emsplit} would accept directly, since
$p^{m}$ is the only anisotropy the model has --- would settle it, and would turn
the four $^{\dagger}$ entries of \cref{tab:fitted} from constraints into fixed
inputs.

Two further caveats on the transfer of this set to
\cref{sec:results}. First, the fit is a $0$-D fit: it is the march's slow step
term for term --- one templated function, the same binary, the same switches,
differing only in whether the mobile species are frozen --- so the parameters
transfer exactly. What does not transfer is everything the $0$-D cannot see: in
the $0$-D the vacancy pool is free and re-equilibrates, where the march pins it
to the fast solve, and the two need not move a given quantity in the same
direction. Second, the fit carries no grain-boundary loop channel,
because the $0$-D has no boundary; the calculation of \cref{sec:results} does.
The parameters and that calculation are therefore not strictly a matched pair,
and \cref{sec:results-volume} reports what the mismatch costs.
