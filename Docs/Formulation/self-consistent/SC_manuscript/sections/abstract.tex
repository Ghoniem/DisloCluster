\begin{abstract}
We present a self-consistent, spatially resolved cluster-dynamics model for the
irradiated microstructure of $\alpha$-zirconium, with the construction that
converts its continuum fields into discrete dislocations and the two-time-scale
method that integrates them. The model is self-consistent in two senses. First,
the bias factors of \emph{all} variants follow from anisotropic diffusion
theory: every capture efficiency in a loop growth law, and every sink strength
those loops impose on the mobile species, comes from the \emph{same} diffusion
tensors that transport them, through one anisotropy parameter per species
rather than from independently fitted bias factors. Second, the transfer
between the continuum fields and the discrete population preserves what defines
that population --- stored defects exactly, loop number to the integer draw, the
dispersion of the size distribution to the truncation of the sampling --- so one
calculation may run as continuum, discrete or hybrid.
The immobile population is resolved into nine crystallographic families ---
three prismatic $\langle a\rangle$ variants of both characters and a three-state
basal vacancy chain --- each carrying the first three moments of its size
distribution, closed by a log-normal ansatz. The mobile species obey a steady
reaction--diffusion problem with Dirichlet conditions at grain boundaries, the
immobile families pointwise ordinary differential equations; freezing the mobile
field removes the spatial coupling by construction, so that $2.1\times10^{8}$
stiff equations become $73\,518$ independent $36$-dimensional systems and the
cost of one factorization falls by $10^{10}$. Grain boundaries are sinks for
both populations by different mechanisms: a Dirichlet condition for the mobile
species, and for the loops a size-gated removal of the fraction large enough to
reach the boundary. Applied to a \SI{500}{\nano\meter} hexagonal single crystal
at \SI{573}{\kelvin} to \SI{40}{\dpaunit}, it resolves mobile boundary
layers of $8$--$46$\,\si{\nano\meter} ordered by species mobility and narrowing
twofold as the sink density grows; closes both atom balances on production, the
boundary absorbing $96\%$ of interstitial production as mobile species and
$27\%$ of vacancy production as loops; and predicts a loop-denuded zone
$\sim5$\,\si{\nano\meter} wide across which the mean loop diameter recovers from
$6.8$ to $144$\,\si{\nano\meter}. The parameters are calibrated against 78
transmission-electron-microscope measurements as a maximum \emph{a posteriori}
estimate under stated priors. Because every bias follows from the diffusion
tensors, the anisotropy cannot absorb a shortfall by refitting a bias; it is
fixed from transport, not from loop counts, with $p^{v}=1.02$ and $p^{i}=0.70$
as inputs, implying an interstitial migration anisotropy of
\SI{0.106}{\electronvolt} at \SI{573}{\kelvin}, open to atomistic test. On that
fixed anisotropy the calibration reproduces the basal loop diameter without
adjusting it --- \SI{100.7}{\nano\meter} against \SI{95}{\nano\meter} measured
--- with the prismatic density to $0.88\times$ and its diameter to
$1.21\times$. What it does not reach is the basal density, which remains
$2.1\times$ the measurement, and a prismatic temperature trend a factor of six
low near \SI{600}{\kelvin}; with the anisotropy fixed, the shortfall rests on
the basal capture prefactor, hence in absorption rather than transport.
\end{abstract}
