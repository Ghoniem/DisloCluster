\section{Continuum-to-Discrete Self-Consistency}\label{sec:conversion}

\subsection{Where the mean field expires, and how the model knows}
\label{sec:coarsening}

Every cluster-dynamics formulation is a mean-field theory of a population of
discrete objects. Its kernels are derived on the assumption that absorbers are
placed independently and at random, so that the probability of overlap between a
growing loop and its neighbors is a function of the volume fraction alone. That
assumption is a \emph{dilute-limit} statement, and it is standard in the
rate-theory literature that it degrades as the swept fraction becomes
appreciable~\citep{BrailsfordBullough1972,Mansur1978,GolubovSingh2001}.
Three distinct mechanisms take it apart, and they take it apart at different
doses:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Spatial correlation from coalescence.} Once loops have merged,
      their positions are no longer independent draws --- a merged loop occupies
      the union of two former positions --- so the Avrami estimate of the
      overlap probability stops estimating anything.
\item \textbf{Local elastic interaction.} The capture efficiencies of
      \cref{eq:Zsk} are derived for an isolated absorber in a uniform far field.
      Two loops within a few radii of each other share a stress field, and the
      drift term that biases capture is not the sum of the two isolated
      ones~\citep{marchrico2023,marchrico2025}.
\item \textbf{Gradients.} A sink strength presumes the diffusion field has
      relaxed to a common far-field value between absorbers. In a boundary layer
      it has not, which is the failure \cref{sec:intro-meanfield} describes and
      which spatial resolution already repairs for the mobile species --- but
      not for the loops, whose own kernels are still mean-field in the
      \emph{local} neighborhood.
\end{enumerate}

The model detects the first of these itself, and that is the useful property.
The Avrami overlap fraction $\varphi_{LL}$ of \cref{eq:avrami} is the model's own
estimate of the probability that a loop has met another; when it approaches
unity the estimate is of order its own maximum and carries no information. The
\emph{coarsening dose}
\begin{equation}
  d_{\mathrm{coarsen}}
  = \min\bigl\{\gamma:\ \varphi_{LL}(\gamma)\ge\varphi^{\star}
    \ \text{on a fraction}\ \ge f^{\star}\ \text{of interior nodes}\bigr\}
  \label{eq:dcoarsen}
\end{equation}
is therefore not an external criterion imposed on the model but a reading of one
of its own state variables, and it costs nothing to evaluate. Which family
reaches it first is a property of the formulation and not of the material: in
otherwise identical calculations differing only in whether the capture
efficiencies are generated from the diffusion tensors or taken as fitted
constants, the family that coarsens first \emph{swaps} between the basal and the
prismatic populations.

\subsection{Why a discrete representation is worth having}
\label{sec:why-discrete}

Beyond repairing the dilute limit, the discrete representation is the interface
to micromechanics. Dislocation dynamics computes the stress a population exerts
and the resistance it offers to a gliding line; a channeling model needs to know
which obstacles a channel has swept and which remain; a fracture model needs the
local obstacle spacing. None of these takes a density as input. They take
positions, radii, Burgers vectors and habit planes.

The engineering objective is therefore stronger than a one-way export. It is
that the continuum and the discrete representations be \emph{equivalent} to
within stated tolerances, so that a calculation may be run in either --- or in a
hybrid, continuum where the population is dilute and discrete where it is not
--- with the choice made on grounds of dose and computational cost rather than
of physics. That is what makes the conversion a problem with conservation laws
attached rather than a rescaling.

\subsection{The conversion and what it must preserve}
\label{sec:transfer}

The requirements cannot all be met exactly, and their ranking is a modeling
decision:

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Stored defects, exactly.} $\sum_\ell m_\ell$ over the discrete
      loops of a family must equal the continuum content of the region they
      replace. A transfer that loses defects appears downstream as a spurious
      sink.
\item \textbf{Loop number, to the integer draw.} A continuum density gives a
      real expected count and a discrete population an integer one, so the best
      available is $|N_{\mathrm{draw}}-\Lambda|\le\tfrac12$.
\item \textbf{The dispersion $\Delta_k$, to the truncation of the size
      sampling.} This requirement did not exist before \cref{sec:moments}: a
      monodisperse family has nothing to reproduce.
\item \textbf{Sink strength, reported but not asserted on.} The line length a
      discrete population presents differs from $\rho^k_{sL}$ for reasons that
      are physical rather than numerical --- ellipticity among them --- and the
      difference is quantified rather than forced to zero.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Only the eight loop families convert.} The stacking-fault pyramid has no
Burgers vector and no line, so it has no discrete representation in a
dislocation code at all; it stays in the continuum across every transfer, and a
conversion that folded its content into $c_f$ would report basal vacancies as
dislocation content the solver would then be free to move.

\paragraph{Nodal volumes.} The fields are known at nodes that are not uniformly
spaced, so each node must be given the volume of material it stands for. A
restricted Voronoi tessellation supplies it, restricted in the sense that sample
points falling outside the crystal are rejected rather than charged to the
nearest boundary node. On a hexagonal prism the bounding box is $4/3$ of the
crystal, so using the box would over-count the population by a third and scatter
loops through six empty wedges outside the outline.

\paragraph{Counts.} The expected number of loops of family $(s,k)$ at node $j$ is
\begin{equation}
  \lambda^k_{sj}=\frac{n^k_{sL}(\xv_j)}{\Omega}\,V_j,
  \qquad
  \Lambda^k_s=\sum_j\lambda^k_{sj},
  \qquad
  N_{\mathrm{draw}}=\operatorname{round}\bigl(\Lambda^k_s\bigr).
  \label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
Both factors are needed and for different reasons: $n^k_{sL}$ says how many loops
belong in unit material, $V_j$ how much material the node stands for. Sampling on
$n^k_{sL}$ alone would give every node equal geometric weight and over-populate a
refined region in exact proportion to how well it was resolved. Rounding rather
than drawing a Poisson total is deliberate: a Poisson total has
$\operatorname{Var}=\Lambda$, and a dose sweep whose loop count fluctuates by
$\sqrt\Lambda$ between snapshots is dominated by counting noise.

\paragraph{Sizes.} Each loop's size is drawn from its home node's own log-normal
\cref{eq:lognormal}, not set to the node mean. With two moments there was no
distribution to draw from and every loop at a node was identical, so the exported
population was monodisperse by construction --- visible immediately in any size
histogram. The sample is then rescaled so that $\sum_\ell m_\ell$ matches the
continuum content exactly, which is requirement~1 above; the rescaling removes
the rounding, and the residual is at the level of machine precision.

\paragraph{Geometry.} A loop of family $k$ becomes a polygon on its habit plane,
of an area matching $\pi R^{2}$ rather than of that circumradius --- a regular
$n$-gon inscribed in a circle of radius $R$ has area
$(n/2)R^{2}\sin(2\pi/n)$, which for a hexagon is only $82.7\%$ of the disc, and
it is the stored content that must match. Its outline is elliptical with axis
ratio set by the anisotropy, and its center is placed by rejection sampling
inside the crystal.

\paragraph{What the domain refuses.} A discrete-dislocation solver silently
declines a loop whose nodes leave the grain, so the conversion must predict that
decision and keep the refused share in the continuum. \textbf{The share must be
counted in defects, not in loops}: on one measured transfer, one refusal in six
was $83\%$ by count and $98\%$ by stored defects. On the geometry of
\cref{sec:results} the basal population becomes effectively infeasible to
transfer above $\sim0.1\,$dpa, because a \cloop{} loop of mean radius $25\,$nm
centered anywhere within $25\,$nm of a face overhangs it; \cref{sec:results-discrete}
reports the measured fraction.

\subsection{Grain boundaries as sinks: two models, not one}
\label{sec:gb-loops}

A grain boundary absorbs mobile point defects and it absorbs dislocation loops,
and the two require different treatments because the objects differ in kind.

\paragraph{Mobile species: a Dirichlet condition.} \Cref{eq:bc} pins the mobile
concentrations at thermal equilibrium on the boundary. Absorption is then a flux,
$-\bm D^{x}\nabla c^{x}\!\cdot\!\bm n$, and the depleted layer in front of the
boundary is solved for. Nothing about this is parameterized: the layer width
follows from the local diffusivity and the local sink density, differs between
species, and evolves through the transient.

\paragraph{Immobile loops: a size-gated removal.} A loop cannot be given a
boundary condition, because it is not transported --- it has no flux to set. What
it has is a \emph{size}, and a loop of radius $R^k=\lambda_k\sqrt m$ centered a
distance $x$ from the nearest face intersects that face when
\begin{equation}
  m\;\ge\;m_{gb}(x)=\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_k}\right)^{2}.
  \label{eq:mgb}
\end{equation}
The absorbed fraction of each moment is therefore the mass of the family's own
size distribution above that threshold. Writing
$\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_\theta)$ for the fraction of the $j$-th moment of
\cref{eq:lognormal} lying above $m_\theta$,
\begin{equation}
  \Phi^{(j)}_k(m_\theta)
  =\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(
     \frac{\ln m_\theta-\mu_k-j\,s_k^{2}}{s_k\sqrt2}\right),
  \label{eq:Phij}
\end{equation}
the three removals of \cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-c,eq:block-q} are
\begin{equation}
  \Theta^k_0=\Phi^{(0)}_k\bigl(m_{gb}\bigr)\,n^k_{sL},
  \qquad
  \Theta^k_1=\Phi^{(1)}_k\bigl(m_{gb}\bigr)\,c^k_{sL},
  \qquad
  \Theta^k_2=\Phi^{(2)}_k\bigl(m_{gb}\bigr)\,q^k_{sL},
  \label{eq:gbloop}
\end{equation}
at a rate $\nu_{gb}$ taken large, so that the touching fraction is removed
essentially instantaneously and the width of the denuded zone is a
\emph{prediction} rather than a rate parameter.

Three properties of \cref{eq:gbloop} are worth stating explicitly.

\textbf{It is automatically realizable.} What remains after removing the mass
above a threshold is a genuine sub-measure of the original distribution, so
$\Delta_k\ge1$ continues to hold by construction. No channel written this way
can drive the moment triple out of the region where it is the moment triple of
something.

\textbf{The defects do not return to the pool.} This is the whole difference
between \cref{eq:gbloop} and the dissolution channel, which credits the freed
vacancies back to $\spvec c_M$. A loop swallowed by a free surface takes its
defects out of the crystal, so \cref{eq:gbloop} is booked to a grain-boundary
ledger and to nothing else. Adding it to the dissolution term as well would
return the atoms twice, once to the pool and once to the boundary --- and the
symptom would be a conservation residual of the wrong sign rather than an error.

\textbf{The distribution must be truncated at the crystal.} The log-normal has
unbounded support and the crystal does not, so without a cap the far tail of
\cref{eq:lognormal} contains loops larger than the grain, and \cref{eq:mgb}
faithfully absorbs them at every distance --- reporting absorption at the center
of a grain that no loop can reach. Capping the family at
$m_{\max}=(R_{\max}/\lambda_k)^{2}$ with $R_{\max}$ the inradius replaces
\cref{eq:Phij} by the mass between the threshold and the cap over the mass below
the cap,
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_\theta)
  =\frac{\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_\theta)-\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_{\max})}
         {1-\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_{\max})},
  \label{eq:trunc}
\end{equation}
which reduces to $\Phi^{(j)}_k$ exactly as $m_{\max}\to\infty$. The carried
moments are still inverted for $(\mu_k,s_k)$ as if untruncated, so closure and
support disagree at order $\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_{\max})$; making them consistent means
inverting a \emph{truncated} log-normal from its truncated moments, which has no
closed form and would put a Newton iteration inside every residual. The
inconsistency is bounded by the very tail being discarded, which is the tail that
has no business existing. Measured on the calculation of \cref{sec:results}, the
truncation leaves the near field unchanged to four digits, sets the absorption
rate at $1\,\mu$m from a boundary to exactly zero where it had been finite, and
raises it at $200\,$nm by $4\%$ through the renormalization.

\subsection{The transfer ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

Every transfer is audited on three totals, each summed over the whole crystal
regardless of which representation holds them:
\begin{align}
  \text{(I) loop number} &&
    N &= \sum_j n^k_{sj}V_j \;+\; \#\{\text{discrete loops}\}, \nonumber\\
  \text{(II) stored defects} &&
    C &= \sum_j \frac{c^k_{sj}}{\Omega}V_j
         \;+\; \sum_\ell \frac{\pi R_\ell^{2}(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k}{\Omega},
         \label{eq:ledger}\\
  \text{(III) sink strength} &&
    S &= \sum_j 2\pi R^k_{sj}n^k_{sj}V_j \;+\; \sum_\ell 2\pi R_\ell . \nonumber
\end{align}
(I) and (II) are conservation statements and hold to the sampling tolerance of
one loop and to machine precision respectively. (III) is a \emph{modeling}
statement, and it is the one that catches mistakes: a discontinuity in it at a
transfer means the two representations disagree about what a loop of a given
content is, and that is a crystallography error rather than a numerical one.
Reducing the residual sink discontinuity from a factor of $4.85$ to $2\%$ was
achieved by removing an inconsistency in the \cloop{} Burgers vector between the
two sides --- the continuum sizing a basal loop with the full $[0001]$ where the
discrete side used $\tfrac12[0001]$ --- and not by tuning anything.

\textbf{A transfer is all-or-nothing per family.} The continuum carries one mean
size per family per node, so a size-selective transfer is not representable:
there is no spectrum to split. More importantly, a family that is discrete
\emph{and} still present in the continuum is a sink twice over --- once through
its Green's function and once through \cref{eq:KL} --- and a loop whose own sink
remains in the continuum samples its own depletion field at its own line. Zeroing
the transferred family removes both faults, and they are the same fault.
