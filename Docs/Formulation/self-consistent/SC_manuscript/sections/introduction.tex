\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Zirconium and its alloys --- Zircaloy-2 and Zircaloy-4 in light-water reactor
fuel cladding, Zr--2.5\,Nb in CANDU pressure tubes, and the low-tin variants M5
and ZIRLO in pressurized-water reactor service --- operate under prolonged
neutron exposure at displacement rates of $10^{-8}$ to
$10^{-7}\,\mathrm{dpa\,s^{-1}}$ and accumulate 5--30\,dpa over a fuel-cycle
lifetime~\citep{Adamson2019,LemaignanMotta1994}. The microstructural changes
that follow --- nucleation and growth of \aloop{} prismatic loops, the
comparatively delayed appearance of \cloop{}-component basal vacancy loops,
dislocation channeling under load, anisotropic irradiation growth and creep, and
stress-driven hydride reorientation --- are the principal source of property
degradation in Zr-based components, and they manifest as hardening, loss of
uniform elongation, axial growth of fuel assemblies and creep-down of pressure
tubes~\citep{Northwood1977,Griffiths1988,OnimusBechade2012,Cinbiz2016}.
Predicting those changes across the joint envelope of dose, temperature, texture
and applied stress is the central quantitative problem of fuel-element and
pressure-tube qualification.

Cluster dynamics, in the form it took in the late 1970s and early
1980s~\citep{Ghoniem1977,Ghoniem1979a,Ghoniem1980,Hayns1976,Kiritani1973}, remains
the workhorse mesoscopic theory for that prediction. It treats each defect
species and size as a population whose concentration evolves under coupled master
rate equations balancing in-cascade production, diffusion-controlled reactions,
thermal emission and absorption at fixed
sinks~\citep{BrailsfordBullough1972,Wiedersich1972,Mansur1978}. Its advantages
are access to time scales far beyond molecular
dynamics~\citep{Bacon2009,Marian2017}, explicit resolution of the cluster-size
distribution and therefore of every quantity entering a macroscopic property
model~\citep{GolubovSingh2001,Holt1988}, and a cost low enough relative to
kinetic Monte Carlo that it can serve as the meeting point between atomistic
parameterization~\citep{Christensen2015,PatraTrinkle2019} and engineering-scale
prediction~\citep{KohnertWirth2017,OnimusBechade2012}.

In its classical form, however, cluster dynamics is a spatially averaged,
mean-field theory. Every concentration is a single number for the whole
material, every sink is represented by a sink strength $k^{2}$ already averaged
over an assumed random and statistically uniform distribution of absorbers, and
the transport connecting a mobile defect to a sink appears only through that
average. Three distinct assumptions are folded into this, and each fails under
conditions that are not exotic. The first is spatial uniformity: a sink strength
is meaningful only if the diffusion field around each absorber relaxes to a
common far-field value before it meets the next absorber, and near a free
surface, a grain boundary or a denuded band it does not, the mobile
concentration varying by orders of magnitude over a distance comparable with the
boundary-layer width. The failure is structural rather than quantitative,
because a single average concentration then describes no point in the material;
in the calculations reported below, the outermost shell of a
\SI{500}{\nano\meter} grain carries essentially the entire prismatic loop
population, so a domain average of the loop density is a statement about the
boundary layer and not about the material. The second is diluteness. The
Avrami-type kernels that represent loop--loop and loop--network interaction are
derived by assuming absorbers are placed independently and at random; once the
volume fraction swept by growing loops becomes appreciable the placement is no
longer independent, since loops that have coalesced are correlated by
construction, and the mean-field estimate of the overlap probability ceases to
estimate anything. That is the point at which the population must be represented
as discrete objects rather than as a density, and locating it is a prediction
the model can make about itself. The third is an isotropy inherited from cubic
metals. Self-interstitial migration in $\alpha$-Zr is strongly anisotropic,
prism-plane mobility exceeding basal mobility by roughly an order of magnitude,
and vacancy migration is anisotropic more
weakly~\citep{Christensen2015,PatraTrinkle2019}; most cluster-dynamics treatments
of zirconium nevertheless retain isotropic diffusion constants taken from the
body-centered cubic literature and recover the missing directionality through
independently fitted bias factors. That conflation permits a model to hold
simultaneously that diffusion is isotropic and that its sinks are biased by the
anisotropy of that same diffusion, and it severs the relation between
atomic-scale parameters and predicted dimensional change.

Spatially resolved cluster dynamics replaces the averaged concentrations by
fields $c(\xv,t)$ obeying reaction--diffusion equations on a meshed domain, so
that the transport a mean-field sink strength summarizes is instead solved
for~\citep{spatialCD2024,christien2009cluster,barashev2015theoretical}. Three
consequences follow that a mean-field model cannot supply. The boundary layer
becomes resolved rather than parameterized: a grain boundary is no longer a
number $k^{2}_{gb}\sim6\sqrt{k^{2}}/d$ but a Dirichlet condition, and the
depleted zone in front of it --- whose width is set by the competition between
the local diffusivity and the local sink density, and which therefore differs for
each mobile species and evolves through the transient --- becomes an output. The
denuded zones seen in irradiated microstructures are a direct consequence, and
they are among the few observations that speak to the transport parameters
rather than to the reaction parameters. Quantities degenerate in a mean-field
model also become distinguishable: \cref{sec:coexistence} shows that diffusional
anisotropy opens a window of the mobile flux ratio over which prismatic
interstitial and basal vacancy loops grow together, but that the window is a
condition on one number, which a mean-field model carries once and a spatially
resolved model carries as a field, so that the observed coexistence of both loop
characters can be a statement about different points of one grain rather than
about a single balance holding everywhere. Finally, the fields are the natural
input to a discrete model, since dislocation dynamics, micromechanical models of
channeling and fracture models all require defect positions and sizes rather than
densities; a spatially resolved continuum field carries the information a
discrete population must reproduce, and the conversion between the two becomes a
well-posed problem with conservation laws attached rather than a rescaling of a
single
average~\citep{li2025coupledCDDD,ji2022concurrent,li2020coupled}.

The model presented here is self-consistent in two distinct and independent
senses, and the term is used in this paper only for their conjunction. In the
first, internal to the continuum model, every bias factor follows from
anisotropic diffusion theory: each capture efficiency appearing in a loop growth
law, and each sink strength the same loops impose on the mobile species, is
generated from the same anisotropic diffusion tensors that transport those
species, through the tensorial form of the diffusional-anisotropy-difference
correction~\citep{woo1988theory,woo1981sink,woo1982intrinsic,po2026tensorialdad}.
This holds for all variants, the three prismatic habits of each character and
the three basal states alike, so one anisotropy parameter per mobile species
fixes the bias of every one of the nine families and no independent set of
phenomenological bias factors appears anywhere in the model. What the constraint
rules out is the possibility described above, of diffusing the mobile species
with one anisotropy while biasing the sinks with an independently fitted pair;
generating both from a single $p^{m}$ removes it by construction, at the price
that a change in transport anisotropy now moves the growth laws and the sink
strengths together, in the directions the transport dictates, and cannot be
absorbed by refitting a bias. In the second sense, which holds between the
continuum and the discrete descriptions, the transfer between them preserves the
quantities that make them descriptions of one microstructure. \Cref{sec:conversion}
states the requirements and their ranking: stored defects exactly, loop number
to the integer draw, the dispersion of the size distribution to the truncation of
the sampling, and the sink strength reported rather than forced. That second
sense is what makes a hybrid calculation meaningful, continuum where the
population is dilute and discrete where it is not, with the choice made on
grounds of dose and computational cost rather than of physics. The two senses
meet at the loop's capture cross-section, which the first makes a function of
the transport tensors alone and which the second requires the discrete
population to reproduce; a failure of either therefore appears in the transfer
ledger of \cref{sec:ledger} as a discontinuity in sink strength, which is why
that ledger is reported and not merely checked.

The immobile population is correspondingly expanded, from the lumped
stress-aligned pair of the earlier mean-field model to nine crystallographically
resolved families: the three prismatic variants $a_1,a_2,a_3$ of both
interstitial and vacancy character, and a three-state basal vacancy chain running
from the stacking-fault pyramid through the faulted loop to the perfect loop. The
expansion follows the experimental loop
character~\citep{jostsons1977nature,griffiths1988review,carpenter1981study,%
tournadre2012experimental,harte2017effect}, which reports prismatic \aloop{}
loops of both signs and basal \cloop{}-component loops that are predominantly
faulted and that form by collapse of a pyramidal precursor rather than by
accretion. Each family carries the first three moments of its size distribution,
so that the width of the distribution and not only its mean is transported, and
the conversion to a discrete population can draw loop sizes from a distribution
rather than assigning every loop at a point the same size.

This paper reports the formulation, its numerical solution, and its first
application to a spatially resolved single crystal. It presents an SRCD
formulation for $\alpha$-Zr that is self-consistent in the first sense above,
with the capture efficiencies and sink strengths of all nine families generated
from the diffusion tensors and no independent bias
(\cref{sec:classes,sec:formulation}); a two-time-scale operator split that makes
of order $10^{6}$ stiff ordinary differential equations per substep tractable,
together with the cost argument justifying it and the splitting error that pays
for it (\cref{sec:split}); a continuum-to-discrete conversion that is
self-consistent in the second sense, with its conservation guarantees stated and
ranked and its residual discrepancies measured, and a grain-boundary treatment
that makes the boundary a sink for immobile loops as well as for mobile defects
(\cref{sec:conversion}); a calibration against 78 experimental measurements of
loop density and diameter, presented as a maximum \emph{a posteriori} estimate
under stated priors and with an explicit account of which parameters the data
determine and which the bounds determine (\cref{sec:calibration}); and a
\SI{500}{\nano\meter} hexagonal single-crystal calculation to 10\,dpa reporting
the mobile and immobile fields, the closed atom balance including the boundary
channel, the predicted denuded zone, the discrete loop population the fields
imply, and the reconstructed size distribution of every variant
(\cref{sec:results}).

The remainder is organized as follows. \Cref{sec:classes} fixes the defect
classes and the reactions among them. \Cref{sec:formulation} gives the
formulation, beginning from the general master equation and reducing it to the
equations actually integrated. \Cref{sec:split} is the solution method,
\cref{sec:conversion} the continuum-to-discrete map and the grain-boundary
models, and \cref{sec:calibration} the calibration. \Cref{sec:results} is the
single-crystal study and \cref{sec:conclusions} draws the conclusions and states
what remains open. Four appendices collect the capture-efficiency derivation,
the moment closure, the peripheral-emission model that replaces fitted annealing
lifetimes, and the state-vector layout.
