\section{Cluster Dynamics Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{From the master equation to the reduced system}
\label{sec:master}

The general cluster-dynamics master equation for a population indexed by a
species label $\alpha$ and a defect content $n$ is a balance of gains and losses
over the edges of the graph of \cref{fig:rag}. It is written here with one term
per edge class of \cref{tab:rag-processes}, because everything the rest of this
paper does to it is a restriction or a moment of \emph{this} equation and
nothing is added to it later:
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial C^{\alpha}_{n}}{\partial t}
  =\;&\underbrace{-\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm J^{\alpha}_{n}}_{\text{transport}}
   \;+\;\underbrace{G^{\alpha}_{n}}_{\text{P4, P5}}
   \;+\;\underbrace{\tfrac12\sum_{\beta\gamma}\sum_{n'n''}
        \nu^{\alpha n}_{\beta n',\gamma n''}\,
        \mathcal K^{\beta\gamma}_{n',n''}\,C^{\beta}_{n'}C^{\gamma}_{n''}}
        _{\text{P1, P2, P6, P7}}
   \nonumber\\[2pt]
  &+\underbrace{\tfrac12\sum_{n'}\mathcal C^{\alpha}_{n',\,n-n'}
        C^{\alpha}_{n'}C^{\alpha}_{n-n'}
      \;-\;C^{\alpha}_{n}\sum_{n'}\mathcal C^{\alpha}_{n,n'}C^{\alpha}_{n'}}
        _{\text{P9, coalescence}}
   \;+\;\underbrace{\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{n+1}C^{\alpha}_{n+1}
        -\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{n}C^{\alpha}_{n}}_{\text{P3, P8}}
   \nonumber\\[2pt]
  &+\underbrace{\sum_{\beta}\bigl[\theta^{\beta\to\alpha}_{n}C^{\beta}_{n}
        -\theta^{\alpha\to\beta}_{n}C^{\alpha}_{n}\bigr]}_{\text{P12}}
   \;-\;\underbrace{\bigl[\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{N}(n)
        +\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{gb}(n)
        +\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{van}}(n)\bigr]C^{\alpha}_{n}}
        _{\text{P10, P11, P14, P15}} ,
  \label{eq:master}
\end{align}
closed on the mobile species by the grain-boundary condition
\begin{equation}
  C^{\alpha}_{n}\big|_{\partial\Omega_D}=C^{\alpha,\mathrm{eq}}_{n},
  \qquad \alpha\in\mathcal M
  \qquad\text{(P13),}
  \label{eq:master-bc}
\end{equation}
so that the boundary enters the formulation twice and at the same level as
every other channel: as \cref{eq:master-bc} on the species that reach it by
diffusion, and as $\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{gb}$ on the loops that reach it by
growing.

The symbols are the following. $\bm J^{\alpha}_{n}=-\bm D^{\alpha}_{n}\nabla
C^{\alpha}_{n}$ is the diffusive flux, which vanishes identically on every
immobile index. $G^{\alpha}_{n}$ is the cascade source, one displacement rate
partitioned over the mobile species (P4) and over the loop families at their
nucleation sizes (P5). $\mathcal K^{\beta\gamma}_{n',n''}$ is the rate constant
of the encounter between $(\beta,n')$ and $(\gamma,n'')$ and
$\nu^{\alpha n}_{\beta n',\gamma n''}$ the net number of $(\alpha,n)$ that one
such event produces --- negative when $(\alpha,n)$ is a reactant, $-2$ when a
single event consumes two of it --- so that one term carries recombination,
mobile clustering, homogeneous loop nucleation and capture at a loop, and
carries them with their stoichiometry rather than with a sign convention
imposed by hand.

$\mathcal C^{\alpha}_{n',n''}$ is the coalescence kernel, the rate at which two
members of the \emph{same} family holding $n'$ and $n''$ defects merge into one
holding $n'+n''$. It is separated from $\mathcal K$ because both of its partners
are immobile, which is exactly the property that keeps it out of the size ladder
of \cref{sec:master} and makes it a Smoluchowski coagulation rather than a
transition on that ladder; its gain and loss terms are the standard pair, and
the $\tfrac12$ in the gain removes the double count over the ordered pairs
$(n',n-n')$.

$\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{n}$ is the thermal emission rate, covering both a mobile
cluster shedding a point defect (P3) and a vacancy loop emitting across its own
perimeter (P8). $\theta^{\beta\to\alpha}_{n}$ is a first-order transfer that
moves a cluster from one family to another at fixed defect content, which is the
basal chain (P12) and nothing else in this model. And the three $\mathcal D$
are first-order removals at fixed sinks: the dislocation network, absorbing
mobile species (P11) and whole loops (P10); the grain boundary, absorbing loops
large enough to reach it (P14); and the dissolution of loops that have shrunk
below the minimum stable size (P15). All three are written as functions of $n$
because two of them are size-gated, and that gate is the only reason the last
two are separate channels rather than one.

\Cref{eq:master} is one equation per $(\alpha,n)$ and per mesh node, and is not
solvable as written for the size ranges of interest.

Two reductions bring it to the system integrated here, and they cut in different
directions.

The first truncates the mobile ladder. Only the monomers and the smallest
interstitial clusters migrate. The mobile set is the $\mathcal M$ of
\cref{eq:mobile-classes}, closed at
$\spvec{m}_M=[-1,+1,+2,+3]^{T}$ --- monovacancy, mono-, di- and tri-interstitial
--- with the signed entries $m^k$ carrying the polarity
$\varsigma^{k}=m^{k}/|m^{k}|\in\{\pm1\}$, positive for an interstitial species
and negative for a vacancy. That $\varsigma$ is the only polarity symbol in this
paper: each immobile family carries the same sign as its character,
$\varsigma^{s}=+1$ for $s=i$ and $-1$ for $s=v$, and the $\varsigma_s(m)$ that
appears in the growth law \cref{eq:growth} and in the flux sums
\cref{eq:d-Sigmas} is nothing but the product $\varsigma^{s}\varsigma^{m}$ of
the two. It is not to be confused with the diffusional anisotropy $p^{m}$ of
\cref{eq:pm}, which is a positive real and shares no meaning with it beyond a
species index. Everything larger is immobile and is carried by the second
reduction. The mobile block retains its spatial derivative, because transport is
what it is for.

The second truncates the immobile ladder in a different variable, keeping the
variants and giving up the size resolution. For each family the ladder in $n$
is replaced by the first three moments of
the size distribution of each family in \cref{eq:families},
\begin{equation}
  n^k_{sL}=\!\int\! f_k\,\mathrm{d}m,
  \qquad
  c^k_{sL}=\!\int\! m f_k\,\mathrm{d}m,
  \qquad
  q^k_{sL}=\!\int\! m^2 f_k\,\mathrm{d}m,
  \label{eq:moments-def}
\end{equation}
closed by the log-normal ansatz of \cref{sec:moments}. The immobile block loses
its spatial derivative entirely: a loop does not diffuse, and the only thing
that couples one node's loops to another's is the mobile field. That is not an
approximation --- it is the property that makes the numerical method of
\cref{sec:split} possible.

\subsection{The mobile block}
\label{sec:mobile}

Because the mobile species reach quasi-steady state on a time scale
$\tau_M\sim(k^2D)^{-1}$ of order microseconds, while the immobile families evolve
on the dose scale ($1\,\mathrm{dpa}=10^{7}\,\mathrm{s}$ at
$G=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{dpa\,s^{-1}}$), the mobile block is solved as a
\emph{steady} reaction--diffusion problem for the immobile state currently held.
Writing $\spvec c_M=[c^v,c^i,c^{2i},c^{3i}]^{T}$ in per-atom units,
\begin{equation}
  \spvec 0 = -\nabla\!\cdot\!\spvec J
    + \spvec P_M
    + \tfrac12\,(\spmat I\otimes\spvec c_M^{T})\,\spmat R_2\,\spvec c_M
    + (\spmat E-\spmat D)\,\spvec c_M
    + \spvec S_{vL},
  \label{eq:master-c}
\end{equation}
where upright sans-serif bold denotes arrays in \emph{species space} and
bold italic ordinary Cartesian vectors and tensors, so that the entries
$\bm J^{x}=-\bm D^{x}\nabla c^{x}$ of the stacked flux $\spvec J$ are themselves
Cartesian vectors and $\nabla\cdot$ acts on each.

The terms are, in order: the cascade production
$\spvec P_M=G\varepsilon\spvec f$, with $G$ the displacement rate, $\varepsilon$
the surviving efficiency and $\spvec f$ the partition of production among the
mobile species; the second-order reactions $\spmat R_2$, whose entries are the
pairwise rate constants
\begin{equation}
  \beta^{m,n}=\frac{A^{m,n}}{\Omega}\,(\overline D^{m}+\overline D^{n})(r^{m}+r^{n}),
  \qquad
  r^{m}=\begin{cases}
    \left(\dfrac{3\Omega}{4\pi}\right)^{1/3}, & |m^{m}|=1,\\[9pt]
    \left(\dfrac{|m^{m}|\,\Omega}{\pi b}\right)^{1/2}, & |m^{m}|>1,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:rates}
\end{equation}
with the recombination-volume factor $\beta_r$ carried on the
interstitial--vacancy channel and the combinatorial factors retained on the
like-species channels; the thermal emission matrix $\spmat E$, whose entries are
$\alpha^{m,n}=\beta^{m,n}\exp(-E^{b}_{m,n}/k_BT)$; the linear absorption matrix
$\spmat D$; and $\spvec S_{vL}$, the vacancies returned to the pool by
peripheral emission from vacancy loops (\cref{app:emission}).

The absorption matrix is where the immobile population acts back on the mobile
one, and it is written so that no defect can be counted twice:
\begin{equation}
  \spmat D = \mathrm{diag}\{\spvec\omega\odot\spvec C_s\}
             + \spmat D_d\,\spmat K_{\mathcal L},
  \qquad
  \spmat D_d=\mathrm{diag}\{\overline D^{v},\overline D^{i},
                            \overline D^{2i},\overline D^{3i}\},
  \label{eq:KM}
\end{equation}
whose first term carries the \emph{network alone},
\begin{equation}
  C_s^{v}=\frac{a^2}{z_c}\rho_N,
  \qquad
  C_s^{i}=\frac{a^2}{z_c}Z_N\rho_N,
  \label{eq:sinkconc}
\end{equation}
and whose second carries the loops through the diagonal sink-strength block
\begin{equation}
  \spmat K_{\mathcal L}=\mathrm{diag}\{\spvec k^{2}\},
  \qquad
  k_m^{2}=\sum_{(s,k)\in\mathcal F_L}\! Z_{sk,m}\,\rho^k_{sL}
        \;+\;Z_{vc_0,m}\,\alpha_{\mathrm{sfp}}\,\pi R^{c_0}_{vL}\,
             \frac{n^{c_0}_{vL}}{\Omega}
        \;+\;Z_{N,m}\,\rho_N .
  \label{eq:KL}
\end{equation}
The loops must not also appear in \cref{eq:sinkconc}, or their absorption would
be counted once as a network sink and once as loop growth. The middle term of
\cref{eq:KL} is the stacking-fault pyramid, which has no line density and enters
through a compact capture cross-section instead; \cref{eq:Pi-identity} below
shows that a cross-section is the only thing either side ever asks of a family.

The block is closed by its boundary conditions. On a free surface or a grain
boundary the mobile species are pinned at thermal equilibrium,
\begin{equation}
  c^{v}=c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}=\exp(-E^{f}_v/k_BT),
  \qquad
  c^{i}=c^{2i}=c^{3i}=0 \quad\text{on }\partial\Omega_D,
  \label{eq:bc}
\end{equation}
which is process class P13 of \cref{tab:rag-processes}. This replaces the
mean-field grain-boundary sink strength $k^2_{gb}\simeq6\sqrt{k^2}/d$ by a
boundary condition, and the depleted layer in front of it becomes an output
rather than a parameter. Faces declared periodic instead carry periodic
conditions on all four species.

\subsection{Anisotropic transport and the single anisotropy parameter}
\label{sec:transport}

Each mobile species carries a diffusion tensor
$D^{x}_{ij}=D^{x}_{0ij}\exp(-E^{xm}_{ij}/k_BT)$, diagonal in the crystal frame
with the basal components equal and the $c$-axis component distinct. The whole
of that anisotropy is expressed by one number per species,
\begin{equation}
  p^{m}=\left(\frac{D^{m}_{\parallel}}{D^{m}_{\mathrm{basal}}}\right)^{1/6},
  \label{eq:pm}
\end{equation}
and the migration energies are generated from it at \emph{fixed} effective
diffusivity,
\begin{equation}
  E^{m}_{\langle11\rangle}=E^{m}_{\langle22\rangle}=E^{m}_{\mathrm{eff}}+2k_BT\ln p^{m},
  \qquad
  E^{m}_{\langle33\rangle}=E^{m}_{\mathrm{eff}}-4k_BT\ln p^{m},
  \label{eq:Emsplit}
\end{equation}
so that the orientation-averaged diffusivity
$\overline D^{m}=(\det\bm D^{m})^{1/3}$ is exactly the scalar $D_m$ of the
mean-field kinetics. No mobility is redefined by the anisotropy --- only
its directional weighting. This is the technical device that makes the
formulation self-consistent: \cref{eq:pm,eq:Emsplit} are a change of variable in
which $p^{m}$ is the only anisotropy the model has, and \cref{sec:efficiencies}
uses that same $p^{m}$ and no other parameter.

\subsection{Capture efficiencies}
\label{sec:efficiencies}

Every capture efficiency is a product of factors, one per degeneracy to be
broken:
\begin{equation}
  Z_{sk,m}(\bm\sigma)
  = \underbrace{Z^{0}_m}_{\text{elastic bias}}\;
    \underbrace{A_{h(k)}(p^{m})}_{\text{DAD, orientation}}\;
    \underbrace{X_{s,m}}_{\text{character}}\;
    \underbrace{\left[1+\frac{\Delta Z^{\mathrm{SIPA}}_{h(k),m}(\sigma_{kk})}
                            {Z^{0}_mA_{h(k)}(p^{m})}\right]}_{\text{stress, SIPA}},
  \label{eq:Zsk}
\end{equation}
with $h(k)\in\{a,c\}$ the habit of family $k$ and the Woo diffusional-anisotropy
factors~\citep{woo1988theory,woo1981sink,po2026tensorialdad}
\begin{equation}
  A_a(p)=\frac{p+p^{-2}}{2},
  \qquad
  A_c(p)=p .
  \label{eq:Afactors}
\end{equation}
Each factor breaks exactly one degeneracy and is silent on the others.
$A_{h(k)}$ depends on the habit alone --- on the angle between the loop normal
and the $c$ axis --- and therefore assigns one value to all six prismatic
families and one to all three basal states, the pyramid included, whose base is
$(0001)$ so that $h(c_0)=c$. It separates prismatic from basal but is degenerate
in character. $X_{s,m}$ is the character factor, which separates an interstitial
from a vacancy loop of the same habit~\citep{marchrico2023,marchrico2025}; the
coexistence window it opens has log-width $\ln\chi$ with
$\chi=X_{iI}X_{vV}/(X_{iV}X_{vI})$.

\Cref{eq:Zsk} contains no free bias. $A_{h(k)}$ is built from the same
$p^{m}$ that \cref{eq:Emsplit} puts into the diffusion tensors, so a change in
the transport anisotropy moves the growth laws and the sink strengths of
\cref{eq:KL} together. The two remaining constants, $Z^{0}_m$ and $X_{s,m}$, are
calibrated (\cref{sec:calibration}); the orientation dependence is not.

The forms in \cref{eq:Afactors} are not monotone in the same way, and the
consequence is quantitative and easily missed. $A_c(p)=p$ increases without
bound, while $A_a(p)=(p+p^{-2})/2$ has its minimum at $p=2^{1/3}=1.2599$. A
single change of $p^{v}$ therefore pushes the basal and prismatic families in
\emph{opposite} directions, starving \cloop{} growth while accelerating \aloop{}
shrinkage, or the reverse. Any statement about the effect of anisotropy that
does not distinguish the two habits is incomplete.

\subsection{Conditions for the growth of every variant}
\label{sec:coexistence}

Whether a family grows reduces to a single number, and that is the difficulty
this subsection resolves. Each compares one flux ratio against a threshold of
its own, so under identical local conditions an interstitial and a vacancy loop
of one orientation cannot both grow --- yet both are observed. What follows
identifies which idealization must fail, shows that diffusional anisotropy alone
cannot be it, and gives the two mechanisms that can. It is also what forces the
formulation to be self-consistent in sense~(a) of \cref{sec:intro}.

Define the mobile flux aggregates and their ratio,
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_I=\sum_{n\in I_{\mathrm{mob}}}n\,\overline D^{I_n}c^{I_n},
  \qquad
  \Phi_V=\overline D^{v}c^{v},
  \qquad
  x\equiv\frac{\Phi_V}{\Phi_I},
  \label{eq:fluxscales}
\end{equation}
reducing to $\Phi_I=\overline D^{i}c^{i}$, $\Phi_V=\overline D^{v}c^{v}$ in the
monomer approximation. With $Z_{s,m}$ the capture efficiency of family $s$ for
species $m$, the driving force on a family of character sign $\eta_s=\pm1$ is
\begin{equation}
  F_s=\eta_s\left(Z_{s,I}\Phi_I-Z_{s,V}\Phi_V\right),
  \label{eq:Fsigma}
\end{equation}
so each family grows or shrinks according to which side of its own threshold $x$
falls on:
\begin{equation}
  \text{interstitial family }s\text{ grows}\iff x<\frac{Z_{s,I}}{Z_{s,V}},
  \qquad
  \text{vacancy family }s\text{ grows}\iff x>\frac{Z_{s,I}}{Z_{s,V}} .
  \label{eq:growconds}
\end{equation}

That is the whole of the growth problem, and it is uncomfortable. Every family
reduces to \emph{one} threshold, the ratio of its own two efficiencies, and $x$
is a single number: whichever side of a shared threshold $x$ lies on, one of the
families sharing it is shrinking. Yet both prismatic characters are observed
together in irradiated
Zr~\citep{jostsons1977nature,griffiths1988review,carpenter1981study,%
marchrico2025}. Coexistence therefore says some idealization has failed ---
homogeneity, identical capture coefficients, nucleation pathways, variants, or
irradiation history.

Diffusional anisotropy removes the first. With \cref{eq:pm,eq:Afactors} the
prismatic and basal thresholds separate,
\begin{equation}
  x_a=\frac{Z^{0}_{I}A_a(p^{i})}{Z^{0}_{V}A_a(p^{v})},
  \qquad
  x_c=\frac{Z^{0}_{I}A_c(p^{i})}{Z^{0}_{V}A_c(p^{v})},
  \label{eq:thresholds}
\end{equation}
and since $A_a(p)=(p+p^{-2})/2$ exceeds $A_c(p)=p$ for $p<1$ and equals it at
$p=1$, $x_c<x_a$ whenever $p^{i}<p^{v}$. Prismatic interstitial and basal
vacancy loops then grow \emph{together} throughout
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;x_c<x<x_a\;},
  \qquad
  \ln\frac{x_a}{x_c}=\ln\frac{g(p^{v})}{g(p^{i})},
  \qquad
  g(p)\equiv\frac{2}{1+p^{-3}},
  \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
with $g$ strictly increasing, so the window opens exactly when $p^{i}<p^{v}$ and
closes as $p^{i}\to p^{v}$; for $p^{v}\simeq1$ its width is
$(Z^{0}_{I}/Z^{0}_{V})(1-(p^{i})^{3})/2(p^{i})^{2}$. At $200\,^{\circ}$C it is
open but narrow, $x_c\approx1.0<x<x_a\approx1.2$, at
$Z^{0}_{I}/Z^{0}_{V}\approx1.05$--$1.15$ and
$p^{i}\approx0.89$--$0.92$~\citep{woo1988theory,christien2009cluster,%
barashev2015theoretical,li2020coupled}.

Three things are routinely conflated. The elastic bias opens nothing:
$Z^{0}_{I}/Z^{0}_{V}$ multiplies \emph{both} thresholds in \cref{eq:thresholds}
and cancels from the width, though it narrows the window by pushing $x_c$ toward
unity. The production bias positions $x$ inside it --- the excess of freely
migrating vacancies left when more of the interstitial yield is locked into
in-cascade clusters, $\eta=\varepsilon_V/\varepsilon_I\gtrsim1$, of which
$0$--$20\%$ suffices. Neither substitutes for the other: at $p^{i}=p^{v}$ no $\eta$
satisfies both conditions, and with $\eta$ misplaced the model grows one family
and dissolves the other. Third, anisotropy is degenerate in character.
\Cref{eq:Zsk} gives every family sharing a habit one factor $A_{h(k)}(p^{m})$,
so the interstitial and vacancy prismatic variants share a threshold,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{Z_{a_k I,I}}{Z_{a_k I,V}}
  \;\xrightarrow[\ \text{DAD alone}\ ]{}\;
  \frac{Z_{a_k V,I}}{Z_{a_k V,V}} ,
  \label{eq:dad-degenerate}
\end{equation}
the prismatic window closes identically, and the mechanism is silent on exactly
the coexistence observed. Two mechanisms remove that degeneracy, and the
formulation carries both.

The first, Resolution~1, promotes $x$ to a field
$x(\xv,t)=\Phi_V(\xv,t)/\Phi_I(\xv,t)$. Interstitials migrate far faster than
vacancies, so their channel responds first to any transient or gradient. In
\emph{time}, Zr's nucleation transient of order $1\,$dpa is interstitial-rich,
so $x\ll x_c$ and interstitial loops grow while vacancy loops nucleate and sit
sub-critical; as the vacancy supersaturation builds, $x$ rises into $(x_c,x_a)$
and the vacancy families grow alongside those already established. In
\emph{space}, sinks --- boundaries, surfaces, precipitates, the network --- make
the fields non-uniform, and the longer interstitial diffusion length drains
interstitials to the far field while vacancies pile up locally, so $x$ is
depressed away from sinks and raised near them, and one specimen holds regions
on both sides of the threshold; \cref{sec:results-mobile} measures that
structure, boundary layers of $8$--$46\,$nm differing by species. It costs
nothing --- a spatially resolved formulation carries $x(\xv,t)$ natively --- but
leaves $Z_{a_kI,\cdot}=Z_{a_kV,\cdot}$ intact and evaluates the two conditions
at different points of space and time, so it predicts \emph{segregated}
coexistence, not local simultaneous growth.

The second, Resolution~2, removes the degeneracy itself. March-Rico, Blondel and
Wirth~\citep{marchrico2025} compute the defect--loop interaction energy
atomistically and replace geometric intersection by a drift--diffusion capture,
\begin{equation}
  \bm J_\alpha=-\bm D_\alpha\left[\nabla c^{\alpha}
    +\frac{c^{\alpha}}{k_BT}\nabla E^{\alpha L}_{\mathrm{int}}\right],
  \qquad \alpha=i,v,
  \label{eq:drift-diffusion}
\end{equation}
which reduces to effective capture radii entering additively in the reaction
volume. \emph{Spontaneous} radii are nearly equal for all loops and separate
nothing; \emph{thermal-drift} radii do, an interstitial loop's dilatational
field attracting interstitials over a longer range than vacancies and conversely
for a vacancy loop. That bias is the factor $X_{s,m}$ of \cref{eq:Zsk}, and
with the character-splitting factor
\begin{equation}
  \chi\equiv
  \frac{Z_{a_kI,I}/Z_{a_kI,V}}{Z_{a_kV,I}/Z_{a_kV,V}}
  =\frac{X_{iI}X_{vV}}{X_{iV}X_{vI}},
  \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
the prismatic window $Z_{a_kV,I}/Z_{a_kV,V}<x<Z_{a_kI,I}/Z_{a_kI,V}$ is
non-empty if and only if $\chi>1$, of logarithmic width $\ln\chi$ --- the
character analogue of \cref{eq:window}, which measures how far anisotropy
separates the two habits where $\ln\chi$ measures how far thermal-drift capture
separates the two characters \emph{within} one. Because $\chi>1$ opens a genuine
interval, one uniform steady $x$ satisfies both conditions at once --- the
stronger and more falsifiable claim.

Two further ingredients are not optional. In-cascade vacancy clustering supplies
the embryos the capture bias acts on, homogeneous nucleation against the
prevailing interstitial bias being prohibitively slow; and the
\emph{same} asymmetry generates $\eta$, so the two must not be fitted
independently. Cascade overlap reduces the generation rate with dose,
$\tilde g(\gamma)=\varepsilon(\gamma)g$, saturating over $0.01$--$0.1\,$dpa,
which lets coarsening rather than unbounded nucleation dominate at high dose; as
one scalar on both species it rescales dose without moving $x$, a rate effect
and not a threshold effect. \Cref{tab:mechanisms} summarizes the division of
labor.

Three requirements follow, and together they are the content of
\cref{sec:classes,sec:formulation}. The immobile families must be
crystallographic: a scalar aligned/non-aligned split says \emph{how much} of a
population a stress favors but not \emph{which} prismatic system, and means
nothing under a multiaxial stress; resolving $a_1,a_2,a_3$ makes the resolved
stress on each habit computed, and carrying both characters on each variant
makes \cref{eq:chi} representable. Splitting the basal population into
faulted and perfect states is forced separately, by the different vacancy
content per unit area the two store (\cref{tab:cd-families}).

The capture efficiencies must come from the transport tensors --- sense~(a) of
\cref{sec:intro} --- and \cref{eq:window} is why that matters rather than merely
being tidy: the window's width depends on the transport anisotropy
\emph{alone}, so a model diffusing with one anisotropy while biasing its sinks
with an independent $(\delta_i,\delta_v)$ can report a window its own transport
does not support. The pair stays recoverable as the exact map
\begin{equation}
  \delta_m=\frac{A_a(p^{m})-A_c(p^{m})}{A_a(p^{m})+A_c(p^{m})}
          =\frac{1-(p^{m})^{3}}{1+3(p^{m})^{3}},
  \label{eq:deltamap}
\end{equation}
giving $\delta_i\approx0.07$--$0.09$ and $\delta_v\approx0$ above;
$Z^{0}_{I}/Z^{0}_{V}$ is absent from it and must be carried separately, which is
why \cref{eq:Zsk} is the more faithful input.

And the mobile field must be spatially resolved, Resolution~1 being unavailable
to a $0$-D model: it needs $x(\xv,t)$, hence \cref{eq:master-c} with real
boundary conditions. That is one of two independent
reasons for the spatial treatment, the other being that surfaces and grain
boundaries are the dominant defect sink in any specimen small enough to examine
(\cref{tab:conservation}).

Two cautions remain, so neither is mistaken for a prediction. The
thermal-drift radii behind \cref{eq:chi} scale as $(T_m/T)^{m_d}$ with
$T_m=2128\,$K, so for $m_d>0$ the same-type bias is \emph{strongest at low
temperature} --- while the observed vacancy \aloop{} fraction \emph{increases}
with temperature. That is no contradiction --- the fraction is controlled
largely by nucleation, vacancy mobility and thermal emission from small vacancy
clusters, none in $\chi$ --- but reproducing it tests the combined model rather
than coming for free. The related hazard is double
counting: the same-type bias \emph{is} an elastic-interaction effect, and so is
the dislocation bias $Z^{0}_{I}/Z^{0}_{V}>1$, and only one should carry the
interstitial--vacancy asymmetry. The calibration of \cref{sec:calibration} fits
$\chi=1$, so $Z^{0}$ carries it alone --- which is to say the present parameter
set exercises Resolution~1 and not Resolution~2.

\subsection{The immobile block}
\label{sec:immobile}

The block has five channels, and they are given in turn. All nine families
obey one growth law, in which the stored
content changes under the net biased flux of the correct sign:
\begin{equation}
  \left.\dot c^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{growth}}
  = \Pi^k_{sL}\;\eta_s\!\!\sum_{m\in\{v,i,2i,3i\}}\!\!
    |m|\;\overline D^{m}\,Z_{sk,m}\,c^{m}\,\varsigma_s(m),
  \label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
with $\varsigma_s(m)=\varsigma^{s}\varsigma^{m}$ the polarity product of
\cref{sec:master}: $+1$ when the polarity of species $m$ matches the character
$s$, so that absorbing it grows the family, and $-1$ otherwise, so that
absorbing it shrinks the family. The geometric prefactor is
\begin{equation}
  \Pi^k_{sL}=\frac{\lambda_k}{\ell}\,\xi_k
             \sqrt{n^k_{sL}c^k_{sL}},
  \qquad
  \ell\equiv\frac{z_c\Omega}{2\pi a^{2}},
  \label{eq:loopprefactor}
\end{equation}
in which $\lambda_k$ is the family's own radius scale of \cref{eq:radius},
$\ell$ a length built from the capture number and the atomic volume, and
$\xi_k$ the one fitted sink scale per habit (\cref{tab:fitted}). And because $\rho^k_{sL}=2\pi\lambda_k\sqrt{n^k_{sL}c^k_{sL}}$ by
\cref{eq:radius},
\begin{equation}
  \Pi^k_{sL}=\xi_k\,\frac{\rho^k_{sL}}{2\pi\ell}
  \qquad\text{identically, for every }(s,k)\in\mathcal F_L .
  \label{eq:Pi-identity}
\end{equation}
\emph{The prefactor in the growth law is the family's own sink density divided
by $2\pi\ell$.} That identity is what makes \cref{eq:growth} and \cref{eq:KL}
two readings of one quantity, and it is what carries the growth law onto the
pyramid unchanged: a family needs no geometry beyond the capture cross-section
it presents, and the pyramid supplies that cross-section as
$\Pi^{c_0}_{vL}=\xi_{c_0}\alpha_{\mathrm{sfp}}
R^{c_0}_{vL}n^{c_0}_{vL}/2\ell$.

Nucleation is the second channel. Cascade production deposits directly into the immobile
families at a rate $G_k=G\,\epsilon_k$ per family, each new cluster carrying
$m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$ defects, so that
$\dot n^k_{sL}|_{\mathrm{nuc}}=J^k_{sL}=G_k/m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$ and
$\dot c^k_{sL}|_{\mathrm{nuc}}=G_k$. The partition $\epsilon_k$ and the
nucleation sizes are calibrated quantities.

Alongside that cascade route the interstitial prismatic families carry a
homogeneous one, from the three mobile--mobile reactions that put a cluster past
the cut-off of \cref{eq:mobile-classes}:
\begin{equation}
  \left.J^{a_k}_{iL}\right|_{\mathrm{hom}}
    =w_{a_k}\bigl(R_{i,3i}+\tfrac12R_{2i,2i}+R_{2i,3i}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \left.\dot c^{a_k}_{iL}\right|_{\mathrm{hom}}
    =w_{a_k}\bigl(4R_{i,3i}+2R_{2i,2i}+5R_{2i,3i}\bigr),
  \label{eq:homnuc}
\end{equation}
with $w_{a_k}$ the share of the three prismatic variants, $\sum_kw_{a_k}=1$.
Neither set of weights is free. Each content weight is the defect content that
same reaction removes from $\spvec c_M$ --- $1+3$ for $i\!+\!3i$, $2$ for every
di-interstitial lost to $2i\!+\!2i$, and $2+3$ for $2i\!+\!3i$ --- so what is
deposited is exactly what is withdrawn, and the three clustering entries of
\cref{eq:d-R2cluster} are the audit of it. Each number weight is one loop per
\emph{event}, which is where the $\tfrac12$ comes from: $R_{i,3i}$ and
$R_{2i,3i}$ are unlike-pair rates and count events already, while $R_{2i,2i}$,
like $R_{i,i}$ in \cref{eq:d-R2vi}, is a di-interstitial \emph{loss} rate and
two are lost per event. Content over number is then $4$, $4$ and $5$ defects on
the three channels --- the clusters $i\!+\!3i$, $2i\!+\!2i$ and $2i\!+\!3i$
actually make.

The third channel removes loops rather than feeding them. Loops are lost by
overlap with other loops and with
the network. With $N=n/\Omega$, $R=\lambda\sqrt{c/n}$, and the radii capped at
the relevant spacing ($R_{LL}=\min(R,(\Omega/n)^{1/3})$,
$R_{LN}=\min(R,\rho_N^{-1/2})$), the Avrami overlap probabilities and rate
scales are
\begin{align}
  \varphi_{LL}&=1-\exp\!\left(-\kappa_{LL}\tfrac43\pi R_{LL}^{3}N\right), &
  \varphi_{LN}&=1-\exp\!\left(-\kappa_{LN}\pi R_{LN}^{2}\rho_N\right),
  \nonumber\\
  \nu_{LL}&=c_{LL}\,v_{\mathrm{abs}}\,N^{1/3}, &
  \nu_{LN}&=c_{LN}\,v_{\mathrm{abs}}\,\sqrt{\rho_N},
  \label{eq:avrami}
\end{align}
giving the number and content losses
\begin{equation}
  \Delta N_{LN}=\nu_{LN}\varphi_{LN}n,
  \quad
  \Delta N=\nu_{LL}\varphi_{LL}n+\Delta N_{LN},
  \quad
  \Delta C=\nu_{LN}\varphi_{LN}c,
  \quad
  \Delta Q=\Delta_k\bigl(\bar m^{2}\Delta N-2\bar m\,\Delta C\bigr).
  \label{eq:coal}
\end{equation}
The like-loop channel merges two loops into a larger one and therefore
\emph{conserves content}, removing density only; the loop--network channel
removes both, and the removed content is booked as network sink absorption so
the point-defect balance stays closed. The second moment moves in opposite
directions on the two, which is why $\Delta Q$ carries one sign and not two:
substituting $\Delta C=\bar m\,\Delta N_{LN}$ collapses it to
$\Delta Q=\Delta_k\bar m^{2}(\Delta N_{LL}-\Delta N_{LN})$, a gain from the
merge and a loss from the removal. Both halves are derived in
\cref{app:derivation}, the second exactly and the first up to the one closure
this model needs for coalescence. The rate scale is set by the
\emph{absorbed}-flux climb speed $v_{\mathrm{abs}}$ rather than the net growth
velocity, so coarsening persists at saturation. The capping is what makes
\cref{eq:avrami} saturate rather than diverge as the number density collapses,
and $\varphi_{LL}\to1$ is the model's own statement that its mean-field
treatment of coalescence has expired --- the detector used in
\cref{sec:coarsening}.

The fourth channel runs the other way. Vacancy loops exchange vacancies across their own
perimeter at a rate set by a size-dependent binding energy computed from the
anisotropic loop self-energy (\cref{app:emission}), one expression serving both
characters, so that the same mechanism shrinks a vacancy loop and grows an
interstitial one and the classical climb threshold emerges rather than being
imposed. This channel replaces the two fitted annealing lifetimes of the
mean-field model. It returns vacancies to the mobile pool through $\spvec S_{vL}$
in \cref{eq:master-c}.

Emission removes content and never a loop, so on its own it leaves coalescence
as the only sink on vacancy loop \emph{number}; the disappearance channel
supplies the missing one. It is the physical content of the lifetime the
emission channel deleted, written as a size condition rather than as a fitted
rate: loops that have shrunk below a minimum stable size $m_{\mathrm{van}}$
dissolve at $\nu_{\mathrm{van}}$, and the fraction that qualifies is read off
the same log-normal, with the gate of \cref{eq:Phij} taken in the opposite
sense,
\begin{equation}
  \Xi^k_0=\bigl[1-\Phi^{(0)}_k(m_{\mathrm{van}})\bigr]n^k_{sL},
  \quad
  \Xi^k_1=\bigl[1-\Phi^{(1)}_k(m_{\mathrm{van}})\bigr]c^k_{sL},
  \quad
  \Xi^k_2=\bigl[1-\Phi^{(2)}_k(m_{\mathrm{van}})\bigr]q^k_{sL},
  \label{eq:vanish}
\end{equation}
the defects returning to the mobile pool as a dissolving loop's must. Because
$\Phi^{(2)}>\Phi^{(1)}>\Phi^{(0)}$ the complements reverse that ordering, so
what \cref{eq:vanish} removes is \emph{smaller} than the family mean: the
density falls while the surviving mean size rises. That is the one trade
coalescence cannot make --- it removes the largest loops and shortens the
survivors' lives --- and it is why the two channels are not interchangeable.

The fifth channel exists only on the basal branch. Its three states are
linked by two one-way
activated transfers,
\begin{equation}
  c_0 \;\xrightarrow{\;\nu_{\mathrm{col}}\;}\; c_f
      \;\xrightarrow{\;\nu_{\mathrm{uf}}\;}\; c_p ,
  \label{eq:basal-chain}
\end{equation}
both size-gated, and both conserving vacancies exactly: the content leaving one
state enters the next, and only the \emph{number} changes, because the states
store different vacancy content per unit area
(\cref{tab:cd-families})~\citep{liu2020two,varvenne2014vacancy,marchrico2025}.
The chain is basal and has no prismatic counterpart: \emph{ab initio}-parameterized
formation energies place the prismatic vacancy \aloop{} loop \emph{below} both
the compact cavity and the faulted basal loop, so the planar prismatic loop is
already the ground-state geometry rather than something a compact cluster must
convert into~\citep{varvenne2014vacancy}. None of the basal-chain rates
is calibrated; the formulation gives their Arrhenius form and no measurement in
this work supplies the barriers, so the chain is switched off in the calculation
of \cref{sec:results} and its consequences are discussed in
\cref{sec:conclusions}.

\subsection{The size distribution and its closure}
\label{sec:moments}

Carrying only $n$ and $c$ assigns every loop at a point the same size, and the
population that produces is monodisperse by construction. The second content
$q^k_{sL}$ removes that restriction. Its Fokker--Planck part is
\begin{equation}
  \left.\dot q^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
  = 2\Pi^k_{sL}\Bigl[\;
      \underbrace{\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k\,\bar m^k_{sL}\,\Delta_k^{3/8}}_{\text{drift}}
      \;+\;
      \underbrace{\tfrac12\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k\,\Delta_k^{-1/8}}_{\text{spreading}}
    \Bigr],
  \label{eq:q-FP}
\end{equation}
to which every other channel of \cref{eq:master} contributes its own second
moment, weighted by $m^{2}$ where \cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-c} weight it by
$m^{0}$ and $m^{1}$:
\begin{equation}
  \dot q^k_{sL}=\left.\dot q^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
    + J^k_{sL}\bigl(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k\bigr)^{2}
    + \Delta Q
    - m_{\min}^{2}\,\Phi^k_{\min}
    \;\pm\;\Gamma^{q}_{\theta}.
  \label{eq:qdot}
\end{equation}
The spreading term does not vanish when the family stops growing: at
$\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k=0$ the drift term is zero and
$\dot q^k_{sL}=\Pi^k_{sL}\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k\Delta_k^{-1/8}>0$, so the
distribution keeps broadening at fixed mean size and fixed number. There is no
value of the two lower moments that expresses that, which is the argument for
carrying the third.

Closing \cref{eq:qdot} with a log-normal $f_k(m)$ determines its two parameters
from the three carried moments,
\begin{equation}
  \bar m^k_{sL}=\frac{c^k_{sL}}{n^k_{sL}},
  \qquad
  \Delta_k\equiv\frac{q^k_{sL}\,n^k_{sL}}{\bigl(c^k_{sL}\bigr)^{2}}=e^{s_k^{2}}\ \ge 1,
  \qquad
  s_k^{2}=\ln\Delta_k .
  \label{eq:lognormal}
\end{equation}
$\Delta_k\ge1$ is Cauchy--Schwarz on a non-negative measure, so
$\Delta_k<1$ is not a loose tolerance but a defect: it says the three
carried numbers are not the moments of any distribution. Every channel of
\cref{eq:master} respects it automatically except the two that carry neither a
distribution nor a gate, and \cref{app:derivation} states what those two need. The
term most easily got wrong is coalescence, and the reason is worth separating
from the remedy: a coalescing loop does not leave the family, it \emph{merges},
so fewer loops holding the same content are \emph{larger} loops and coalescence
\emph{raises} $q$. That is not a rule but a consequence --- the second moment of
a coagulation operator has a non-negative integrand, \cref{eq:d-coagmoments} ---
and debiting $q$ by the removed number times $\langle m^{2}\rangle$, the
natural-looking reading, inverts the sign of the largest single term in the
equation.

The closure reaches the rest of the model in exactly one place, as
$\Delta_k^{-1/8}$ on the sink prefactor, and it reaches the discrete conversion
of \cref{sec:conversion} as the distribution loop sizes are drawn from.

\subsection{Summary of the equations}
\label{sec:summary-equations}

The system integrated is two blocks with different structure, and the difference
between them is the whole of the numerical method.

Mobile block --- four fields, spatially coupled, no time derivative.
\begin{equation}
  \spvec 0 = \nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(\spmat D_{\mathrm{ten}}\nabla\spvec c_M\bigr)
    + \spvec P_M
    + \tfrac12(\spmat I\otimes\spvec c_M^{T})\spmat R_2\spvec c_M
    + (\spmat E-\spmat D)\spvec c_M + \spvec S_{vL},
  \qquad
  \spvec c_M\big|_{\partial\Omega_D}=\spvec c_M^{\mathrm{eq}},
  \label{eq:block-mobile}
\end{equation}
$4\times N_{\mathrm{node}}$ unknowns, nonlinear and algebraic, coupled across
the mesh by the divergence term alone.

The immobile block is what \cref{eq:master} becomes after the second reduction
of \cref{sec:master} and three successive approximations, and naming them is
the quickest way to say what the equations below are. Restricting
\cref{eq:master} to indices that do not diffuse deletes the transport term
outright, which is what leaves the block pointwise; within one family the
surviving pairwise terms move a loop along the size axis $m$ and do nothing
else, so the family's size density $f_k(m,t)$ obeys a birth--death equation on
the integer $m$. Treating $m$ as continuous and truncating the Kramers--Moyal
expansion at second order turns that into a Fokker--Planck equation, the first
approximation, and it is second order in the jump size measured against the
width of the distribution rather than in the jump size itself. Multiplying by
$m^{j}$ and integrating gives an \emph{exact} hierarchy in the moments;
retaining only $j=0,1,2$, the three fields of \cref{eq:moments-def}, is the
second. That hierarchy does not close, because a perimeter-limited rate carries
$\sqrt m$ and so calls for half-integer moments, and supplying them from the
log-normal \cref{eq:lognormal} is the third --- the only place the closure
reaches the rest of the model, as the factor $\Delta_k^{-1/8}$ carried on
$\Pi^k_{sL}$.

The channels that are not size jumps are not appended to that result; they are
the remaining terms of \cref{eq:master}, and the same operation
$\int m^{j}(\cdot)\,\mathrm dm$ produces them. Which terms those are is fixed by
the partition of the index set and not by a modelling choice: the pairwise
kernel $\mathcal K$ with one mobile partner is what becomes the ladder, and
what is left over is the coagulation $\mathcal C$, whose partners are both
immobile and which therefore moves no loop along $m$; the transfer $\theta$,
which moves a cluster between families at fixed content; and the three removals
$\mathcal D$. Each of the last four is first order in its own moments or
quadratic in them, and its $j$-th moment integral is elementary. The coagulation
gives $-\tfrac12\iint\mathcal C f f$ at $j=0$, \emph{exactly zero} at $j=1$
because a merge conserves content, and $+\iint mm'\,\mathcal C f f$ at $j=2$;
so the statement that coalescence \emph{raises} the second moment, which
\cref{sec:moments} needs and which is the sign most easily inverted, is a
property of \cref{eq:master} and not a rule imposed on the result. A removal
with an $n$-independent rate gives $-\nu\{n,c,q\}$ and a size-gated one the same
thing weighted by the gated fraction of each moment. \Cref{app:derivation}
carries every step out, and \cref{tab:d-terms} is the accounting that no term of
\cref{eq:master} is used twice or dropped.

The result is 27 fields, pointwise, with no spatial coupling. For each family
$(s,k)\in\mathcal F$ at each node independently,
\begin{align}
  \dot n^k_{sL} &= J^k_{sL} - \Delta N - \Phi^k_{\min} \pm \Gamma^{n}_{\theta}
                   - \nu_{gb}\,\Theta^{k}_{0} - \nu_{\mathrm{van}}\,\Xi^{k}_{0},
  \label{eq:block-n}\\
  \dot c^k_{sL} &= \left.\dot c^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{growth}}
                   + G_k - \Delta C - m_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min} \pm \Gamma^{c}_{\theta}
                   - \nu_{gb}\,\Theta^{k}_{1} - \nu_{\mathrm{van}}\,\Xi^{k}_{1},
  \label{eq:block-c}\\
  \dot q^k_{sL} &= \left.\dot q^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
                   + J^k_{sL}(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k)^{2} + \Delta Q
                   - m^{2}_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min}
                   \pm \Gamma^{q}_{\theta} - \nu_{gb}\,\Theta^{k}_{2}
                   - \nu_{\mathrm{van}}\,\Xi^{k}_{2},
  \label{eq:block-q}
\end{align}
whose terms are collected in \cref{tab:block-terms}. Two features of the set
are worth reading off it directly. Each channel appears in all three equations
with the weights $1$, $m$ and $m^{2}$ evaluated at the size where it acts ---
$m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$ for nucleation, $m_{\min}$ for the floor current --- which
is what makes the triple $(n,c,q)$ the moments of one distribution rather than
three independent fields; and the only term that is not a rate times a moment is
the growth flux, which is where the mobile block enters.

Together with the evolving network density $\rho_N$ and the atom-balance
accumulators of \cref{sec:results-conservation}, this is a system of ordinary
differential equations at each node --- $3\times9+1$ fields plus accumulators ---
in which no neighbor's state appears. The mobile species are the only
thing that couples one node to another, and freezing them, as
\cref{sec:split} does, removes the spatial coupling by construction rather
than by approximation.
