\section*{Supplementary Material}

The material collected here supports the main text and is not needed to follow
it. \Cref{app:derivation} carries out in full the two reductions that take the
general master equation \cref{eq:master} to the mobile and immobile blocks of
\cref{sec:summary-equations}, and supplies along the way the definitions of the
two flux sums $\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k$ and $\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k$ and of the
floor current $\Phi^k_{\min}$, which the main text uses without deriving; it
closes with the three rules that keep the moment triple realizable, two of them
consequences of the reduction and the third the one closure the immobile block
needs beyond the log-normal itself. \Cref{app:woo} gives the orientation factors
that build the capture efficiencies and the co-growth criterion that follows
from them; \cref{app:emission} the peripheral emission channel and the loop
binding energy it is computed from; and \cref{app:state} the layout of the
integrated state vector.

\section{From the master equation to the two blocks}
\label{app:derivation}

\Cref{eq:master} is one equation per species label $\alpha$, per defect content
$n$ and per mesh node, and \cref{sec:master} states the two reductions that
bring it to \cref{eq:block-mobile,eq:block-n,eq:block-c,eq:block-q} without
carrying either out. They are carried out here. Both start from one partition of
the index set $(\alpha,n)$: the mobile set $\mathcal M$ of
\cref{eq:mobile-classes}, four species of fixed defect content and finite
diffusivity, and its complement, the immobile clusters, grouped by habit and
character into the nine families $\mathcal F$ of \cref{eq:families} and indexed
within a family by the defect content $m$ alone. That partition is what
separates the two reductions, because the terms of \cref{eq:master} that couple
different indices --- the pairwise kernel $\mathcal K$, the coagulation kernel
$\mathcal C$, the emission rates $\varepsilon$, the transfer $\theta$ and the
absorption $\mathcal D$ at fixed sinks --- each split according to how many of
their partners lie in $\mathcal M$, and every piece lands in exactly one of the
two blocks. \Cref{tab:d-terms} is that accounting, and the rest of this appendix
is its justification.

One claim is worth making before the algebra, because it is what the accounting
is for. Nothing below is added to \cref{eq:master}. The mobile block is that
equation restricted to $\mathcal M$; the immobile block is the same equation
restricted to one family and integrated against $m^{j}$ for $j=0,1,2$. Every
term of \cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-c,eq:block-q} is therefore the moment of a
term of \cref{eq:master}, including the four --- coalescence, the basal
transfers, the grain-boundary removal and the dissolution channel --- that an
earlier presentation of this model appended to the moment equations after the
fact. Appending them is what makes their weights look like conventions; deriving
them is what fixes those weights, and in one case fixes a sign.

Take the mobile block first. Restricting \cref{eq:master} to $\alpha\in\mathcal
M$ and writing $c^{x}$ for the per-atom concentration of species $x$, the
pairwise sum contributes to the $x$ line every reaction whose two partners are
both mobile. Collecting the rate constants \cref{eq:rates} into one matrix
$\spmat R_2^{x}$ per line and stacking the four into $\spmat R_2$, that
contribution is the quadratic form whose $x$-th entry is
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl[\tfrac12(\spmat I\otimes\spvec c_M^{T})\,\spmat R_2\,\spvec c_M\Bigr]_{x}
  =\tfrac12\,\spvec c_M^{T}\,\spmat R_2^{x}\,\spvec c_M ,
  \label{eq:d-quadratic}
\end{equation}
in which the $\tfrac12$ removes the double count that the unrestricted double
sum of \cref{eq:master} performs on each unordered pair $\{m,n\}$ with $m\ne n$.
The like-species channels $i\!+\!i$ and $2i\!+\!2i$ are counted once by that
same sum and so carry their combinatorial factors explicitly instead.

Written out, \cref{eq:d-quadratic} is four symmetric $4\times4$ matrices of the
pairwise constants \cref{eq:rates}, one per mobile line. Order the rows and
columns as $\spvec c_M=[c^{v},c^{i},c^{2i},c^{3i}]^{T}$ and abbreviate
$\beta^{x,y}=\beta^{y,x}$ for the constant of the channel $x+y$, so that
$\beta^{i,v}$ carries the recombination factor $\beta_r$ and every other
$\beta^{x,y}$ is $\omega^{x}+\omega^{y}$ with $\omega^{x}=z_c\nu_x
\exp(-E^{m}_x/k_BT)$ the jump rate of species $x$ --- the geometric prefactor
$A^{x,y}a^{2}/z_c\Omega$ times the capture radius $(r^{x}+r^{y})$ of
\cref{eq:rates} being absorbed into $A^{x,y}$, which is unity on every channel
but $i\!+\!v$. Then
\begin{equation}
\small
  \spmat R_2^{v}=-\begin{bmatrix}
      0            & \beta^{i,v}  & \beta^{2i,v} & \beta^{3i,v}\\
      \beta^{i,v}  & 0            & 0            & 0\\
      \beta^{2i,v} & 0            & 0            & 0\\
      \beta^{3i,v} & 0            & 0            & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \spmat R_2^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}
      0             & -\beta^{i,v}   & +\beta^{2i,v} & 0\\
      -\beta^{i,v}  & -2\beta^{i,i}  & -\beta^{i,2i} & -\beta^{i,3i}\\
      +\beta^{2i,v} & -\beta^{i,2i}  & 0             & 0\\
      0             & -\beta^{i,3i}  & 0             & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:d-R2vi}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\small
  \spmat R_2^{2i}=\begin{bmatrix}
      0             & 0             & -\beta^{2i,v}   & +\beta^{3i,v}\\
      0             & +\beta^{i,i}  & -\beta^{i,2i}   & 0\\
      -\beta^{2i,v} & -\beta^{i,2i} & -4\beta^{2i,2i} & -\beta^{2i,3i}\\
      +\beta^{3i,v} & 0             & -\beta^{2i,3i}  & 0
    \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \spmat R_2^{3i}=\begin{bmatrix}
      0             & 0             & 0              & -\beta^{3i,v}\\
      0             & 0             & +\beta^{i,2i}  & -\beta^{i,3i}\\
      0             & +\beta^{i,2i} & 0              & -\beta^{2i,3i}\\
      -\beta^{3i,v} & -\beta^{i,3i} & -\beta^{2i,3i} & 0
    \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:d-R2cluster}
\end{equation}

Each matrix is read one entry at a time, and the entry is a stoichiometric
coefficient rather than a rate: the $(x,y)$ position of $\spmat R_2^{\alpha}$ is
$\beta^{x,y}$ times the net number of $\alpha$ produced by one $x\!+\!y$ event,
negative when $\alpha$ is one of the two reactants. The four off-diagonal
patterns are the four channels that keep a mobile product. Recombination
$i\!+\!v\to\varnothing$ appears as $-\beta^{i,v}$ in $\spmat R_2^{v}$ and in
$\spmat R_2^{i}$ and nowhere else. Its two cluster analogues do keep a product:
$2i\!+\!v\to i$ debits $c^{v}$ and $c^{2i}$ and credits $c^{i}$, which is the
$+\beta^{2i,v}$ sitting in $\spmat R_2^{i}$, and $3i\!+\!v\to2i$ likewise puts
$+\beta^{3i,v}$ into $\spmat R_2^{2i}$. Clustering $i\!+\!2i\to3i$ is the
$-\beta^{i,2i}$, $-\beta^{i,2i}$, $+\beta^{i,2i}$ column of entries running down
the three interstitial matrices.

The diagonals are where the combinatorial factors of the preceding paragraph
sit, and they are the reason the four matrices cannot be read off the reaction
list by inspection. For $i\!+\!i\to2i$ the event rate is
$\tfrac12\beta^{i,i}(c^{i})^{2}$, each event consumes \emph{two} monomers and
makes one dimer, so the $(i,i)$ entry is $-2\beta^{i,i}$ in $\spmat R_2^{i}$ and
$+\beta^{i,i}$ in $\spmat R_2^{2i}$: a factor of two apart, and of opposite
sign, from one channel. The $(2i,2i)$ entry is $-4\beta^{2i,2i}$ for the same
reason once, and once again because $2i\!+\!2i$ leaves the mobile set entirely
--- it nucleates a loop, so there is no positive counterpart anywhere.

That last remark is the structural statement the matrices make. Five of the
eight channels stay inside $\mathcal M$ and conserve defect content exactly:
with $\spvec m_M=[-1,+1,+2,+3]^{T}$ the signed content of \cref{eq:mobile-classes},
the identity $\sum_{\alpha}m^{\alpha}\bigl[\spmat R_2^{\alpha}\bigr]_{xy}=0$
holds at the $(i,v)$, $(2i,v)$, $(3i,v)$, $(i,2i)$ and $(i,i)$ positions --- the
$i\!+\!2i$ column, for instance, gives $1(-\beta)+2(-\beta)+3(+\beta)=0$. It
fails at the remaining three, $(i,3i)$, $(2i,2i)$ and $(2i,3i)$, and that failure
is not an error: those are the clustering channels, they have no mobile product,
and the content they remove from $\spvec c_M$ is exactly what \cref{tab:d-terms}
sends to the nucleation current $J^{a_k}_{iL}$ of the immobile block --- $4$
defects per $i\!+\!3i$ event, $2$ per $2i$ lost to $2i\!+\!2i$, and $5$ per
$2i\!+\!3i$. The mobile block does not conserve defects on its own; the two
blocks together do, and the three non-vanishing entries of
$\sum_{\alpha}m^{\alpha}\spmat R_2^{\alpha}$ are the audit of that transfer.

Reactions with one mobile and one immobile partner are, at frozen immobile
state, linear in $\spvec c_M$, and so are the fixed-sink terms $\mathcal
D^\alpha_nC^\alpha_n$. Together they give $-\spmat D\spvec c_M$ of
\cref{eq:KM}: the network through the sink concentrations \cref{eq:sinkconc},
the loops through the sink-strength block \cref{eq:KL}. This is the only place
the immobile state enters the mobile block, and \cref{eq:KL} builds it from the
same efficiencies $Z_{sk,m}$ that drive growth in \cref{eq:growth}. What a
family absorbs is therefore what the mobile species lose, family by family and
species by species, which is the operational content of the word
self-consistent and the reason \cref{tab:d-terms} has no row appearing twice.

The emission terms divide by emitter. A mobile cluster emitting a point defect
moves within $\mathcal M$ and gives the linear term $\spmat E\spvec c_M$, with
$\alpha^{m,n}=\beta^{m,n}\exp(-E^{b}_{m,n}/k_BT)$; a loop emitting a vacancy
across its own perimeter puts that vacancy into the pool and gives the source
$\spvec S_{vL}$ of \cref{app:emission}. What is left is the transport term and
the time derivative. Since $\bm D^\alpha_n$ vanishes on every immobile index,
$-\nabla\!\cdot\!\bm J^\alpha_n$ survives on $\mathcal M$ alone and becomes
$\nabla\!\cdot\!(\spmat D_{\mathrm{ten}}\nabla\spvec c_M)$; and the time
derivative is dropped by the scale separation of \cref{sec:why-split}, with
$\tau_M\sim(k^{2}\overline D)^{-1}$ of order microseconds against immobile
fields evolving over $10^{7}\,$s. Assembling the pieces gives
\cref{eq:block-mobile}, closed by \cref{eq:bc}. Note what that limit does and
does not say: the mobile concentrations are not constant in time, since they
track the immobile state through $\spmat D$ at every instant, but they carry no
memory of it, which is what permits the fast solve to be called at intervals
rather than at every step.

The second reduction applies to the complement of $\mathcal M$, where transport
is absent outright and the remaining question is what becomes of the size index.
Fix a family $(s,k)\in\mathcal F$ and let $f_k(m,t)\,\mathrm dm$ be the number of
its loops per host atom holding between $m$ and $m+\mathrm dm$ defects, so that
the fields the block carries are its first three moments
\cref{eq:moments-def}. The pairwise terms with exactly one mobile partner move a
loop along $m$ and nowhere else: absorbing a mobile species $x$ carries the loop
by $\mu_x=|m^{x}|\in\{1,2,3\}$, the defect content of the arriving cluster,
upward when the polarity of $x$ matches the family's character and downward when
it does not, and a thermal emission is the same move with $\mu=1$. Restricted to
one family, \cref{eq:master} is therefore a birth--death process on the integer
$m$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \frac{\partial f_k}{\partial t}=\sum_{x}\Bigl[
    &W^{+}_{x}(m-\mu_x)\,f_k(m-\mu_x)
    +W^{-}_{x}(m+\mu_x)\,f_k(m+\mu_x)\\
    &-\bigl(W^{+}_{x}(m)+W^{-}_{x}(m)\bigr)f_k(m)\Bigr]
    +J^k_{sL}\,\delta_{m,m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k},
\end{split}
  \label{eq:d-birthdeath}
\end{equation}
with $W^{+}_{x}$ and $W^{-}_{x}$ the rates of the matching-polarity and the
opposite-polarity step. Those rates are not free. A loop captures on its
perimeter, so $W^{\pm}_{x}\propto R^k_{sL}=\lambda_k\sqrt m$ by
\cref{eq:radius}, and the constant of proportionality is the same efficiency and
the same mobility that \cref{eq:growth} already carries,
\begin{equation}
  W^{\pm}_{x}(m)=\pi_k\sqrt m\;\overline D^{x}\,Z_{sk,x}\,c^{x},
  \qquad
  W^{\mathrm{emit}}(m)=\pi_k\sqrt m\;\overline D^{v}\,Z_{sk,v}\,
    c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k(m),
  \qquad
  \pi_k=\frac{\lambda_k\,\xi_k}{\ell},
  \label{eq:d-Wrates}
\end{equation}
so that the geometric prefactor $\Pi^k_{sL}=\pi_k\sqrt{n^k_{sL}c^k_{sL}}$ of
\cref{eq:loopprefactor} is $\pi_kn^k_{sL}\langle m^{1/2}\rangle_k$ evaluated on a
monodisperse family. No parameter enters the size distribution that the growth
law does not already contain.

$W^{\mathrm{emit}}$ is peripheral emission, \cref{app:emission}, and it belongs
in \cref{eq:d-birthdeath} as a fourth process rather than as a correction
applied afterwards. It has the same prefactor and the same efficiency as vacancy
capture --- it is the same event run backwards --- and it steps the loop by
$\mu_v=1$ in the direction opposite to that capture, against the loop's own
equilibrium concentration $c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k$ of \cref{eq:Eb} rather than
against the field. Carrying it here rather than bolting it on is what makes the
opposite signs it acquires in the two flux sums below a consequence instead of a
convention. Thermal \emph{interstitial} emission has no counterpart term: it is
$\sim10^{-8}$ of this at $873\,$K and is dropped.

Treating $m$ as continuous and expanding the shifted terms of
\cref{eq:d-birthdeath} about $m$, the zeroth-order terms cancel the loss term
identically --- which is the statement that a size jump neither creates nor
destroys a loop --- and what survives is the Kramers--Moyal series
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial f_k}{\partial t}
  =\sum_{p\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!}\,
    \frac{\partial^{p}}{\partial m^{p}}\bigl[a_p(m)f_k\bigr]
   +J^k_{sL}\,\delta\!\left(m-m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k\right),
  \qquad
  a_p=\sum_{x}\mu_x^{\,p}\bigl[W^{+}_{x}+(-1)^{p}W^{-}_{x}\bigr].
  \label{eq:d-KM}
\end{equation}
Truncating \cref{eq:d-KM} at $p=2$ is the Fokker--Planck equation for the size
density,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial f_k}{\partial t}
  =-\frac{\partial}{\partial m}\bigl[F_kf_k\bigr]
   +\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial m^{2}}\bigl[D_kf_k\bigr]
   +J^k_{sL}\,\delta\!\left(m-m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k\right),
  \label{eq:d-FP}
\end{equation}
whose drift is the difference of the two directions and whose diffusion is half
their sum,
\begin{equation}
  F_k=a_1=\pi_k\sqrt m\;\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k,
  \qquad
  D_k=\tfrac12a_2=\tfrac12\,\pi_k\sqrt m\;\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k,
  \label{eq:d-FPcoeffs}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k
   =\eta_s\!\!\sum_{x\in\mathcal M}\!\!
      \varsigma_s(x)\,\mu_x\,\overline D^{x}Z_{sk,x}
      \bigl(c^{x}-c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k\delta_{x,v}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k
   =\sum_{x\in\mathcal M}\!\mu_x^{2}\,\overline D^{x}Z_{sk,x}
      \bigl(c^{x}+c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k\delta_{x,v}\bigr).
  \label{eq:d-Sigmas}
\end{equation}
\Cref{eq:d-Sigmas} is where the two flux sums the main text uses are defined,
and the two of them differ in \emph{three} places, not one. The obvious one is
the sign: $\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k$ carries $\varsigma_s(x)$ and
$\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k$ does not, because a defect of either polarity moves
the loop along $m$ and a move in either direction spreads the distribution. The
second is the power of the step. $\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k$ is not the drift sum
with its signs made positive: the second Kramers--Moyal coefficient weights each
channel by $\mu_x^{2}$, one power more than the drift, because variance
accumulates as the square of the step. For the monomer channels the two
weightings coincide and the distinction is invisible, and for $2i$ and $3i$ it is
not --- those two carry $3.1\%$ of the interstitial arrival in
$\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k$ and between $6\%$ and $9\%$ of $\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k$,
so using the drift weighting for both under-estimates the spreading by $3$ to
$6\%$.

The third is the one most easily missed, and it is the reason
$c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k$ of \cref{eq:Eb} appears in both with \emph{opposite}
signs, both of them inherited from $W^{\mathrm{emit}}$ through $a_1$ and $a_2$
of \cref{eq:d-KM}. The drift subtracts it, $a_1$ differencing the two
directions, so the loop is driven not by $c^{v}$ but by its departure from the
loop's own equilibrium and the channel vanishes identically at
$c^{v}=c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k$ --- detailed balance by construction. That is the
same statement as \cref{eq:Semit} entering \cref{eq:block-c} with the opposite
sign, written as one substitution instead of two terms, and with it
$\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k$ is exactly the signed sum multiplying $\Pi^k_{sL}$ in
\cref{eq:growth} together with that emission channel: the drift of
\cref{eq:d-FP} \emph{is} the growth law and carries no independent content. The
spreading takes the \emph{sum} $c^{v}+c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k$, because absorption
and emission are independent events that each add variance; they do not cancel.
A family sitting exactly at its own equilibrium therefore has
$\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k=0$ and $\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k>0$: it does not grow,
and it keeps broadening. That is the same conclusion \cref{eq:q-FP} reaches
from the other side, and it is the strongest argument in the formulation for
carrying $q$ at all. Only the vacancy species carries the substitution, thermal
interstitial emission being $\sim10^{-8}$ of it (\cref{app:emission}); and both
reduce to the plain $c^{x}$ when the emission channel is switched off, which is
what makes that switch a clean regression.

The truncation is second order in a parameter that is not the jump size but the
jump size measured against the width of the distribution: successive terms of
\cref{eq:d-KM} carry one more power of $\mu_x\partial_m$, so the ratio of term
$p+1$ to term $p$ is of order $\mu_x/\sigma_m$ with
$\sigma_m^{2}=(\bar m^k_{sL})^{2}(\Delta_k-1)$, which is where the dispersion
enters this appendix and is worth restating. $\Delta_k$ is the third carried
moment expressed as a shape: by \cref{eq:lognormal} it is
$\Delta_k=q^k_{sL}n^k_{sL}/(c^k_{sL})^{2}=\langle m^{2}\rangle_k/\langle
m\rangle_k^{2}$, the second moment of the family's size distribution divided by
the square of its first, so it is a pure number, it is $1$ exactly when every
loop in the family has the same size, and it exceeds $1$ by the relative
variance, $\Delta_k-1=\mathrm{var}(m)/\bar m^{2}$. It is dimensionless because
$q$, $n$ and $c$ enter it in the combination that cancels $m$; it carries no
information about how big the loops are, only about how unequal they are; and
under the log-normal closure it is the shape parameter outright,
$\Delta_k=e^{s_k^{2}}$, so $s_k=\sqrt{\ln\Delta_k}$ is the standard
deviation of $\ln m$. Three things make that small
here, and only the first is about the absorption step alone. The mobile set
closes at $3i$, so $\mu_x\le3$ against family means of $10^{2}$ to $10^{5}$
defects, a ratio whose worst value anywhere on the reference march is
$1.6\times10^{-2}$ --- reached by the prismatic interstitial family at
$10^{-4}\,$dpa, where it has barely left its nucleus of $300$. The rates go as
$\sqrt m$ by \cref{eq:d-Wrates}, so their relative change over one event is
$\mu_x/2m$, the same smallness again and half of it; a rate varying
exponentially in $m$ would not permit the truncation however small the step. And
the distribution is broad in units of the step, which is the condition the
expansion parameter actually names: a loop that has made $N_{\mathrm{ex}}$
exchange events has $\sigma_m=\mu\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{ex}}}$ and hence
$\mu/\sigma_m=N_{\mathrm{ex}}^{-1/2}$, with
$N_{\mathrm{ex}}=2\mu^{-2}(D_k/F_k)\Delta m$ --- so the same near-cancellation
that makes the second moment necessary, $D_k/F_k$ between $7$ and $44$, also
multiplies the number of exchanges a loop has made for a given net growth. The
basal family reaches $N_{\mathrm{ex}}\simeq1.5\times10^{7}$ and the prismatic one
$3.5\times10^{4}$, giving $\mu/\sigma_m$ of $2.6\times10^{-4}$ and
$5\times10^{-3}$.

Two places where the expansion is worst are worth naming, because at both of
them the model works outside \cref{eq:d-FP} rather than relying on it. A loop
that has just nucleated has made no exchanges at all, its distribution is a delta
function, and no gradient expansion describes it; that defect is real, is
confined to a region of width $\sim\mu_x$ about $m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$, which is a
percent of the nucleus, and is cured by the first few exchange events. The floor
$m_{\min}$ is the other, where $f_k$ is cut off and its gradient is largest, and
there the model carries an explicit current, derived next, instead of a
Fokker--Planck flux.

Multiplying \cref{eq:d-FP} by $m^{j}$ and integrating over $[m_{\min},\infty)$
turns it into a hierarchy in the moments. Integrating the drift term once by
parts and the diffusion term twice, with $f_k$ and its derivative vanishing as
$m\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\!\int_{m_{\min}}^{\infty}\!\!m^{j}f_k\,\mathrm dm
  =j\!\int\!m^{j-1}F_kf_k\,\mathrm dm
   +j(j-1)\!\int\!m^{j-2}D_kf_k\,\mathrm dm
   +J^k_{sL}\bigl(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k\bigr)^{j}
   -m_{\min}^{\,j}\,\Phi^k_{\min},
  \label{eq:d-hierarchy}
\end{equation}
the last term being the boundary contribution at the floor, with
\begin{equation}
  \Phi^k_{\min}
   =\Bigl[\,D_k\,\partial_mf_k+\bigl(\partial_mD_k-F_k\bigr)f_k\,\Bigr]_{m=m_{\min}},
  \qquad
  f_k(m_{\min})=\frac{n^k_{sL}}{m_{\min}s_k\sqrt{2\pi}}
    \exp\!\left[-\frac{(\ln m_{\min}-\mu_k)^{2}}{2s_k^{2}}\right],
  \label{eq:d-floorcurrent}
\end{equation}
the Fokker--Planck flux through the floor, evaluated on \cref{eq:lognormal} and
therefore analytic in the three carried moments. \Cref{eq:d-floorcurrent} is the
floor current that \cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-q} write as $\Phi^k_{\min}$. Two
properties of it should be recorded. Because a delta function has no tail,
$\Phi^k_{\min}\equiv0$ at $\Delta_k=1$: in a two-moment model the dissolution
rate is not small but identically zero, which is why such a model under a pure
anneal gives a falling mean size at exactly constant density, where a measurement
reports the density falling and the surviving mean rising. And the exact
integration by parts leaves one further boundary term,
$j\,m_{\min}^{\,j-1}D_kf_k$ evaluated at $m=m_{\min}$, which
\cref{eq:d-hierarchy} drops. Dropping it is the statement that loops leaving
through the floor are booked at exactly $m_{\min}$ defects each, so that they
debit $\Phi^k_{\min}$, $m_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min}$ and $m_{\min}^{2}\Phi^k_{\min}$
from the three moments and release $m_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min}$ vacancies to the
pool. That is exactly conservative, which retaining the term would not be.

The three cases are then taken in turn. At $j=0$ both integrals vanish, $j$ and
$j(j-1)$ being zero together: drift and diffusion move loops along the size axis
and can neither create nor destroy one, so the density changes only by
nucleation and by the floor current. At $j=1$ the diffusion integral still
vanishes --- spreading a distribution at fixed mean moves no defects --- and the
drift integral is, with $F_k$ from \cref{eq:d-FPcoeffs},
\begin{equation}
  \int_{m_{\min}}^{\infty}\!\!F_kf_k\,\mathrm dm
   =\pi_k\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k\,n^k_{sL}\bigl\langle m^{1/2}\bigr\rangle_k
   =\Pi^k_{sL}\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k\,\Delta_k^{-1/8},
  \label{eq:d-j1}
\end{equation}
which is the growth law \cref{eq:growth}, reduced by the one factor
$\Delta_k^{-1/8}$ that a spread family contributes because $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is
concave and a spread family therefore presents less perimeter than a
monodisperse one of the same density and content. At $j=2$ both integrals
survive,
\begin{equation}
  2\!\int\!mF_kf_k\,\mathrm dm
   =2\,\Pi^k_{sL}\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k\,\bar m^k_{sL}\,\Delta_k^{3/8},
  \qquad
  2\!\int\!D_kf_k\,\mathrm dm
   =\Pi^k_{sL}\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k\,\Delta_k^{-1/8},
  \label{eq:d-j2}
\end{equation}
and their sum is \cref{eq:q-FP} term for term.

Each of \cref{eq:d-j1,eq:d-j2} needs a half-integer moment of $f_k$, and
supplying those is the whole work of the closure. With $f_k$ log-normal in $m$
and its two parameters fixed by the three carried moments through
\cref{eq:lognormal},
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\langle m^{\,j}\bigr\rangle_k
   =\bigl(\bar m^k_{sL}\bigr)^{j}\,\Delta_k^{\,j(j-1)/2}
  \qquad\text{for every real }j,
  \label{eq:d-closure}
\end{equation}
which returns $\bar m^{1/2}\Delta_k^{-1/8}$ at $j=\tfrac12$ and
$\bar m^{3/2}\Delta_k^{3/8}$ at $j=\tfrac32$ --- the two exponents that appear in
\cref{eq:q-FP} and the only ones the model needs. A Gaussian closure gives
neither in closed form and admits $m<0$, and that, rather than positivity alone,
is why the closure is log-normal. At $\Delta_k=1$, \cref{eq:d-closure} returns
$\bar m^{j}$ for every $j$, and everything in this appendix collapses onto the
two-moment model exactly, term by term and without any rate being set to zero,
which is the regression test on the third moment.

Every power of $\Delta_k$ in \cref{eq:d-j1,eq:d-j2} arrives the same way, and
none of them is an additional modelling assumption. A loop captures on its
perimeter, so by \cref{eq:radius,eq:d-Wrates} both Fokker--Planck coefficients
carry $\sqrt m$, and each of the three integrals is therefore a \emph{fractional}
moment of $f_k$: $\langle m^{1/2}\rangle_k$ from the drift at $j=1$,
$\langle m^{3/2}\rangle_k$ from the drift at $j=2$, and
$\langle m^{1/2}\rangle_k$ again from the diffusion at $j=2$, $D_k$ carrying one
power of $m$ less than $mF_k$. The prefactor is the same integral evaluated as
though every loop in the family had the mean size,
$\Pi^k_{sL}=\pi_k\sqrt{n^k_{sL}c^k_{sL}}=\pi_kn^k_{sL}(\bar m^k_{sL})^{1/2}$, so
writing an integral as $\Pi^k_{sL}$ times something is precisely dividing the
true moment by its monodisperse value, and by \cref{eq:d-closure} that ratio is
a power of $\Delta_k$ and of nothing else:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\langle m^{\,j}\rangle_k}{(\bar m^k_{sL})^{\,j}}=\Delta_k^{\,j(j-1)/2},
  \qquad
  j=\tfrac12\ \Rightarrow\ \Delta_k^{-1/8},
  \qquad
  j=\tfrac32\ \Rightarrow\ \Delta_k^{+3/8}.
  \label{eq:d-jensen}
\end{equation}
That is the whole origin of the two exponents. The $-1/8$ of \cref{eq:d-j1} and
of the second half of \cref{eq:d-j2} is $j(j-1)/2$ at $j=\tfrac12$; the $+3/8$ of
the first half of \cref{eq:d-j2} is the same expression at $j=\tfrac32$.

The sign of the exponent is the sign of the curvature, which is what makes the
two factors pull in opposite directions. $j(j-1)/2$ is negative for $0<j<1$ and
positive for $j>1$, so by Jensen's inequality the concave $m^{1/2}$ gives a
family that is short of its monodisperse perimeter and the convex $m^{3/2}$ one
that is over it: at fixed density and content, spreading a family reduces what it
absorbs and increases how fast its second moment grows. The magnitudes are not
equal --- $3/8$ against $1/8$ --- so broadening feeds back on $q$ three times as
strongly, in the exponent, as it withdraws from $c$. A family whose size
distribution has a relative variance of one, $\Delta_k=2$, therefore presents
$\Delta_k^{-1/8}=0.92$ of the perimeter a monodisperse family of the same $n$ and
$c$ would, and broadens at $\Delta_k^{+3/8}=1.30$ of the monodisperse rate. Both
factors are $1$ at $\Delta_k=1$, which is the two-moment model.

Collecting the three cases gives the Fokker--Planck part of the block. Writing
\cref{eq:d-hierarchy} at $j=0,1,2$ and substituting
\cref{eq:d-j1,eq:d-j2,eq:d-closure},
\begin{align}
  \left.\dot n^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
    &= J^k_{sL} - \Phi^k_{\min},
  \label{eq:d-assemble-n}\\
  \left.\dot c^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
    &= \underbrace{\Pi^k_{sL}\,\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k\,\Delta_k^{-1/8}}
       _{=\;\left.\dot c^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{growth}}}
     + \underbrace{J^k_{sL}\,m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k}_{=\;G_k}
     - m_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min},
  \label{eq:d-assemble-c}\\
  \left.\dot q^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
    &= \underbrace{2\Pi^k_{sL}\Bigl[\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k\bar m^k_{sL}
         \Delta_k^{3/8}+\tfrac12\Sigma^{\mathrm{gro}}_k\Delta_k^{-1/8}\Bigr]}
       _{\text{\cref{eq:q-FP}}}
     + J^k_{sL}\bigl(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k\bigr)^{2}
     - m_{\min}^{2}\Phi^k_{\min}.
  \label{eq:d-assemble-q}
\end{align}
Three identifications close the gap between these and
\cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-c,eq:block-q}, and each is a definition rather than a
step. The drift integral at $j=1$ \emph{is} the growth law: \cref{eq:d-j1}
returns $\Pi^k_{sL}\Sigma^{\mathrm{net}}_k$ reduced by $\Delta_k^{-1/8}$, and
that factor is carried on $\Pi^k_{sL}$ itself, so the growth term of
\cref{eq:block-c} and the drift of \cref{eq:d-FP} are one quantity written
twice. The cascade content rate is the nucleation current weighted by the size
it deposits at, $G_k=J^k_{sL}m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$, which is why
\cref{eq:block-c} needs no separate nucleation symbol where
\cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-q} carry $J^k_{sL}$ and
$J^k_{sL}(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k)^{2}$. And the $j=2$ pair is \cref{eq:q-FP} term
for term, the first half the drift and the second the spreading.

\Cref{eq:d-assemble-n,eq:d-assemble-c,eq:d-assemble-q} are everything
\cref{eq:d-FP} can produce, and they are not yet the block. What is missing is
not missing physics: it is the four terms of \cref{eq:master} that the
restriction to one family leaves standing but that do not move a loop along
$m$, and so never entered \cref{eq:d-birthdeath}. They are the coagulation
$\mathcal C$, the transfer $\theta$, and the removals $\mathcal D_{N}$,
$\mathcal D_{gb}$ and $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{van}}$. Each is taken in turn by the
same operation as before --- multiply by $m^{j}$, integrate --- and the weights
that \cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-c,eq:block-q} carry come out of the integral
rather than being assigned to it.

Coalescence has both partners immobile, which is what makes it a coagulation
rather than a transition on the size ladder. Restricting the $\mathcal C$ terms
of \cref{eq:master} to one family and writing $M_j=\int m^{j}f_k\,\mathrm dm$,
the gain and loss combine under the substitution $m\to m+m'$ into a single
symmetric double integral,
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\mathrm dM_j}{\mathrm dt}\right|_{\mathcal C}
  =\tfrac12\!\iint\!\mathcal C_k(m,m')\,f_k(m)f_k(m')\,
    \bigl[(m+m')^{j}-m^{j}-m'^{j}\bigr]\,\mathrm dm\,\mathrm dm' ,
  \label{eq:d-coagmoments}
\end{equation}
whose bracket is $-1$ at $j=0$, $0$ at $j=1$ and $2mm'$ at $j=2$. The three
statements the model makes about coalescence are therefore all properties of
\cref{eq:master}: one loop is lost per event; content is conserved
\emph{identically}, not approximately; and the second moment \emph{rises}, the
integrand at $j=2$ being non-negative. That last is the sign most easily
inverted, and taking it from the integral rather than from a rule about a debit
is what makes it a theorem about a coagulation operator; the consequences of
getting it wrong are collected at the end of this appendix.

What \cref{eq:d-coagmoments} does not do on its own is close. The model carries
no kernel $\mathcal C_k$; it carries the mean-field Avrami rate of
\cref{eq:coal}, which is the $j=0$ integral,
$\Delta N_{LL}=\tfrac12\iint\mathcal C_kf_kf_k$. The $j=1$ result needs nothing
further. The $j=2$ result needs $\iint mm'\mathcal C_kf_kf_k$, which asks which
\emph{pairs} coalesce and is not determined by $\Delta N_{LL}$ alone. Two
closures bracket it. A size-independent kernel gives
$\iint mm'\mathcal C_kf_kf_k=\mathcal C_k(c^k_{sL})^{2}$ against
$\Delta N_{LL}=\tfrac12\mathcal C_k(n^k_{sL})^{2}$, hence
$2\bar m^{2}\Delta N_{LL}$; the shape-preserving closure stated below as rule
3, which holds $\Delta_k$ fixed through the merge and is the one implemented, gives
$\Delta_k\bar m^{2}\Delta N_{LL}$. Both are positive, and the choice between
them moves a magnitude and never the sign.

What the choice does move is the \emph{shape}, and the two closures differ there
in kind rather than in degree. Under the implemented one the merge is exactly
shape-neutral. With $\mathrm dc^k_{sL}=0$ on that channel,
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\dot\Delta_k}{\Delta_k}\right|_{LL}
   =\frac{\dot q^k_{sL}}{q^k_{sL}}+\frac{\dot n^k_{sL}}{n^k_{sL}}
    -\frac{2\dot c^k_{sL}}{c^k_{sL}}
   =\frac{\Delta_k\bar m^{2}\Delta N_{LL}}{\Delta_k\bar m^{2}n^k_{sL}}
    -\frac{\Delta N_{LL}}{n^k_{sL}}=0 ,
  \label{eq:d-shapeneutral}
\end{equation}
and the loop--network channel, being a removal in proportion, leaves $\Delta_k$
alone for the same reason. So coalescence as this model carries it is a pure
translation of the family to larger sizes: the density falls, the content is
untouched, the mean $\bar m^k_{sL}=c^k_{sL}/n^k_{sL}$ rises, and the width in
units of the mean does not change. That is also why $\Delta Q$ is not an
optional refinement. Delete it and \cref{eq:d-shapeneutral} returns
$-\Delta N_{LL}/n^k_{sL}$ instead of zero, so $\Delta_k$ falls at the rate at
which coalescence removes loops --- $98\%$ of the loop-number loss --- and
reaches the unrealizable region in a few dpa.

A true coagulation is not shape-neutral, and the same combination evaluated on
the size-independent kernel says where it goes instead:
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\dot\Delta_k}{\Delta_k}\right|_{LL}
   =\frac{\Delta N_{LL}}{n^k_{sL}}\left(\frac{2}{\Delta_k}-1\right),
  \label{eq:d-deltaflow}
\end{equation}
which vanishes only at $\Delta_k=2$ and carries the sign of $2-\Delta_k$
everywhere else. The three moment equations integrate in closed form on that
kernel --- $n(t)=n_0/(1+\tfrac12\mathcal C_kn_0t)$, $c$ constant,
$q(t)=q_0+\mathcal C_kc^{2}t$ --- and give
$\Delta_k(t)\to2$ from either side whatever $\Delta_k(0)$ is. That number is
not arithmetic: the self-similar solution of constant-kernel coagulation is
exponential in $m$, and an exponential has $\langle m^{2}\rangle=2\langle
m\rangle^{2}$. $\Delta_k=2$ is therefore the coagulation's own attractor, and
the two closures agree exactly there because that is the one shape a merge
leaves alone.

Naming the approximation that way is more useful than a bound on it. The
shape-preserving closure holds $\Delta_k$ where it finds it, where the kernel
would relax it toward $2$; on the two interior families of \cref{sec:results} it
therefore under-broadens the basal one, at $\Delta_k=1.59$, and under-narrows
the prismatic interstitial one, at $2.71$. Both errors are in the width alone,
neither touches $n$ or $c$ directly, and both reach the rest of the model only
through $\Delta_k^{-1/8}$ on $\Pi^k_{sL}$ and through the gates. Taking the
distance to the fixed point as the largest error the closure can make in
$\Delta_k$ bounds the first of those at $(2/\Delta_k)^{-1/8}$, which is $0.972$
on the basal family and $1.039$ on the prismatic one --- under $4\%$ either way,
because a one-eighth power is a poor lever. That is why this is the last
approximation in the appendix worth worrying about rather than the first, and it
is a bound on the \emph{prefactor}: the gates carry $\Delta_k$ through
$s_k=\sqrt{\ln\Delta_k}$ and are not bounded by the same argument.

The loop--network channel is not a coagulation at all, and separating it from
the like-loop channel is what makes its weights exact. The network is a fixed
sink, so it enters \cref{eq:master} as $\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{N}(n)$, and over one
family the Avrami factor of \cref{eq:coal} is built from the family mean and so
carries no dependence on $m$. A first-order removal at an $m$-independent rate
$\nu$ takes every moment down in proportion,
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\mathrm dM_j}{\mathrm dt}\right|_{\mathcal D_N}=-\nu M_j
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \bigl(-\Delta N_{LN},\;-\Delta C,\;-\Delta_k\bar m^{2}\Delta N_{LN}\bigr)
  \label{eq:d-LN}
\end{equation}
on $(n,c,q)$, using $\Delta C=\nu c^k_{sL}=\bar m\,\Delta N_{LN}$ and
$q^k_{sL}=\Delta_k\bar m^{2}n^k_{sL}$. No closure is involved: the $q$ debit is
$-\nu q$ and nothing else. The implementation carries the two channels as one
shape-preserving expression,
$\Delta_k(\bar m^{2}\Delta N-2\bar m\,\Delta C)$ with
$\Delta N=\Delta N_{LL}+\Delta N_{LN}$, and splitting it recovers exactly the two
results above --- $+\Delta_k\bar m^{2}\Delta N_{LL}$ from the merge and
$-\Delta_k\bar m^{2}\Delta N_{LN}$ from the removal. That the second is exact and
only the first rests on a closure is the useful thing to know about that
expression, and it is not visible until the two channels are derived apart.

The basal transfers \cref{eq:basal-chain} are the $\theta$ term of
\cref{eq:master}, which moves a cluster between families at fixed defect
content. Its $m^{j}$ integral over the donor's gated distribution is
$\Gamma^{n}_{\theta},\Gamma^{c}_{\theta},\Gamma^{q}_{\theta}$, and because the
same term appears in the donor's equation with a minus and the receiver's with a
plus, the $j=1$ pair cancels identically: content is conserved across the chain
by the structure of the term, not by a constraint imposed on its rates. Only the
number changes, the two states storing different content per unit area.

The last two are the removals $\mathcal D_{gb}$ and $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{van}}$,
and they are one integral read in opposite senses because they are the same
kind of term gated on opposite sides. With
$\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{gb}(m)=\nu_{gb}\mathbf 1[m\ge m_{gb}]$ from
\cref{eq:mgb},
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\mathrm dM_j}{\mathrm dt}\right|_{gb}
   =-\nu_{gb}\!\!\int_{m_{gb}}^{\infty}\!\!m^{j}f_k\,\mathrm dm
   =-\nu_{gb}\,\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_{gb})\,M_j
   =-\nu_{gb}\,\Theta^{k}_{j},
  \label{eq:d-gb}
\end{equation}
by the definition \cref{eq:Phij} of $\Phi^{(j)}_k$ as the fraction of the
$j$-th moment above a threshold, and those defects leave the crystal.
$\mathcal D^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{van}}(m)=\nu_{\mathrm{van}}\mathbf 1[m\le
m_{\mathrm{van}}]$ gives the complement,
$-\nu_{\mathrm{van}}[1-\Phi^{(j)}_k(m_{\mathrm{van}})]M_j
 =-\nu_{\mathrm{van}}\Xi^{k}_{j}$ of \cref{eq:vanish}, and those defects return
to the mobile pool through $\spvec S_{vL}$. Both are realizable without a rule:
what remains after removing the mass of a distribution on one side of a
threshold is a genuine sub-measure, so $\Delta_k\ge1$ survives by construction.
That is the same argument as \cref{eq:d-coagmoments} at $j=2$ made on the other
side --- these two channels cannot break realizability, and the coagulation
cannot either, so the only channels that need a rule are the ones that carry no
distribution and no gate.

Collecting \cref{eq:d-coagmoments,eq:d-LN,eq:d-gb} and the transfer with
\cref{eq:d-assemble-n,eq:d-assemble-c,eq:d-assemble-q} gives
\begin{align}
  \dot n^k_{sL} &= J^k_{sL} - \Delta N - \Phi^k_{\min} \pm \Gamma^{n}_{\theta}
                   - \nu_{gb}\Theta^{k}_{0} - \nu_{\mathrm{van}}\Xi^{k}_{0},
  \nonumber\\
  \dot c^k_{sL} &= \left.\dot c^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{growth}}
                   + G_k - \Delta C - m_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min}
                   \pm \Gamma^{c}_{\theta}
                   - \nu_{gb}\Theta^{k}_{1} - \nu_{\mathrm{van}}\Xi^{k}_{1},
  \nonumber\\
  \dot q^k_{sL} &= \left.\dot q^k_{sL}\right|_{\mathrm{FP}}
                   + J^k_{sL}(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k)^{2} - m^{2}_{\min}\Phi^k_{\min}
                   \pm \Gamma^{q}_{\theta}
                   - \nu_{gb}\Theta^{k}_{2} - \nu_{\mathrm{van}}\Xi^{k}_{2},
  \label{eq:d-block}
\end{align}
which are \cref{eq:block-n,eq:block-c,eq:block-q} term for term, and every one
of those terms is now a moment of a term of \cref{eq:master}. One closure was
needed along the way and only one --- the pair-weighted second moment of the
coagulation, \cref{eq:d-coagmoments} at $j=2$ --- and it is stated below
alongside the two channels that carry no distribution of their own and so must
not be allowed to imply one.

What remains is to record why the triple $(n,c,q)$ the reduction produces stays
a triple of moments. $\Delta_k=q^k_{sL}n^k_{sL}/(c^k_{sL})^{2}\ge1$ is the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality on a non-negative measure. A state with $\Delta_k<1$
--- or with $q<0$ outright --- is therefore not an inaccurate description of a
distribution; it is not the moment triple of any distribution. Most of the
channels keep the triple realizable on their own, and the reduction above is
where that is shown: the gated removals leave a genuine sub-measure,
\cref{eq:d-gb}; the loop--network removal is first order at a rate independent
of $m$ and takes every moment down in proportion, \cref{eq:d-LN}; the
coagulation raises $q$ by \cref{eq:d-coagmoments}; and the Fokker--Planck part
is the moment of a non-negative density. What is left needs stating, and three
rules cover it --- the first two exact, the third a closure.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Nucleation deposits at its declared size:} $J^k_{sL}
      (m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k)^{2}$. This is genuine broadening,
      $J(m^{\mathrm{nuc}}-\bar m)^{2}\ge0$, and it is correct because the channel
      really does deposit at $m^{\mathrm{nuc}}_k$.
\item \textbf{The floor current debits at $m_{\min}$:}
      $-m_{\min}^{2}\Phi^k_{\min}$. This is a genuine statement about the lower
      tail.
\item \textbf{Channels with no distribution of their own are
      shape-preserving:} $\mathrm dq=\Delta_k(2\bar m\,\mathrm dc
      -\bar m^{2}\mathrm dn)$. This is a closure and the only one the immobile
      block needs beyond the log-normal itself. It is what supplies the
      pair-weighted second moment of the coagulation,
      \cref{eq:d-coagmoments} at $j=2$, which $\Delta N_{LL}$ alone does not
      determine; and it is what the legacy annealing surrogate would need and
      cannot have.
\end{enumerate}

Rule 3 fixes a magnitude, not a sign, and the distinction matters because the
sign is where this term was got wrong. That coalescence \emph{raises} $q$ is
\cref{eq:d-coagmoments}: a coalescing loop does not leave the family, it
\emph{merges}, its defects stay, and fewer loops holding the same content are
\emph{larger} loops, so the $j=2$ integrand is $2mm'\ge0$ whatever the kernel.
Debiting it instead by the removed number times $\langle m^{2}\rangle$, which is
the natural-looking reading, inverts the sign of the term that carries $98\%$ of
the loop-number loss. The symptom in a calculation is not an error but a slow
drift: on one nine-family march, $q$ crossed zero at 1\,dpa and was negative on
$40\%$ of the basal nodes at 10\,dpa. What rule 3 then settles is how much, and
\cref{eq:d-shapeneutral,eq:d-deltaflow} say it in the form that carries meaning:
the rule makes coalescence exactly shape-neutral, where a true coagulation
relaxes $\Delta_k$ toward the fixed point $\Delta_k=2$ at which a merge changes
no shape. It is therefore not a bound but a substitution of one attractor for
none, and its cost is $\Delta_k^{-1/8}$ on the sink prefactor and nothing else.

The legacy annealing surrogate cannot be made consistent, only avoided.
It debits content at a \emph{fixed} size, so the pair $(\mathrm dn,\mathrm dc)$
it declares corresponds to a removed sub-population of mean $M\ne\bar m$.
Debiting $q$ proportionally over-removes; debiting at $M$ --- the smallest value
Cauchy--Schwarz permits for the removed part --- lowers $\Delta_k$ by
$a(M/\bar m-1)^{2}$ on a narrow family. No debit is realizable, because the
surrogate is removing loops of a size that is not there. That is an independent
argument for the peripheral-emission channel of \cref{app:emission}, which
deletes the fitted lifetimes outright.

The two blocks differ, in the end, in exactly one structural respect, and it is
the transport term. It survives on $\mathcal M$, where it couples every node to
its neighbors and makes \cref{eq:block-mobile} a global nonlinear problem; and
it is absent on $\mathcal F$, where $\bm D^\alpha_n=\bm0$ leaves a system of
ordinary differential equations in which no neighbor's state appears. That the
immobile block has no spatial coupling is not an approximation made for the
convenience of the solver. It is a property of \cref{eq:master} restricted to
clusters that do not diffuse, and it is what the operator split of
\cref{sec:split} exploits.

\section{Capture efficiencies from the diffusion tensor}
\label{app:woo}

The orientation factors $A_{h}(p)$ of \cref{eq:Afactors} follow from Woo's
treatment of a dislocation loop as an absorber in an anisotropically diffusing
medium~\citep{woo1981sink,woo1982intrinsic,woo1988theory}, and in the tensorial
form given by Po~\citep{po2026tensorialdad} the correction to the sink strength of a loop
of axis $\bm\alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\tr\Dten+\bm\alpha^{T}\Dten\bm\alpha}{4\overline D},
  \label{eq:tensorialDAD}
\end{equation}
which is universal in loop orientation and in the degree of anisotropy. For a
diagonal tensor with basal components $D_\perp$ and axial component
$D_\parallel$, and with $p=(D_\parallel/D_\perp)^{1/6}$ and
$\overline D=(\det\Dten)^{1/3}=D_\perp^{2/3}D_\parallel^{1/3}$,
\cref{eq:tensorialDAD} evaluates on a basal loop ($\bm\alpha\parallel[0001]$) to
$A_c(p)=p$ and on a prismatic loop ($\bm\alpha\perp[0001]$) to
$A_a(p)=(p+p^{-2})/2$.

The two forms differ in a way that has quantitative consequences and is easy to
overlook. $A_c$ is monotone increasing, so raising the anisotropy of a species
monotonically raises its capture by basal loops. $A_a$ has a \emph{minimum} at
$p=2^{1/3}=1.2599$, so raising the anisotropy from $p=1$ first \emph{lowers}
prismatic capture. A single change in $p^{v}$ therefore moves the two habits in
opposite directions. Setting $p^{v}=1$ where a fit had placed it at $1.179$
lowers $Z_{\mathrm{basal}}(v)$ by a factor of $0.848$ while \emph{raising}
$Z_{\mathrm{prism}}(v)$ by $1.053$ --- starving basal growth and accelerating
prismatic shrinkage at once. Any statement about the effect of anisotropy that
does not distinguish the habits is incomplete.

The co-growth condition of \cref{sec:coexistence} follows directly. Both growth
inequalities in \cref{eq:growconds} reduce to bounds on the single arrival ratio
$x$, and the window between them has width $g(p^{v})/g(p^{i})$ with
$g(p)=2/(1+p^{-3})$, strictly increasing --- so the window is open exactly when
$p^{i}<p^{v}$.

Two limits on this result should be stated. It governs the \emph{absorption}
flux only: cascade nucleation $G_k$ is added independently and no anisotropy
affects it, so the criterion does not govern net population evolution. And an
isotropic calculation is not a zero of the criterion but another parameter
point, so a ratio taken against one cannot test a statement about signs.

\section{Peripheral emission and the loop binding energy}
\label{app:emission}

The mean-field model represents loop annealing by two fitted lifetimes, one per
character. They are replaced here by an emission channel with no fitted rate, in
which a loop exchanges vacancies across its own perimeter against its own
equilibrium concentration.

The binding energy of a vacancy to a loop of $m$ defects is the difference of
the loop self-energies, signed by the family's character and shifted by the work
the applied stress does on the defect it adds:
\begin{equation}
  E^{b}_k(m)=E^{f}_v
    -\varsigma_s(v)\bigl[E_L^{k}(m)-E_L^{k}(m-1)\bigr]
    -\Sigma_k\Omega ,
  \label{eq:Eb}
\end{equation}
with $\varsigma_s(v)=\pm1$ the polarity product of \cref{eq:growth} --- adding a
vacancy grows a vacancy family and shrinks an interstitial one, which is the
whole of the sign --- and $\Sigma_k=(\bvec_k\!\cdot\!\bm\sigma\!\cdot\!\nvec_k)/
(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k$ the resolved stress the tensor supplies.

$E_L^{k}$ is the \emph{anisotropic} elastic self-energy of a circular loop of
radius $R=\lambda_k\sqrt m$, plus the family's own fault term:
\begin{equation}
  E_L^{k}(m)=\frac{\overline K_k\,|\bvec_k|^{2}\,R}{2}
         \left[\ln\frac{8R}{|\bvec_k|}-2\right]
       + \pi R^{2}\gamma_k ,
  \qquad
  \lambda_k=\sqrt{\frac{\Omega}{\pi(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k}} .
  \label{eq:EL}
\end{equation}
Three things in \cref{eq:EL} are not the textbook isotropic loop and are the
reason it is written out. The prefactor is $\overline K_k$, the
perimeter-averaged \emph{anisotropic} energy factor --- the Stroh/Barnett--Lothe
$K(\theta)$ for the family's own $(\bvec_k,\nvec_k)$, integrated over the loop
line and evaluated at Fisher and Renken's $300\,$K constants for
$\alpha$-Zr~\citep{fisher1964single} --- and \emph{not} $\mu/(1-\nu)$. The
logarithm is scaled by $|\bvec_k|$, the full Burgers magnitude, while the
\emph{area} the loop presents per stored defect is set by the edge component
$(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k$; the two differ for the faulted basal state, whose
$\tfrac16\langle20\bar23\rangle$ vector carries a basal partial. And $\gamma_k$
is per family rather than one $\gamma_{\mathrm{sf}}$: only $c_f$ is faulted, but
nothing in the expression assumes that.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccccc@{}}
\toprule
Family & $\overline K_k$ & $|\bvec_k|$ & $(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k$
       & $\lambda_k$ & $\gamma_k$ & $\overline K_k|\bvec_k|^{2}$
       & $\overline K_k\big/\tfrac{\mu}{1-\nu}$ \\
       & [GPa] & [nm] & [nm] & [nm] & [J\,m$^{-2}$] & [nJ\,m$^{-1}$] & \\
\midrule
$a_1$--$a_3$, interstitial & 53.1 & 0.3230 & 0.3230 & 0.1514 & 0     & 5.54  & 1.062 \\
$a_1$--$a_3$, vacancy      & 53.1 & 0.3230 & 0.3230 & 0.1514 & 0     & 5.54  & 1.062 \\
$c_f$, faulted basal       & 53.0 & 0.3179 & 0.2575 & 0.1696 & 0.124 & 5.36  & 1.060 \\
$c_p$, perfect basal       & 57.8 & 0.5150 & 0.5150 & 0.1199 & 0     & 15.33 & 1.156 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
$|\bvec_{c_f}|=\sqrt{(c/2)^{2}+a^{2}/3}$ and every length follows from the same
$a=3.23\,$\AA, $c=5.15\,$\AA{} and $\Omega$ the rest of the model uses;
$\mu/(1-\nu)=50.0\,$GPa at $\mu=33\,$GPa, $\nu=0.34$. The anisotropy is not a
uniform rescaling, which is why it cannot be absorbed into the fit: it is
$+6\%$ on the prismatic families and $+16\%$ on the perfect basal one, so an
isotropic prefactor would not merely shift the emission barrier, it would
misorder the two habits by $9\%$. The fault energy is the one number here worth
a sensitivity check --- \emph{ab initio} values for the $I_1$ intrinsic basal
fault scatter over $0.10$--$0.15\,$J\,m$^{-2}$ and \cref{eq:EL} is strictly
linear in it.

What the model evaluates is not \cref{eq:EL} but its derivative, the difference
in \cref{eq:Eb} taken in the continuum limit:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm dE_L^{k}}{\mathrm dm}
    = \underbrace{\frac{\gamma_k\Omega}{(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k}}_{\text{fault, size-independent}}
    + \underbrace{\frac{\overline K_k|\bvec_k|^{2}\lambda_k^{2}}{4R}
      \left[\ln\frac{8R}{|\bvec_k|}-1\right]}_{\text{capillary, }\sim\ln R/R} ,
  \label{eq:dEdm}
\end{equation}
which follows from $\mathrm dR/\mathrm dm=\lambda_k^{2}/2R$. The split is what
separates the two basal states. At $R=5\,$nm and zero stress the capillary term
is $0.184\,$eV for $c_f$ against $0.231\,$eV for $c_p$ --- the faulted state has
the \emph{smaller} capillary term, its $\overline K_k|\bvec_k|^{2}$ being $2.9$
times smaller --- but $c_f$ carries a further $0.070\,$eV of fault that does not
fall with size at all, and that reverses the total: $0.254\,$eV against
$0.231\,$eV, so $E^{b}$ is $1.346\,$eV for $c_f$ and $1.369\,$eV for $c_p$ and
the faulted state is the \emph{less} strongly bound of the two. The gap widens
monotonically with size, $0.011\,$eV at $R=3\,$nm to $0.061\,$eV at $50\,$nm,
because only the capillary halves decay; that widening is the driving force
behind the unfaulting transfer of \cref{eq:basal-chain}, recovered here rather
than imposed. The prismatic families sit at $0.151\,$eV with no fault term at
all, and their $E^{b}$ splits by character: $1.449\,$eV for a vacancy family and
$1.751\,$eV for an interstitial one, the $\varsigma_s(v)$ of \cref{eq:Eb}
carrying the same $0.151\,$eV in opposite directions.

The local equilibrium vacancy concentration at the loop periphery is then
$c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k(m)=\exp\bigl[-E^{b}_k(m)/k_BT\bigr]$, and the
emission current is the same capture expression run backwards against it,
\begin{equation}
  S^{k}_{vL}=\Pi^k_{sL}\,\overline D^{v}Z_{vk,v}\;
             c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k(\bar m^k_{sL}),
  \label{eq:Semit}
\end{equation}
which enters \cref{eq:master-c} through $\spvec S_{vL}$ and
\cref{eq:block-c} with the opposite sign. It is not assembled as a separate
term: the solver carries it as a signed driving concentration
$c^{v}-c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k(\bar m^k_{sL})$ inside the same capture sum that
\cref{eq:growth} already forms, which is the same quantity and makes detailed
balance exact by construction --- the channel vanishes identically at
$c^{v}=c^{v,\mathrm{eq}}_k$ --- rather than by the cancellation of an absorption
and an emission term carrying different prefactors. Thermal \emph{interstitial}
emission is not carried at all: at $873\,$K it is $e^{-1.4/k_BT}\approx10^{-8}$
of the vacancy channel.

Three properties recommend this over a fitted lifetime. \emph{One expression
serves both characters}: the same mechanism shrinks a vacancy loop, by emitting
the vacancies it holds, and grows an interstitial one, by emitting vacancies that
recombine with its interstitials, so no separate interstitial-loop annealing
model is needed. \emph{The climb threshold emerges rather than being imposed}:
growth requires the arriving flux to exceed \cref{eq:Semit}, which reproduces the
classical critical radius without it appearing as a parameter. And \emph{it
removes content, not loops}, which is physically correct --- emission shrinks a
loop continuously --- but has a consequence that must be recognized: after this
replacement, coalescence becomes the \emph{only} sink on vacancy loop number.
That is stated in \cref{sec:conclusions} as the leading open problem, and it is a
consequence of doing the emission physics correctly rather than an artifact of
it.

\section{State-vector layout and the conservation accumulators}
\label{app:state}

The integrated state at each node is a single flat vector whose width depends on
the model controls. The layout is fixed and every index keeps its meaning as the
controls add fields:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule
Indices & Contents & Count \\
\midrule
$0$--$3$   & mobile: $c^{v},c^{i},c^{2i},c^{3i}$ & 4 \\
$4$--$7$   & number of families $0$--$3$ & 4 \\
$8$--$11$  & content of families $0$--$3$ & 4 \\
$12$--$17$ & accumulators: production, recombination, sink absorption,
             each per character & 6 \\
$18$       & network dislocation density $\rho_N$ & 1 \\
$19$--$23$ & number of families $4$--$8$ & 5 \\
$24$--$28$ & content of families $4$--$8$ & 5 \\
$29$--$37$ & second content $q$ of all nine families & 9 \\
$38$--$39$ & grain-boundary loop ledger, per character & 2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Widths are therefore 19 (four families, two moments), 29 (nine families, two
moments), 38 (nine families, three moments) and 40 with the boundary ledger.
Two properties of this layout are worth recording because both were established
by their failure modes.

The appended families are appended, not interleaved. Every index below
19 means what it meant in the four-family model, so a state written by one
configuration is readable by another up to its own width. The corollary is that
a narrower state is always \emph{accepted} by a wider solver, the appended slots
defaulting to zero --- which is exactly why a mismatch between the width a driver
allocates and the width the model requires is silent rather than fatal, and why
the checkpoint fingerprint records the actual width.

The block written to the spatial code is ordered the other way. It is
moment-major --- all nine numbers, then all nine contents, then all nine $q$ ---
so the bridge between the two is a scatter and not a copy. Exactly one routine
knows that mapping; performing it at a call site is what once put an 18-wide
block into an 8-wide slice.

The accumulators are per-interval; the ledger is cumulative. The six
conservation accumulators are reset at the start of each dose interval, so a
stored snapshot holds one interval's production and must be summed to give a
running total. The two grain-boundary accumulators are never reset and are
already cumulative. Differencing the wrong one against the wrong other gives a
channel that appears to carry $6600$ times total production, or none at all,
depending on which way the error runs.
