\section{Defect Classes and Reactions}\label{sec:classes}

\subsection{The general class set}
\label{sec:classes-general}

The primary irradiation defects in hcp zirconium are vacancies, self-interstitial
atoms and their clusters. Because the lattice is elastically and
crystallographically anisotropic, a cluster is not specified by its size alone:
its Burgers-vector character and habit plane determine its contribution to
irradiation growth, to hardening, and to the sink field seen by every other
species. The observed loop population in irradiated Zr and its alloys is
conventionally grouped into \aloop{} loops and \cloop{}-component
loops~\citep{kelly1973characterization,ChoiKim2013,tournadre2012experimental},
with
\begin{equation}
  \bvec_a=\tfrac13\langle11\bar20\rangle,
  \qquad
  \bvec_c \parallel [0001],
  \label{eq:burgers-general}
\end{equation}
the \cloop{} loops carrying a Burgers-vector component along the hexagonal axis.

Four principal cluster families span the population, together with the two free
monomers~\citep{Carpenter1988,Northwood1977,OnimusBechade2012,Yi2013,Doriot2015}:
\begin{equation}
  I_n^{a},\qquad V_n^{a},\qquad S_n,\qquad V_n^{c},
  \label{eq:zr-loop-classes}
\end{equation}
where $I_n^{a}$ is an interstitial prismatic \aloop{} loop of $n$ excess
self-interstitials on a $\{10\bar10\}$ habit; $V_n^{a}$ a vacancy prismatic loop
of the same habit and Burgers vector but opposite character; $S_n$ a basal
stacking-fault pyramid (SFP) of $n$ aggregated vacancies, bounded by basal
$\tfrac16\langle20\bar23\rangle$ and prismatic facets, formed directly from
collapsed cascade vacancy debris; and $V_n^{c}$ a basal vacancy \cloop{}-component
loop, which nucleates above a temperature- and dose-rate-dependent threshold, in
part by unfaulting of supercritical SFPs, and which drives breakaway irradiation
growth at high fluence~\citep{holt1986c,HoltCausey1993,Yi2013}. The free monomers
are the mono-interstitial $C_i$ and the monovacancy $C_v$. No helium and no
three-dimensional void population is carried: Zr alloys generate a negligible
$(n,\alpha)$ helium yield, and multi-vacancy cascade debris in $\alpha$-Zr
collapses onto basal or prismatic habits rather than forming compact
cavities~\citep{Northwood1977,OnimusBechade2012}.

The classification separates \emph{character} --- interstitial or vacancy ---
from \emph{crystallography} --- prismatic, basal pyramidal, or basal planar ---
and both distinctions are load-bearing. Two families that share a habit plane
and a Burgers vector but differ in character, $I_n^a$ and $V_n^a$, respond to
the same mobile fluxes with opposite sign; two families that share a character
but differ in habit, $V_n^a$ and $V_n^c$, present differently oriented capture
cross-sections to an anisotropic diffusion field and therefore compete for the
same vacancies on unequal terms. It is that second competition, taken up in
\cref{sec:coexistence}, that the self-consistent formulation exists to describe
without a free parameter.

\subsection{The reduced set actually integrated}
\label{sec:classes-reduced}

\Cref{eq:zr-loop-classes} is size-resolved: each family is an infinite ladder in
$n$. The model solved here replaces the ladder by a small number of \emph{moments}
of the size distribution per family (\cref{sec:moments}) and resolves instead the
crystallographic \emph{variants} that a size-resolved but variant-lumped model
cannot distinguish. Writing the character as $s\in\{i,v\}$ and the habit as
$k\in\{a_1,a_2,a_3,c_0,c_f,c_p\}$, the families carried are
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F=
  \underbrace{\{(i,a_1),(i,a_2),(i,a_3)\}}_{\text{prismatic interstitial}}
  \cup
  \underbrace{\{(v,a_1),(v,a_2),(v,a_3)\}}_{\text{prismatic vacancy}}
  \cup
  \underbrace{\{(v,c_0),(v,c_f),(v,c_p)\}}_{\text{basal vacancy chain}},
  \label{eq:families}
\end{equation}
nine in all, of which eight are dislocation loops and one is not: $c_0$ is the
stacking-fault pyramid, the precursor from which the basal loops form. Write
$\mathcal F_L=\mathcal F\setminus\{(v,c_0)\}$ for the eight loop families;
every statement below that invokes a perimeter, a line density or a coalescence
channel is a statement about $\mathcal F_L$.

Three features distinguish \cref{eq:families} from the lumped, stress-aligned
pair of the earlier mean-field model, and each is a statement about
crystallography rather than about the solver.

\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{The prismatic loops are resolved into their three variants
      $a_1,a_2,a_3$, of both characters.} The variants carry their own habit
      normals $\hat{\bm a}_k$, so the resolved stress on each is computed rather
      than parameterized, and the response to an arbitrary stress state is a
      redistribution among the three rather than a shift of one scalar. At zero
      deviatoric stress they are equivalent by symmetry, receive equal shares,
      and summing them recovers the lumped population exactly --- which is the
      regression test on the change.
\item \textbf{There is no basal interstitial family.} Basal interstitial
      \cloop{} loops are not a reported population in neutron-irradiated Zr. A
      formal $c_i$ population is retained in the growth inequalities of
      \cref{sec:coexistence} for symmetry but is not a state variable.
\item \textbf{The basal vacancy population is a three-state chain,}
      $c_0\to c_f\to c_p$. The pyramid $c_0$ is not planar and stores its
      vacancies with a nearly isotropic relaxation volume, whereas the faulted
      state $c_f$ and the perfect state $c_p$ are planar and store different
      vacancy content per unit area. Both transitions are thermally activated
      and both are one-way.
\end{enumerate}

The two habits are mutually \emph{orthogonal}: the normals $\hat{\bm a}_k$ of the
three prismatic variants lie in $(0001)$, while the basal normal is $[0001]$
itself. That orthogonality is why no single electron-beam direction images both
populations at true size --- a zone axis showing one family face-on views the
other edge-on --- so any comparison of \cref{eq:families} against TEM densities
must combine at least two zone axes, and a single-axis micrograph constrains at
most one habit. It is also why the calibration of \cref{sec:calibration} treats
the \aloop{} and \cloop{} data as separate target sets rather than as one.

\subsection{Geometry: from content to radius}
\label{sec:classes-geometry}

Each family carries a character sign $\eta_s$ ($+1$ interstitial, $-1$ vacancy)
and three spatially resolved fields: the \emph{cluster density}
$n^k_{sL}(\xv)$, clusters per host atom; the \emph{defect content}
$c^k_{sL}(\xv)$, point defects stored in that family per host atom; and the
\emph{second content} $q^k_{sL}(\xv)$ of \cref{sec:moments}, which carries the
width of the size distribution. All three are dimensionless, so a number density
per unit volume is $n/\Omega$ and a stored-defect density is $c/\Omega$, with
$\Omega$ the atomic volume.

The quantity that converts loop area into stored defects is not $|\bvec|$ but the
edge component $\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec$: a loop of radius $R$ holds
$\pi R^2(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)/\Omega$ defects. The mean radius, the mean size and
the line density of family $(s,k)$ therefore follow from its content and density
as
\begin{equation}
  R^k_{sL}=\lambda_k\sqrt{\frac{c^k_{sL}}{n^k_{sL}}},
  \qquad
  \lambda_k=\sqrt{\frac{\Omega}{\pi\,(\bvec\!\cdot\!\nvec)_k}},
  \qquad
  m^k_{sL}=\frac{c^k_{sL}}{n^k_{sL}},
  \qquad
  \rho^k_{sL}=2\pi R^k_{sL}\,n^k_{sL},
  \label{eq:radius}
\end{equation}
with one geometric length per habit: $\lambda_a$ shared by all six prismatic
families, and $\lambda_{c_f}$, $\lambda_{c_p}$ for the two basal states.
\Cref{eq:radius} describes a planar object and does not apply to the pyramid,
whose stored vacancies fill a volume rather than bound an area; its size follows
from the volumetric relation
\begin{equation}
  R^{c_0}_{vL}=\left(\frac{m^{c_0}_{vL}\,\Omega}{\sqrt8}\right)^{1/3},
  \label{eq:Rsfp}
\end{equation}
so the pyramid carries no $\lambda_k$, no Burgers vector and no line density.
What it does carry is a capture cross-section, and \cref{sec:formulation} shows
that a cross-section is the only thing either the growth law or the sink
strengths ever ask of a family. \Cref{tab:cd-families} lists the nine families
with the crystallography that fixes their geometry.

\subsection{Reaction classes}
\label{sec:classes-reactions}

The coupled kinetics are generated from a directed multigraph --- the
\emph{reaction admissibility graph} (RAG) --- whose vertices are the admissible
cluster states and whose edges are the elementary reactions permitted by the
polarity, habit-plane and mobility constraints above. The master equations
follow by a deterministic graph walk: at each vertex the time derivative is the
signed sum of the rate kernels of all incoming (gain) and outgoing (loss) edges.
Vertices partition by polarity,
\begin{align}
  \mathcal V^{+} &= \{C_i\}\cup\{C_{2i},C_{3i}\}\cup\{(i,a_k)\}, \\
  \mathcal V^{-} &= \{C_v\}\cup\{(v,a_k)\}\cup\{(v,c_0),(v,c_f),(v,c_p)\},
\end{align}
so the interstitial prismatic families lie on the positive branch and the six
vacancy families on the negative one. \Cref{tab:rag-processes} catalogs the edge
classes; \cref{fig:rag} draws the graph for the family set of
\cref{eq:families}.
