\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Three changes were made to the DisloCluster 0-D side and to its coupling with
the spatially resolved code. (i) The right-hand side was moved into a single
scalar-type-templated core so that the residual and an \emph{exact} Jacobian,
obtained by forward-mode automatic differentiation, are two instantiations of
one definition; SUNDIALS' difference-quotient Jacobian is thereby retired.
(ii) The six point-defect conservation accumulators were recognized as pure
quadratures and moved out of the implicit system into CVODES quadrature
variables, shrinking the Newton block from $19\times19$ to $13\times13$
standalone and to $9\times9$ under the operator split. (iii) The 0-D\,$\to$\,3-D
handoff, which previously collapsed the marched state to four scalars, was
replaced by a genuine per-node field exchange through MoDELib3's own
cluster-dynamics configuration block.

Measurement then redirected the effort: at $n=19$ the dense linear algebra was
never the bottleneck. Profiling with the integration window collapsed to zero
showed that per-case construction and destruction of SUNDIALS objects consumed
$1.31\,$s of thread time against $0.2\,$s of actual solving in a 256-point
coupling substep. Reusing one solver workspace per OpenMP thread cut the
end-to-end march wall time by a factor of $1.67$ at $q=1024$ quadrature points
--- more than the two originally requested changes combined. The report states
all three outcomes as measured, including the two that did not pay off in wall
time.

Two further sections document the coupling in operation.
Section~\ref{sec:fieldcost} measures the full 1--26 \dpa\ march on the
1~\textmu m single crystal in three configurations, separating the genuine
solver speedup ($1.95\times$ at identical work) from the cost of resolving a
field on 24\,115 nodes instead of averaging 25 line points to four scalars.
Section~\ref{sec:integrators} characterizes MoDELib3's own nodal immobile
integrator against the CVODE march --- correcting, along the way, the common
description of it as explicit Euler: it is a first-order semi-implicit scheme,
and it has to be, because the measured stiffness puts its step 46\,000 times
beyond the forward-Euler limit. Section~\ref{sec:provenance} then traces where
each scheme came from, against the correct upstream:
\code{mlm335/MoDELib-fullCD}, which already carries a full immobile field and
its own IMEX integrator. MoDELib3's immobile solver is therefore a
re-discretization of an existing scheme --- nodal collocation and a single fused
semi-implicit update, in place of fullCD's Galerkin mass-matrix projection
followed by explicit Euler and sequential implicit loss factors --- and not,
as an earlier revision of this report stated on the strength of the wrong
repository, an invention from nothing.

A later revision corrects the coupled route itself. As first written it did not
perform an operator split: it read its mobile field out of a previously recorded
standalone run, so $\mathbf C_M$ never responded to the immobile state the march
was building. Section~\ref{sec:fastinloop} puts MoDELib3's own steady mobile
solve back inside the loop, through a new \code{useImmobileSolver} switch that
lets a DDomp run perform the fast step alone.
Section~\ref{sec:seedrelax} then measures what that repairs: a 0-D seed at
0.1 \dpa\ carries a $C_v$ some $690\times$ above the quasi-steady value and no
boundary layer at all, and the fast step supplies both corrections on its first
call.
\end{abstract}

\section{Starting point}
% ============================================================================

The 0-D model integrates $N_{\mathrm{eq}}=19$ ordinary differential equations:
12 physical species, 6 point-defect conservation accumulators, and the evolving
network dislocation density $\rho_N$. In the operator-split coupling the four
mobile species $\mathbf C_M=[C_v,C_i,C_{2i},C_{3i}]$ are frozen at the value
supplied by the 3-D fast solve (\code{freeze\_mobile=1}), and the remaining
state is marched independently at every quadrature point, one case per point in
an OpenMP batch subprocess.

Before this work the solver had the following properties.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No Jacobian was supplied.} \code{CVodeSetJacFn} was never called,
      so SUNDIALS formed the $19\times19$ dense Jacobian by difference
      quotients, costing 19 extra residual evaluations per Jacobian setup.
\item \textbf{All 19 equations were implicit.} The accumulators were carried as
      ODE state, entered the Newton system and the error test, and were
      factorized in every LU.
\item \textbf{Every case built its own solver.} \code{integrate\_one} created
      and destroyed a \code{SUNContext}, state vector, dense matrix, linear
      solver and CVODE memory per invocation --- i.e.\ once per quadrature
      point per substep.
\item \textbf{The 0-D\,$\to$\,3-D carrier was four scalars.} The driver
      averaged the marched state over all points and wrote one number density
      and one mean radius per loop family into MoDELib's microstructure
      generator inputs.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================================
\section{Structural observation: the accumulators are pure quadratures}
% ============================================================================

Rows 12--17 of the right-hand side are written but columns 12--17 are never
read: no equation in the system depends on an accumulator value. Inspection of
\code{rate\_equations.cpp} confirms that \code{y[12..17]} appears nowhere on the
right of any assignment. The Jacobian therefore has the block structure
\begin{equation}
J=\begin{pmatrix}
J_{ss} & 0 \\[2pt]
J_{as} & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
s=\{\text{species},\rho_N\},\quad a=\{\text{accumulators}\},
\end{equation}
so the accumulators are quadratures of the state trajectory,
$\mathbf a(t)=\int \mathbf g(\mathbf y_s(\tau))\,\mathrm d\tau$, and belong in
CVODES' quadrature module rather than in the Newton system.

Combining this with the operator split, which freezes the four mobile species,
gives the implicit block dimensions of Table~\ref{tab:dims}.

\section{Implementation}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{One templated core, two instantiations}

The physics was moved verbatim from \code{rate\_equations.cpp} into
\code{rate\_equations\_core.h} as
\begin{center}
\code{template <class T> void rhs\_core(const T* y, T* ydot, const Parameters\& P)}
\end{center}
instantiated with \code{T = double} for the residual and with
\code{T = Dual<N>} (\code{dual.h}) for the Jacobian. Forward-mode automatic
differentiation evaluates value and all $N$ directional derivatives in a
\emph{single} sweep: \code{sqrt}, \code{exp} and \code{cbrt} are evaluated once
and their derivatives propagated by a multiply, rather than the $N$ complete
residual evaluations a difference quotient needs.

Two properties motivated AD over a hand-written analytic Jacobian. First,
exactness: there is no perturbation size to choose and no truncation error.
Second, and more important for maintenance, the Jacobian cannot drift out of
sync with the residual --- hand-differentiating the coalescence Avrami gates and
the smoothstep minimum-size gate would have created a second copy of the physics
to keep aligned, on a code base that already maintains a Python mirror.

\paragraph{Bit-identity.} Floating-point addition is not associative, so the
transcription preserved the exact grouping of every expression. Where the
original wrote \code{k1 * k2 * C * D}, meaning $((k_1k_2)C)D$, the templated
core hoists $k_1k_2$ into a named \code{double} and keeps the remaining order.
This was verified rather than asserted: a reference binary was built from the
git-HEAD sources and the outputs compared byte for byte
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Reduced implicit block}

With \code{--reduced=1} the state vector holds only the implicit components and
the accumulators are advanced by \code{CVodeQuadInit}. Quadrature error control
is left at its default (off), so the accumulators ride the step sequence the
state equations demand instead of constraining it. The 19-column output
contract is unchanged: each row is reassembled from the reduced state, the
frozen mobile values and the quadrature vector.

\label{sec:workspace}

frozen-mobile batch with the integration window collapsed to

constructions per substep to 24, one per thread.

\section{Verification}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{The AD Jacobian}

A standalone tool (\code{jac\_check.cpp}) compares the AD Jacobian against a
central-difference Jacobian of the \emph{same} core, differencing column $j$
with a relative step $h_j=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}|y_j|$ because the state
spans some 34 decades. It was run at 20 states sampled along a converged
trajectory from $10^{-8}$ to $100$ \dpa.

Two classes of entry are excluded, for reasons belonging to the finite
difference and not to the AD:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FD-unresolvable entries.} A central difference can only see an
      entry whose contribution $J_{ij}h_j$ rises above the floating-point
      granularity of $f_i$. At the initial condition, $\mathrm dC_i/\mathrm dt$
      is pinned near $G_i\approx10^{-7}$ while its $C_v$ derivative moves it by
      ${\sim}10^{-28}$, i.e.\ $10^{-5}$ of one unit in the last place. The
      difference quotient returns exactly zero there at \emph{any} step size;
      AD returns the exact value. Entries are compared only where the signal
      exceeds 100 ULP. Note that this is precisely the blindness SUNDIALS'
      difference-quotient Jacobian suffers, so the AD Jacobian is strictly more
      informative in this region.
\item \textbf{Components on the concentration floor}, where \code{fl()} makes
      the residual non-smooth: AD returns the one-sided derivative while a
      central difference straddles the kink.
\end{itemize}

The result is in Table~\ref{tab:jac}. The discrepancy falls by two decades per
decade of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}$ across the usable range, the $O(h^2)$
signature of a correct derivative with the finite difference's own truncation
error dominating.

\subsection{Where the improvement actually came from}

Stated plainly, and against the expectation that motivated the work:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Workspace reuse is the whole wall-clock gain:} $1.67\times$ at
      $q=1024$, and it grows with $q$ because the per-case setup it removes
      scales with the point count while the per-thread construction does not.
\item \textbf{The AD Jacobian delivers its target metric:} residual evaluations
      fall by 33\% ($366\,170\to243\,682$) and difference-quotient evaluations
      go to zero. Wall time is neutral, because one \code{Dual<19>} sweep costs
      about what the 19 scalar evaluations it replaces cost. Its durable value
      is exactness, the elimination of the FD-blind entries of
      Table~\ref{tab:jac}, and a Jacobian that cannot fall out of step with the
      residual.
\item \textbf{The $9\times9$ reduction delivers its target metric --- a 92\%
      cut in LU flops --- but LU was never the bottleneck at $n=19$.} It also
      reduces the step count by 18\%. It nonetheless loses slightly in wall
      time, because of the quadrature cost analyzed next.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================================
\subsection{A step-size pathology the reduction cures}
\label{sec:pathology}
% ============================================================================

The \code{warn} column of Table~\ref{tab:phaseB} is not cosmetic. The legacy
path emits, on every substep of every point,
\begin{center}
\code{[WARNING] Internal t = 9.88525e+06 and h = 2.10129e-12 are such that
      t + h = t on the next step}
\end{center}
2048 of them in a 1024-point, 5-substep march.

The cause is the accumulators. Each substep restarts at $t\approx10^{7}$~s with
the accumulators reset to zero, and with atol $=10^{-20}$ their error weights
$w_i=1/(\mathrm{rtol}\,|y_i|+\mathrm{atol})$ are ${\sim}10^{20}$. CVODE's
initial-step heuristic uses the weighted norm of $\dot{\mathbf y}$; with
$\dot a\sim10^{-7}$ this gives $\|\dot{\mathbf y}\|_{\mathrm{WRMS}}\sim10^{13}$
and hence $h_0\sim10^{-13}$, while $\varepsilon t\approx2\times10^{-10}$. The
first step is below the representable resolution of $t$ and is wasted.

Removing the accumulators from the error test eliminates this outright: zero
warnings, and 18\% fewer internal steps. This is the reduction's most defensible
benefit --- it is a robustness property, independent of problem size, whereas
the LU saving is not.

A second, subtler effect works the other way. The error test is a root-mean
square over components, $\|e\|_{\mathrm{WRMS}}=\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_i(e_iw_i)^2}$,
so removing six well-behaved components makes the norm $\sqrt{19/9}=1.45$ times
more sensitive per remaining component. The 19-equation system was being
flattered by six dummy components diluting its error norm; the reduced system
is held to a genuinely tighter standard at the same nominal tolerance.

% ============================================================================
\subsection{The quadrature memo, and why the reduction does not win on time}
\label{sec:memo}
% ============================================================================

CVODES calls the quadrature right-hand side once per completed step. Because
that call needs the same core evaluation the residual has just performed, the
implementation memoizes the accumulator rates on the exact $(t,\mathbf y)$ bit
pattern and reuses them on a match.

The measured hit rate is poor: 1 hit against 2564 misses in the standalone run.
CVODE's corrector evaluates the residual at its Newton iterates, and the
accepted step point $\mathbf y_n$ is generally not one of them, so the memo
misses and a second full core sweep is paid per step. That is exactly the
${+}28\%$ in $n_{\mathrm{core}}$ visible in Table~\ref{tab:phaseA}, and it
cancels the LU saving.

The identified remedy, not implemented here, is to keep the accumulators in the
state vector but neutralize their error weights with a vector \code{atol}
(\code{CVodeSVtolerances} with atol $=10^{300}$ on those six components). That
would retain the step-size cure of Section~\ref{sec:pathology} at \emph{zero}
extra core evaluations, at the cost of a $15\times15$ rather than $9\times9$
block --- which, on this evidence, is the better trade. It is recorded as the
next step rather than claimed as a result.

% ============================================================================
\section{The 0-D $\leftrightarrow$ 3-D field handoff}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{What was wrong}

The previous 0-D\,$\to$\,3-D carrier
(\code{modelib\_fem.write\_sink\_field}) wrote four scalars per dose step --- one
number density and one mean radius per loop family --- into
\code{inputFiles/aLoopsDensity.txt} and \code{inputFiles/frankLoopsDensity.txt}.
Those files drive MoDELib's \emph{microstructure generator}, which places
discrete loops at a uniform target density. The driver therefore had to collapse
the marched state to its volume average before handing it over:
\begin{center}
\code{A = np.mean(np.asarray(Y), axis=0)}
\end{center}
Every bit of spatial structure the slow march produced was discarded on the way
out. In the reverse direction the mobile field was read from
\code{F/quadrature\_*.txt} through a column map whose source comment conceded it
had never been checked against a real run.

\subsection{What MoDELib3 actually offers}

MoDELib3 does not need a scalar. \code{ClusterDynamicsFEM} already carries the
immobile population as a finite-element \emph{field} --- \code{immobileClusters},
with $\code{iSize}=8$ components per node --- and \code{ImmobileSinks} builds the
sink strength from the local value at every quadrature point.
\code{ClusterDynamics::output} writes that field, together with the
$\code{mSize}=4$ mobile components, into the CD block of
\code{evl/evl\_<N>.txt}. Decisively, \code{ClusterDynamicsFEM::\allowbreak
initializeConfiguration} \emph{reads it back}:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
if(size\_t(configIO.cdMatrix().size())==mobileClusters.gSize()+immobileClusters.gSize())\\
\{\\
\hspace*{1em}mobileClusters   = configIO.cdMatrix().block(0,0,nNodes,mSize)\ldots\\
\hspace*{1em}immobileClusters = configIO.cdMatrix().block(0,mSize,nNodes,iSize)\ldots\\
\}
\end{quote}

The CD block is therefore a read/write field channel in both directions, on the
node set the two codes already share. \code{evl/cdNodes.txt} gives the node
coordinates in the same row order, so no search or interpolation is required.

\begin{equation}

four mobile species \emph{are} the 0-D mobile species, in the same order and
units.

\subsection{Notebook wiring}

\code{coupled\_0d\_3d\_ZrMicro.ipynb} gains a \code{"field"} fast-solve backend,
selected automatically when a CD field is present. In that mode the march runs
on the CD node set rather than the synthetic 1-D line, seeds the immobile state
from the 3-D field itself, and after each dose interval writes
\code{evl/evl\_coupled\_<dose>dpa.txt} --- a complete configuration, with the
dislocation records copied through unchanged, that MoDELib can be restarted
from. The previous scalar path and the analytic placeholder both remain
available and unmodified.

Because MoDELib reads its configuration at start-up, the handback takes effect
on a \emph{restart} from the written file. That is the rhythm the operator split
requires anyway (fast solve $\to$ slow march $\to$ fast solve), but it does mean
the coupling is restart-driven rather than in-process: at the default six
snapshots that is six DDomp launches, which is unremarkable; at hundreds of dose
intervals it would argue for driving MoDELib through \code{pyMoDELib} instead.

\section{Integrator comparison: MoDELib's nodal update versus the CVODE march}
\label{sec:integrators}
% ============================================================================

The two codes advance the \emph{same} immobile physics --- eight components per
node --- with entirely different schemes. This section characterizes both, since
the difference is a systematic source of 0-D/3-D discrepancy that is independent
of the physics non-correspondences already documented in \code{CLAUDE.md}.

Everything said here about ``MoDELib'' means MoDELib3, the standalone route
actually used in this work. The upstream \code{MoDELib-fullCD} discretizes the
same physics differently again --- a Galerkin mass-matrix projection of the rate,
then explicit Euler and sequential implicit loss factors, one step per dose step
rather than twenty --- and is characterized separately in
Section~\ref{sec:fullcd}.

\subsection{What MoDELib actually does}

It is worth stating precisely, because the scheme is often described as explicit
Euler and it is not. \code{ClusterDynamicsFEM::solveImmobileClusters} loops over
nodes and, inside each node, over \code{nSub = 20} substeps of
$\mathrm dt = \mathrm{dtMax}/20$, applying

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
// implicit for the linear losses, explicit for the sources\\
const double nNew((n(k)+dt*nucRate)/(1.0+dt*lossN));\\
const double cNew((c(k)+dt*(gdot(k)+Gk+clusShare(k)*clusC(pol)-contentSink))/(1.0+dt*lossC));
\end{quote}

This is a first-order \textbf{semi-implicit (IMEX)} update: the sources
(nucleation, absorbed flux, cascade seed) are explicit, while the linear loss
coefficients --- coalescence, $1/\tau_{vL}$, and the thermal-emission rate
recast as a first-order coefficient --- sit in the denominator. The source
comment records the reason: \emph{``$\tau_{vL}$ is short compared with a dose
step \ldots an explicit update would give $n(1-\mathrm dt/\tau)<0$ and collapse
the vacancy-loop density.''}

With $G=10^{-7}$~\dpa/s and \code{dtMax = 6.95868964e19} in MoDELib units (time
unit $1.4371\times10^{-13}$~s), one dose step is $10^{7}$~s and the immobile
substep is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm dt_{\mathrm{MoDELib}} = \frac{10^{7}\ \mathrm s}{20}
 = 5\times10^{5}\ \mathrm s .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Test method}

Three instruments, all exercising the \emph{same} \code{rate\_equations\_core.h}
right-hand side so that no model difference contaminates the comparison:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Spectrum.} The frozen-mobile $9\times9$ immobile Jacobian is formed
      at states sampled along a converged trajectory and its eigenvalues taken.
      The forward-Euler stability limit is $\mathrm dt<2/|\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda|_{\max}$.
\item \textbf{Fixed-step explicit reference.} A \code{--euler\_nsub=N} mode was
      added to the solver: $N$ forward-Euler substeps per output interval on the
      same core, with the per-substep positivity clamp MoDELib applies. This is
      the \emph{control} --- what the update would do without the implicit loss
      treatment --- not a model of MoDELib's scheme.
\item \textbf{Reference solution.} CVODE at \code{rtol=1e-12}, \code{atol=1e-26}
      with the AD Jacobian, against which every other result is measured.
\end{enumerate}

Two nodes of the 1~\textmu m case bracket the range: the interior node of
maximum $C_v$ and the near-boundary node of minimum $C_v$.

\paragraph{What can and cannot be claimed for MoDELib's scheme.} Its IMEX

local error estimate, no step rejection, no adaptivity, and 20 fixed substeps per

first order and no adaptivity. At the scale of the coupled march this is the
difference between the 3-D solve's nodal update being free and the 0-D march
costing the $574$~s of Table~\ref{tab:march1um}.

\subsection{The upstream scheme, and how MoDELib3 re-discretized it}
\label{sec:fullcd}

fullCD advances the immobile field in three distinct stages per DD step.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Rate, by Galerkin projection.} \code{ImmobileSinkRate} is an
      \code{EvalFunction} evaluated at \emph{quadrature points} --- absorption
      growth, cascade nucleation, and homogeneous cluster nucleation from
      $-\tfrac12\,\mathbf C^{\!\top} \mathsf R_2 \mathbf C$ --- assembled into the
      linear form $(\,\text{test}(c_I),\,\dot c_I)\,\mathrm dV$ and then
      \emph{projected} onto the trial space by solving the consistent mass
      matrix $\mathsf M=(\text{test}(c_I),c_I)\,\mathrm dV$ with conjugate
      gradients to $10^{-4}$ (\code{SpatialODESolver}). The stored rate is
      $\mathsf M^{-1}\mathbf b$.
\item \textbf{Explicit Euler.} \code{updateImmobileClusters(dt)} does
      $\mathbf c_I \mathrel{+}= \dot{\mathbf c}_I\,\mathrm dt$ with
      $\mathrm dt=\code{dtMax}$ --- one step per DD step, no sub-cycling.
\item \textbf{Implicit loss corrections, applied in sequence.}
      \code{applyImmobilePopulationCorrections} then divides the result by
      $(1+\mathrm dt\,\lambda)$ once per loss channel, in a fixed order:
      thermal annealing (with the content debited by
      $n_{\mathrm{nuc}}(N_{\mathrm{old}}-N_{\mathrm{new}})$), content removal,
      then loop--loop and loop--network coalescence with their Avrami factors.
      A clamp enforces $N\ge0$ and a minimum loop content
      $c \ge n_{\min}\Omega N$.
\end{enumerate}

MoDELib3 keeps the physics of stages 1 and 3 and changes how both are
discretized. The rate is evaluated \textbf{at nodes} rather than projected, so
the mass matrix and its CG solve disappear; and the explicit step and the loss
divisions are \textbf{fused into a single semi-implicit formula},
$n^{\mathrm{new}}=(n+\mathrm dt\,\dot n_{\mathrm{nuc}})/(1+\mathrm dt\,
\lambda_{\mathrm{tot}})$, sub-cycled 20 times per dose step.

Neither choice is strictly better, and the trade is worth stating. The Galerkin
projection is the more accurate representation of a smooth field, because it
respects the element shape functions instead of sampling them at nodes; but it
couples neighbouring nodes, and on a sharp gradient it overshoots. fullCD says
so in its own comment --- the corrected sink value exists ``to adjust for
negative values at the barycenter of the quadratic elements where we have a
sharp gradient of sink values between two end nodes''. Nodal collocation cannot
produce that artifact, at the cost of ignoring the sub-element variation
entirely. Given that the 1~\textmu m case has a boundary layer spanning twelve
orders of magnitude in $C_v$ (Table~\ref{tab:seedrelax}), sharp gradients are
the normal condition here rather than the exception, and MoDELib3's choice is
defensible on those grounds --- though it was not, so far as the change log
records, made for that reason.

The sequencing also differs in a way that matters for large $\mathrm dt$.
fullCD applies its loss factors \emph{one after another}, so with three active
channels the result is $\prod_k (1+\mathrm dt\,\lambda_k)^{-1}$, whereas
MoDELib3's fused form gives $(1+\mathrm dt\sum_k \lambda_k)^{-1}$. The two agree
to $O(\mathrm dt^2)$ and diverge when any $\mathrm dt\,\lambda_k \gtrsim 1$,
which --- given the measured $\mathrm dt\,\lambda \approx 46\,000$ of
Section~\ref{sec:integrators} --- is the operating regime, not a corner case.
Both remain unconditionally positive, which is the property that matters most
here; neither is a controlled approximation at that step size.

\subsection{The coupled march scheme: outward from the 0-D solver}

The march scheme has the opposite history. It did not have to discover
stiffness, because it inherited a stiff integrator: ZrMicro's CVODE BDF solver
predates the coupling entirely, built for the standalone 0-D 19-equation model.
The coupling was then added in three steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{\code{freeze\_mobile}} --- the operator split. The four mobile
      derivatives are zeroed \emph{last}, after every reaction term has already
      read them as parameters, so the same right-hand side serves as the local
      immobile micro-model with an externally supplied $\mathbf C_M^\star$.
\item \textbf{Pointwise dispatch} --- because the split makes the slow march
      decoupled between points, each quadrature point becomes one case of the
      existing OpenMP \code{--batch\_file} mode.
\item \textbf{This work} --- accumulators moved to CVODES quadrature
      ($19\to9$), the exact AD Jacobian, and per-thread workspace reuse.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Three routes to the same conclusion}

All three codes end up treating the immobile loss channels implicitly, and all
three are right to, but they arrive from three different directions --- and the
agreement is stronger evidence than any one of them alone.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{fullCD} put the losses in implicit denominators \emph{by
      construction}: annealing, content removal and both coalescence channels
      are applied as $(1+\mathrm dt\,\lambda)^{-1}$ factors from the outset,
      while the source terms stay explicit. It is a deliberate IMEX split.
\item \textbf{MoDELib3} re-derived the update, started fully explicit, and
      found the stiffness \emph{empirically} --- twice, as loop populations
      collapsing onto the positivity floor (issues 7 and 14) --- then fixed it
      term by term until it had reconstructed the same partition fullCD already
      had.
\item \textbf{The 0-D side} never exposed the problem at all, because BDF
      absorbed it silently from the start; it took the spectrum of
      Table~\ref{tab:spectrum} to put a number on what was being absorbed
      ($46\,000\times$ the forward-Euler limit).
\end{itemize}

That MoDELib3 rediscovered fullCD's partition by symptom, one channel at a time,
is the clearest single argument that the partition is forced by the physics and
not a modelling preference. It is also an argument for reading the upstream: the
two failures documented as issues 7 and 14 were both already answered by
\code{applyImmobilePopulationCorrections}.

That difference in route leaves a difference in robustness, and it is the
practical point of this section. MoDELib3's scheme is unconditionally stable on
the losses that \emph{have been} moved into \code{lossN}/\code{lossC}. It offers
no protection for one that has not. A newly added immobile loss term will be
stable only if it is first recast as a first-order coefficient and placed in the
denominator; written as an explicit rate it will reproduce issue 7 or issue 14,
and --- with no local error estimate and no step rejection --- the only symptom
will again be a species quietly pinned on the floor. CVODE would have flagged
the same term through step rejection and error-test failures without anyone
having to notice a collapsed population.

history, having been flattened from a submodule --- the commits present are

One earlier piece of evidence should be retired rather than repeated.
\code{MoDELib3/Library/Materials/Zr4.txt} does contain zero immobile keys, and
that fact is correct; the \emph{inference} drawn from it --- that the upstream
standalone route therefore could not produce an \aloop/\cloop\ microstructure at
all --- was wrong. \code{Zr4.txt} is simply an older material file that happens

file in this repository says nothing about the upstream code.

\subsection{The fast step belongs inside the loop}
\label{sec:fastinloop}

The first version of this comparison did not perform an operator split at all,
and the defect is worth stating plainly because it invalidated part of the
earlier numbers.

MoDELib3's \code{ClusterDynamicsFEM::solve()} \emph{is} the split:

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.88\textwidth}
\begin{verbatim}
solveMobileClusters();      // FAST -- steady C_M*(x). No time derivative
                            // anywhere; the immobile population enters only
                            // through ImmobileSinks, evaluated at every
                            // quadrature point from the LOCAL state.
solveImmobileClusters();    // SLOW -- dt update of the nodal n_I, c_I
\end{verbatim}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\noindent
The coupled route was meant to keep the first call and replace the second. What
it actually did was read the mobile field out of the \emph{standalone run's}
recorded snapshots. Under that arrangement $\mathbf C_M$ never responds to the
immobile state the march is building: the coupling is one-way, and the march has
no mechanism to relax a seed that is not already at quasi-steady state. Worse,
the nearest recorded snapshot to the 0.1 \dpa\ seed sits at 1.1 \dpa, outside
the 0.75 \dpa\ acceptance window, so the entire first interval --- a quarter of
the march --- ran on the raw uniform seed field.

The fix is a new DD.txt scalar, \code{useImmobileSolver} (default 1, so every
pre-existing case is untouched). With \code{useImmobileSolver=0} a DDomp run
performs the fast step and passes the immobile field straight through. The march
then calls it as a subroutine: write the current per-node immobile state into
the \code{evl} CD block, run one DDomp step to obtain $\mathbf C_M^*(x)$
\emph{for that state}, freeze it, and integrate the immobile ODEs at every node.
Because \code{runSingleStep} writes output \emph{before} incrementing
\code{runID}, a run with \code{startAtTimeStep=0} and \code{Nsteps=1} reads
\code{evl\_0.txt} and overwrites the same file, which makes the exchange an
in-place round trip. \code{py\_utils/modelib\_qssa.py} drives it and asserts on
DDomp's \code{immobile solver SKIPPED} line, so a binary predating the flag
fails loudly rather than advancing the immobile field twice.

\subsection{A non-terminating mobile solve, and its cause}
\label{sec:mobilehang}

Closing the loop exposed a defect that the standalone route had never triggered.
Asked to solve the mobile field against an immobile state supplied by the march,
\code{solveMobileClusters} does not converge: \code{cError} falls from
$5.8\times10^{5}$ to $1.3\times10^{-4}$ in nine iterations and then oscillates
around $5\times10^{-5}$ indefinitely. The loop is

\begin{center}\code{while (cError > cTol)}\end{center}

\noindent
with \code{cTol}~$=10^{-5}$, \emph{no iteration cap and no stagnation test} ---
inherited verbatim, since MoDELib2-NNL, MoDELib-fullCD and MoDELib3 all carry
the identical loop. So DDomp never returns.

\paragraph{Cause.} The one structural difference from upstream is the cause.
fullCD has \emph{no mobile clamp at all}; MoDELib3 inserted
\code{clampMobileClusters()} between the Newton update and the error test, which
makes the iteration a \emph{projected} Newton method.

The obvious explanation --- that the active set oscillates --- is wrong, and an
instrumented run refutes it: the set freezes at 7090 of 96\,460 dofs by
iteration~3 and reports zero entering and zero leaving on every iteration after.
What actually fails is that the Newton matrix
\code{dmBWF+bWF\_R1+bWF\_R2+bWF\_RI} is the \emph{unconstrained} Jacobian and
does not know any dof is pinned. Each iteration it computes an increment for
every dof, including moves for the pinned ones; the projection discards exactly
those moves, but the increment for the \emph{free} dofs was computed on the
assumption that the pinned ones would move. The free dofs therefore respond,
every iteration, to a coupling that is immediately cancelled, and that
inconsistency does not decay. Solve-then-project is not a consistent method for
a constrained system; the consistent form is an active-set method, in which the
pinned rows are eliminated and the reduced system solved.

The intent is visible in the routine's own comment --- the clamp mirrors
ZrMicro, ``which applies $y=\max(y,C_{\mathrm{floor}})$ before every \emph{rate
evaluation}''. In ZrMicro the floor belongs to the rate evaluation. Here it
mutates the state inside the iteration, and only that breaks convergence.

Deferring the clamp restores \textbf{quadratic} convergence ---
$2.95\times10^{4} \to 1.23\times10^{3} \to 6.77\times10^{1} \to

square of the last --- and is the fastest option by a factor of 2.5.

Three of the alternatives are instructive failures. Changing the error metric is
\emph{bit-identical} to the baseline, all sixteen digits, iteration for
iteration: a floored dof has $c^{\mathrm{new}}=c^{\mathrm{old}}$ and already
contributes nothing to the step norm, so the free dofs are genuinely
oscillating. Under-relaxation does converge, but only linearly, at roughly a
factor of two per iteration. And relaxing \code{cTol} is actively dangerous: it
reports success at iteration 19 while accepting a field that is 49.9\% wrong in
$C_i$ at the worst node.

\paragraph{Verification.} The chosen fix was checked four ways rather than
assumed from the fact that it terminates. Convergence is quadratic; the fields
at \code{cTol}~$=10^{-5}$ and $10^{-8}$ are \emph{bit-identical}, so five
iterations already reach machine precision; the independent damped route reaches
the same field to within 0.19\%, with the means agreeing to five figures, so the
clamp was corrupting the iteration and not the answer; and the result is
physical --- zero negative entries and exactly the same 7090 floored dofs as the
clamped runs, because the floor is still applied, once, after the loop.

The stalled iterate is not merely unrecognized as converged. Its means agree with
the converged field to four figures, but two nodes exceed 1\% and one reaches
53.6\% in $C_i$. A handful of nodes at the clamp boundary drives the whole
failure, and because the error test is a maximum over nodes, those few alone
prevent it from ever settling.

\paragraph{What was changed.} Five optional DD.txt scalars
(\code{mobileSolverTolerance}, \code{mobileSolverMaxIterations},
\code{mobileSolverRelaxation}, \code{mobileSolverErrorMode},
\code{mobileSolverClampInLoop}), all defaulting to the historical behaviour
except the iteration cap, which now retains the \emph{best} iterate and so
converts a hang into a bounded, reproducible result. The coupled fast solve sets
\code{mobileSolverClampInLoop=0} in its own simulation directory and warns if any
solve returns capped rather than converged. The standalone route's defaults are
deliberately left unchanged: making the deferral global is very likely correct,
but it alters a path that currently runs to completion, and that warrants a full
standalone verification first.

$C_{2i}$& $1.113\times10^{-12}$ & $8.966\times10^{-15}$ & $124$ & $3.4\times10^{-53}$ & $3.46\times10^{-14}$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The seed was very far from quasi-steady, and the fast step supplies the whole
difference. Two corrections are happening at once, and they are worth
separating:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Magnitude.} The seeded $C_v$ is $690\times$ the quasi-steady mean.
      The 0-D reaches its own steady balance against a homogenized sink
      strength; the 3-D domain has, in addition, six Dirichlet faces holding the
      mobile species at thermal equilibrium and draining the interior.
\item \textbf{Structure.} The seed is uniform by construction, so its spatial
      contrast is exactly 1. The relaxed $C_v$ field runs from $10^{-20}$ at the
      faces to $6.15\times10^{-8}$ in the interior --- twelve orders of
      magnitude. The whole boundary layer is absent from the seed and is created
      by the fast solve. No amount of immobile marching could have produced it,
      because the immobile equations carry no spatial derivative at all.
\end{itemize}

The $C_i$ minimum of $4.1\times10^{-27}$ is the imposed boundary value
$\exp(-3.0\,\mathrm{eV}/kT)$, deliberately \emph{not} floored at $10^{-20}$:
flooring it would raise the boundary concentration by seven orders and quietly
disable the grain boundary as an interstitial sink, which is the one piece of
physics the 3-D model adds over the 0-D.

So the answer is yes. The split reaches quasi-steady state on its own, and it
does not need the 0-D seed to be right --- which is fortunate, because a 0-D
seed can never be right about a boundary layer it does not represent.

\subsection{A calibration error that voids the earlier physics numbers}
\label{sec:calibration}

\paragraph{Every loop density and mobile concentration reported in earlier
revisions of this section is void.} The cause is not in the coupling or in the
solvers; it is that the driver built its 0-D model straight from the input
workbook.

The workbook is \emph{not} the calibrated model. Section~6c of
\code{coupled\_0d\_3d\_ZrMicro.ipynb} says so explicitly --- 28 parameters have
drifted from the fitted set and 11 are absent from the workbook entirely --- and
pins every fitted value before building the model chain.
\code{run\_coupled\_vs\_standalone.py} and the seed builder both skipped that
step, so they integrated a different model. Integrated to 20 \dpa\ at 573~K and
$10^{-7}$~\dpa/s:

\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
0-D parameter set & $N_a$ & $c_a$ & $C_v$ \\
\midrule
workbook only (what the marches used) & $2.32\times10^{-2}$ & $8.77$ & $4.2\times10^{-10}$ \\
\textbf{calibrated} & $8.05\times10^{-8}$ & $1.34\times10^{-4}$ & $5.01\times10^{-6}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Five orders of magnitude in loop density. $c_a$ is an atom fraction, so the
uncalibrated $8.77$ is impossible on its face; the calibrated $N_a$ is
$6.7\times10^{21}$~m$^{-3}$ with a mean radius of 4.4~nm, which is where
irradiated Zr should be. The fitted set is now carried by
\code{py\_utils/calibration.py} and both drivers build through it.

Two conclusions that were drawn from the uncalibrated runs are withdrawn. The
\aloop\ content was reported as reaching a value greater than unit atom
fraction, which is impossible; with the calibrated set it does not. The
\aloop\ \emph{number density} does still grow without bound when averaged over
the whole domain, but for a reason that has nothing to do with the parameter
set --- see Section~\ref{sec:boundaryartifact}, where it is localized to the
Dirichlet faces. And the 3-D mobile field was reported as
sitting some $180\times$ below the 0-D, which was read as either grain-boundary
physics or a scaling error in the sink term. It was neither: with the calibrated
set the interior plateau of the fast solve is $4.85\times10^{-6}$ against the
0-D's $5.01\times10^{-6}$ --- agreement to 3\% --- with the boundary layer
falling to the thermal-equilibrium value $C_v^{\mathrm{eq}}=8.45\times10^{-15}$
at the faces. The well-mixed limit is satisfied, and no separate test of it was
needed.

\paragraph{What is unaffected.} The mobile-solver defect and its fix
(Section~\ref{sec:mobilehang}) are pure numerics: the iteration either converges
or it does not, for any parameter set, and the measurements there were made on a
single saved state with all configurations sharing it. The 0-D solver itself was
also checked and is not implicated --- built from the pre-rewrite commit
\code{acb884d} and from HEAD, it produces identical output to every printed
digit, and it reproduces the experimental reference run
(\code{output/20260806\_090557\_15c465f\_coupled0d3d}) to four significant
figures in the loop content at 0.1, 0.5 and 1~\dpa.

\paragraph{A comparison to make carefully.} That reference directory contains
both a pure 0-D run and a \code{march\_state.npz} from a coupled march with the
placeholder fast solve. They are different objects, and comparing a free 0-D
integration against the marched states --- which this work did twice before
noticing --- looks like a bug and is not one. Freezing the mobile field holds
the nucleation driver $C_{3i}$ at its frozen value, so the marched \aloop\
density grows to $3.63\times10^{-7}$ at 1 \dpa\ where the free 0-D saturates at
$8.05\times10^{-8}$. That $4.5\times$ gap is the operator-splitting signature
itself, present in the old placeholder run as well.

\subsection{Does the seed dose matter? Seeding inside and outside the transient}
\label{sec:seedingstudy}

The coupled march freezes the mobile field for \code{FEM\_EVERY} immobile
substeps at a time, while the 0-D is a genuinely adaptive integration that
resolves the mobile transient with whatever step size that takes. Where
$C_M^*$ is changing quickly the frozen-mobile approximation is therefore doing
something the 0-D never does, and at 0.1 \dpa\ it is changing quickly. The
concern this raises is direct: is a march seeded at 0.1 \dpa\ --- inside the
transient --- carrying an error that a march seeded at 1 \dpa\ would not?

sensitive to is the \emph{spatial} deficiency of a 0-D seed, not its dose --- and
that is repaired by the first fast solve. The corollary is that the offset from
the 0-D visible in the table above is not a seeding artifact and not an
accumulating splitting error: it is a genuine difference between the
spatially-resolved interior and a well-mixed 0-D at the same dose, established
early and then held.

\subsection{What this does and does not isolate}

With the fast step closed, both routes now solve the same fast problem with the
same code, each against its own immobile state. Two differences remain, and
neither is the integrator alone:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Different immobile equations.} The coupled route integrates
      ZrMicro's immobile right-hand side; the standalone integrates MoDELib3's.
      These are two models, not two integrations of one model.
\item \textbf{Different integrators}, as characterized in
      Section~\ref{sec:integrators}, and a different splitting cadence:
      \code{FEM\_EVERY} substeps between fast solves against the standalone's
      one per internal substep.
\end{enumerate}

Attributing any number below to the integrator alone would therefore still be
wrong. Section~\ref{sec:attrib} separates the contributions.

20.1 & $2.32\times10^{-2}$ & $2.23\times10^{-2}$ & $1.72\times10^{-4}$ & \textbf{0.961} & \textbf{0.0074} \\

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The coupled route \textbf{converges onto the plain 0-D answer} as dose
accumulates ($0.255 \to 0.961$): the spatially resolved mobile field delays the
0-D result but does not change where it goes. The standalone sits about
$130\times$ below the 0-D throughout.

Refining the splitting interval settles the remaining ambiguity. Halving is not
enough to be conclusive, so the interval was cut by five, holding the total
substep count fixed:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
& $\Delta\gamma = 5$ \dpa & $\Delta\gamma = 1$ \dpa & change \\
\midrule
coupled \aloop\ at 20.1 \dpa & $2.23\times10^{-2}$ & $2.77\times10^{-2}$ & $+24\%$ \\
ratio to standalone          & 130 & 161 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A five-fold refinement of the splitting moves the answer by 24\%, and moves it
\emph{away} from the standalone rather than toward it. So:

\begin{itemize}
\item the \textbf{model difference is ${\sim}130\times$} and dominates
      everything else by two orders of magnitude;
\item the \textbf{splitting error is ${\sim}20$--$25\%$} at $\Delta\gamma = 5$
      \dpa, and is not what separates the routes;
\item the \textbf{integrator difference is bounded well below both}, consistent
      with Section~\ref{sec:integrators}.
\end{itemize}

The \cloop\ family shows the mirror image: the standalone holds
$1.78\times10^{-8}$ while the coupled route sits at $5.50\times10^{-10}$, a
factor 32 the other way, flat in dose in both routes.

\subsection{A discrepancy with the calibration record}

This conflicts with what \code{ZR3D\_GHONIEM\_CHANGES.md} records. Its issue 14
states that after moving coalescence into the implicit update, ``\aloop\ loop
density went from 3--4$\times$ the 0-D to within ${\sim}30\%$''. Here MoDELib3
sits at 0.7--1.4\% of the 0-D --- roughly $100\times$ \emph{below}, not within
30\%.

One difference from the calibration conditions is visible in the run itself and
should be checked first. The calibration used the tutorial's \emph{generated}
microstructure; this run seeds MoDELib with the 0-D state at 0.1 \dpa, for which
$N_a = 4.94\times10^{-4}$. MoDELib immediately coalesces that down to
$1.43\times10^{-4}$ by 5.1 \dpa\ and then holds it essentially flat
($1.72\times10^{-4}$ at 20.1 \dpa) while the 0-D grows a hundredfold. Whether
that is the seeded start putting MoDELib outside its calibrated regime, or a
genuine regression in the calibration, is not resolved here. It is flagged
rather than explained, and no physics conclusion should be drawn from these
fields until it is.

\noindent
Two findings are worth carrying forward independently of the changes
themselves. First, the accumulators were quietly setting the initial step of
every coupling substep, and removing them from the error test cures a
roundoff-limited first step that had been occurring 2048 times per march
(Section~\ref{sec:pathology}). Second, the two codes integrate the same immobile
equations to very different error levels --- first order with no error control
on the 3-D side, adaptive to $10^{-6}$ on the 0-D side --- which is a systematic
0-D/3-D discrepancy distinct from the three physics non-correspondences on
record (Section~\ref{sec:integrators}).

Items 1--3 of the original list have been implemented and measured
(Section~\ref{sec:recs}); what follows is what remains, ordered by expected
return.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Drive MoDELib in process.} This is now the binding constraint.
      Item~1 of the previous version of this list --- put the FEM mobile solve
      inside the march loop --- has been done (Section~\ref{sec:fastinloop}),
      and doing it exposed the cost: every fast solve is a separate DDomp
      invocation, so the mesh load, the FE assembly and the factorization of the
      diffusion operator are repeated on each of them, at 174\,s per call,
      against a standalone run that amortizes all of it once over twenty steps.
      \code{pyMoDELib} removes the restart, and with it the dominant cost of a
      short splitting interval. Until then \code{FEM\_EVERY} is as much a
      wall-clock knob as a numerical one.
\item \textbf{Guard the standalone immobile solver against the next stiff term.}
      Section~\ref{sec:provenance} shows the same failure was found twice, by
      symptom, and there is still no mechanism that would catch a third. Any new
      loss channel added to \code{solveImmobileClusters} must be recast as a
      first-order coefficient and placed in \code{lossN}/\code{lossC}; a cheap
      standing check is to run one case at \code{nSub} and at $10\,\code{nSub}$
      and assert the answers agree, which turns a silent floor collapse into a
      visible regression.
\item \textbf{Revisit GPU offload only after the above.} With the core-evaluation
      count now the dominant cost and the LU already irrelevant at $n=15$, the
      case for batched GPU integration rests entirely on how large $q$ becomes
      in a live coupling --- which is not yet known.
\end{enumerate}
